Parts

Abstract

Parts is our collection of thirty-five parts in five modules: a green-light switch in Bacillus subtilis with a secreted plant protectant behind it.

Four of the five modules were joined into one plasmid at Level 2 on 1 September, and on 19 September three independent clones gave a band at all three internal junctions. That construct is junction-checked and not sequence-verified. What we would submit is seven parts sequence-confirmed at Level 1, plus a protectant series of codon-optimised and truncated variants. No part is characterised for light response: no induction curve, no dose response, no dark control. Seventeen fixes stay open.

The collection

ReLeaf puts a plant protectant behind a green-light switch in Bacillus subtilis. Getting from a lamp to a secreted protein takes four things to happen in order, and the collection is organised as those four things plus a fifth that lets us test the cargo on its own.

  1. Module 01PCB production3 parts

    Makes the chromophore: Bacillus does not build phycocyanobilin on its own, so ho1 and pcyA arrive as one fused operon that turns the cell's own heme into it.

  2. Module 02Light sensing5 parts

    Holds CcaS-m3, the photoreceptor that autophosphorylates under green light once that chromophore is bound, built behind two constitutive promoters of different strength.

  3. Module 03Transduction4 parts

    Holds CcaR, the response regulator that takes the phosphoryl group from the photoreceptor and turns it into transcription from PcpcG2.

  4. Module 04Green-light responsive expression9 parts

    Where that transcription lands: PcpcG2-veg drives a secretion peptide fused to whichever protectant is sitting in the cargo slot.

  5. Module 05Secretion reporter14 parts

    The same cargoes behind a constitutive promoter in a shuttle vector, so that a negative protectant result has one fewer explanation to rule out.

Four of the five modules were joined into one plasmid at Level 2 on 1 September. The detail of every part lives on the Registry, and the evidence behind each status word is folded into the build record at the foot of this page.

No part here is characterised for light response

Nothing in the wet lab archive shows PcpcG2-veg output changing with green light. There is no induction curve, no dose response and no dark control series. Every statement on this page about what the switch does is design intent, taken from the two CcaSR papers [1, 2], and the measurements we do hold are cloning measurements: amplicon sizes, restriction digests and sequencing reads. What has no measurement lists this out part by part.

Status key

Every card carries one of six words, and a part gets the highest word it has earned. The small tag after the word says at which assembly level it earned it: L0 for an entry part, L1 for a transcription unit, L2 for the joined circuit.

  • Designeda sequence, usually a synthesis plasmid, and no bench step after that
  • Clonedin an entry plasmid, with the digest or the read still missing
  • Assembledin its destination vector and giving colonies, not yet confirmed
  • Colony-PCR checkedthe expected band came back, and nobody has read it
  • Sequence-confirmeda named colony and a date: somebody read the DNA back
  • Unsuccessfulthe build record marks it failed, and later gels dispute that
Where each of the six words comes fromThe build spreadsheet's own states, written out, and the one rung the collection does not reach.

The words are the build spreadsheet's own states, not a scale we invented for the wiki: the Level 0 sheet records plasmid, glycerol stock, PCR product, TA cloning, enzyme digestion and sequencing as separate columns, the Level 1 sheet records an assembly status and a colony PCR length, and the sequencing file records a colony number and a date.

  • Designed

    A sequence exists and in most cases a synthesis plasmid exists, and no bench step after that is recorded. In the Level 0 sheet these rows read not started across every column.

  • Cloned

    The part is in an entry plasmid. PCR, gel, clean-up and TA cloning are recorded, and either the digest or the sequencing read is still missing.

  • Assembled

    The transcription unit went into its destination vector and gave colonies. The Level 1 sheet writes this as Assembly, meaning under way and not yet confirmed.

  • Colony-PCR checked

    A colony PCR or a restriction digest returned the expected band. The construct is the right size and the junctions are where they should be. Nobody has read it.

  • Sequence-confirmed

    A named colony and a sequencing date, both recorded in sequencing positive clone.docx. This is the only word on the page that means somebody read the DNA back.

  • Unsuccessful

    The build record marks it failed. Three Level 1 assemblies carry this word, and all three are disputed by later gels; they are named under contested assemblies.

There is a seventh rung the collection does not reach in a way we can attribute. B. subtilis 168 takes the shuttle vector between 2.0 and 2.3 kV, and the transformation counts are on the Engineering page, but the archive nowhere records which cargo-bearing plasmid was in which transformant. So no individual part on this page is marked as being in B. subtilis, and the one protein-level signal we have, a single band at roughly 41 kDa on the 24 August blot, cannot be assigned to a named part either.

PCB production module

This module supplies the chromophore the light sensor needs. The ho1 and pcyA genes are carried as a single fused operon: heme oxygenase-1 opens the cell's endogenous heme ring, and phycocyanobilin:ferredoxin oxidoreductase reduces the product to phycocyanobilin. Without that molecule the CcaS photoreceptor is apoprotein and green light does nothing at all, which is why this module sits first in the chain even though it is not the part that senses anything.

Light sensing module

CcaS-m3 is a miniaturised variant of the cyanobacterial CcaS histidine kinase. With phycocyanobilin bound, green light drives it to autophosphorylate and to pass the phosphoryl group to CcaR. We built it behind two constitutive promoters of different strength, PliaG and PlepA, because the useful dynamic range of a two-component system depends on how much sensor is sitting in the cell, and we had no way to predict which level would work in our hands.

Transduction module

CcaR is the response regulator half of the pair, an OmpR-class protein that accepts the phosphoryl group from activated CcaS-m3 and then activates transcription from PcpcG2. It is the piece that turns a phosphorylation event into a promoter firing, and it is deliberately on a constitutive promoter of its own so that the amount of regulator in the cell does not itself depend on the light.

Green-light responsive expression

This is the output stage. PcpcG2-veg is a chimera: the cyanobacterial CcaR-responsive promoter with B. subtilis promoter features grafted on so that the host's own polymerase will read it. Behind it sits EEBD-RBS0, and behind that a secretion peptide fused to whichever protectant is being tested. Several cargoes went through the slot, and they are the reason this module carries nine cards: the promoter, the ribosome binding site and the terminator are shared, and only the cassette changes.

Secretion reporter module

Same cargoes, constitutive promoter, shuttle vector. The point of this module is diagnostic. A construct that has to be induced by light before it makes anything gives a negative protectant result two possible causes, the switch or the cargo, and with no induction curve there is no way to tell them apart. Putting each cargo behind Pveg in pSTK-EXP-1-sfGFP, which replicates in both E. coli and B. subtilis, removes one of the two.

Registry contributions

Most of the regulatory parts came out of a kit, several of the coding sequences came from teams who submitted them years ago, and what we added is a set of fusions and variants that did not exist before this summer.

Reused parts

Eleven entries come straight from the SubtiToolKit [5], a Golden Gate kit written for B. subtilis and Gram-positive hosts, along with every destination vector we used: pSTK-0 for entry parts, pSTK-1A through pSTK-1D for transcription units, pSTK-2 for the joined circuit and pSTK-EXP-1 and pSTK-EXP-2 for the shuttle builds. The kit parts are Pveg, PlepA, PliaG, PtagC+RBS, RBS0, the EEBD blocking device, B0015, L3S2P21, and the Csn, AmyQ and GanA secretion tags.

Five coding and regulatory parts come from earlier iGEM teams: BBa_K1232004 for the Ho1:pcyA operon, BBa_K592002 for CcaR, BBa_K823000 and BBa_K823002 for PliaG and PlepA through BioBrick Box 2.0, and the Pveg accession in dispute under P1. The alternate promoter and RBS panel adds five more that were ordered and not used: BBa_K143013, BBa_K3317064, BBa_K2086002, BBa_K5178006 and BBa_K3317072.

What has no measurement

Registry documentation is judged partly on characterisation, so here is exactly which measurements this collection has and which it does not.

What we hold is cloning evidence: plasmid sizes, colony PCR amplicons, restriction digests and Sanger reads, all listed in the folds below with their dates. We also hold a transformation efficiency curve for B. subtilis 168 on the Engineering page, which is a measurement about the host and not about any part in this collection.

What we do not hold is any output measurement for any part. No part here has a fluorescence, absorbance or enzyme activity number attached to it. PcpcG2-veg has never been shown to change output in response to green light, in either direction, so the promoter at the centre of the design is uncharacterised and so is everything downstream of it. The three Western blots of 2, 7 and 24 August are the only protein-level data, and the 24 August blot, the only one whose ladder resolves, carries a single band at roughly 41 kDa in the lane loaded with the 18 August culture; the lane holding the culture that was actually sprayed on plants is blank. That band cannot be tied to a named part in this collection.

Every registry entry we submit should therefore describe its characterisation as absent and say what measurement would close the gap. For PcpcG2-veg the missing measurement is a green-light induction curve against a dark control in strain 168.

Build record

Every status word on the cards above comes from here. This record is assembled from four files kept separately during the summer: the Level 0 progression sheet, the Level 1 assembly sheet with its cloning and colony PCR lengths, the sequencing file with colony numbers and dates, and the monthly gel decks.

They disagree, and the disagreements reach the main constructs. The spreadsheet called PARTS PROGRESSION FINAL!!.xlsx is the most organised of the four and also the most out of date: its Level 2 sheet contains no Level 2 work at all (P15), and its Level 1 sheet still reads Not started for three constructs that were sequenced in August and September (P6). It is treated here as one source among several and not as the record. Where a gel and a sheet disagree, the tables in the folds below follow the gel, and the disagreement is written up under To fix before the freeze.

Level 2 is junction-checked and not sequence-verified

The joined construct has never been read end to end. Both Level 2 rows in sequencing positive clone.docx have a blank sequence date, which is the same fact stated a second way. Everything a junction check establishes is that the right modules are adjacent in the right order; it says nothing about what is inside them, and the modules were confirmed individually at Level 1 in a different plasmid. Nothing on this wiki should describe the Level 2 circuit as verified.

Truncation seriesWhy the peptide was cut down, and how far the two truncations and the two substitutions got.

The truncation work goes the other way from the mutations. Instead of stabilising the whole peptide, it cuts it down, on the reasoning that a smaller peptide both crosses plant tissue more easily and secretes more efficiently. Two constructs were planned for direct assembly into the shuttle vector, with the originals held on a pTwist Amp backbone: the full-length peptide behind Pveg, and the truncated fragment behind Pveg. Neither has a colony number or a sequencing date. Both are Designed.

Two further single-substitution variants, S15A and G17A, sit alongside them at the same stage. What the substitutions were chosen to do, and on what evidence, belongs to Peptide Design, and that page also carries the claims the team retracted in writing. Nothing on this page should be read as a comparison between protectants: LEA14 and BoPep4 have never touched a plant, and a four-way protectant comparison cannot be made from this evidence.

Below is what the team has written for each part so far, in their words, with the construct diagram for each one. Where an entry is still the blank template, this page says so rather than filling it in.

The mRNA secondary structure around the ribosome binding site and start codon heavily influences the translation-initiation efficiency inside bacteria, independent of the encoded protein sequence. Synonymous codons, meaning codons that result in the same amino acids, in the 5′ end of a CDS can impact how the mRNA folds near the binding site. The folding can either hide or expose the Shine Dalgarno sequence and start codon, impacting the efficiency of ribosome binding and translation initiation. Following this concept suggested by Castillo-Hair [1], the design pipeline treated the first 15 codons of each CDS as an editable region. ViennaRNA 2.7.2 [6] was used to generate and fold tens of thousands of synonymous variants in the RS-inclusive 236 nt 5′ window (residual recombination site, MF001 RBS, spacer, SamyQ ORF and the first 90 nt of the CDS), determining the mean base-pair unpaired probability across the 15 nt following the ATG and across the MF001 Shine-Dalgarno core (AAGGAGG) and creating a balance score.

To fix before the freeze

Seventeen things on this page are unresolved, and almost every one of them is a disagreement between two of our own records rather than a gap in the writing: an accession written two ways, a part name spelled three ways, two ribosome binding sites claimed for one construct. They used to sit as boxes beside the claims they qualify, and they are collected here to keep the cards above short. Each box still names both claims and both sources. To fix · P1 · Two different accessions for Pveg, and they are not the same sequence

Parts_.docx gives Pveg as BBa_K143012. The build record gives it as BBa_K823003: the STK parts sheet names the entry STK028_pSTK-0A-Pveg (BBa_K823003) and adds, in its own notes column, that we used the medium expression Pveg (BBa_K823003), that differs in 4 nucleotides from the strong version.

This matters more than a citation slip. Those four nucleotides are the difference between a medium and a strong constitutive promoter, and Pveg drives CcaR in module 3 and the whole cargo in the shuttle build. Decide which accession the parts we submit actually carry, put that one in both the docx and the Registry entry, and say in the entry that it is the medium variant.

To fix · P2 · SamyQ does not exist in the kit we took it from

Every table in Parts_.docx calls the secretion signal SamyQ and attributes it to B. subtilis. The SubtiToolKit inventory has no SamyQ. It has STK330_pSTK-0Cx-AmyQ secretion tag, the signal peptide of the Bacillus amyloliquefaciens alpha-amylase AmyQ, and the S in our name is presumably signal peptide of. If that is right, the Registry entries should say so in one line, because a reader searching for AmyQ will not find SamyQ and a reader searching for SamyQ will find nothing at all. If it is not right, then the source of the peptide is undocumented and needs to be established before submission.

To fix · P3 · The BoPep4 truncation is numbered two different ways

In the green-light module table of Parts_.docx the truncation is SamyQ-Bopep4-15-23, described as amino acids 15 to 23. Two pages later, in the secretion reporter table of the same file, it is SamyQ-Bopep4 (9-23). The protein design registry list in Parts writing.docx has it a third way, Bopep4_9-23_NHis, with an N-terminal histidine tag the parts tables do not mention.

Nine to twenty-three and fifteen to twenty-three are different peptides. The cards on this page use 9-23, because two of the three sources say so, but that is a majority vote and not a decision. Check the ordered sequence, fix the name in every file, and decide whether the His tag is part of the submitted construct or a purification aid stripped before submission.

To fix · P4 · Our own naming convention is not applied to our own part names

Parts_.docx opens by setting the convention: species italic with an initial capital, gene names italic lower case, protein names roman with an initial capital. The part names in the same file do not follow it. LEA appears as Csn:Lea in two tables, as Csn:LEA in the sequencing file and as csn:lea in the Level 1 record for the same construct. The photoreceptor is filed in gene form, ccaS-m3, and the response regulator beside it in protein form, CcaR. BoPep4 is written BoPep4 and Bopep4 in adjacent rows.
