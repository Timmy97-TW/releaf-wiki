Part collection

iGEM asks for parts that another team could pick up and use. That means a working part, documented on the Registry, with the evidence behind it stated at the strength the evidence actually has.

What iGEM asks for →

Overview

ReLeaf puts a plant protectant behind a green-light switch in Bacillus subtilis. Getting from a lamp to a secreted protein takes four things to happen in order, and the collection is organised as those four things plus a fifth that lets us test the cargo on its own.

The PCB production module makes the chromophore. Bacillus does not make phycocyanobilin on its own, so the ho1 and pcyA genes are supplied as a fused operon that turns the cell's own heme into it. The light sensing module holds the photoreceptor CcaS-m3, which only works once that chromophore is present. Green light makes it autophosphorylate. The transduction module holds CcaR, the response regulator that takes the phosphoryl group and turns it into transcription. The green-light responsive expression module is where that transcription lands: PcpcG2-veg drives a secretion peptide fused to a protectant. The secretion reporter module is the same cargo behind a constitutive promoter in a shuttle vector, so a negative protectant result has one fewer explanation to rule out.

Four of those five modules were assembled into one plasmid at Level 2 on 1 September. What that assembly has behind it, and what it does not, is set out in the build record.

No part here is characterised for light response

Nothing in the wet lab archive shows PcpcG2-veg output changing with green light. There is no induction curve, no dose response and no dark control series. Every statement on this page about what the switch does is design intent, taken from the two CcaSR papers [1, 2], and the measurements we do hold are cloning measurements: amplicon sizes, restriction digests and sequencing reads. What has no measurement lists this out part by part.

Status vocabulary

Every part in every table below carries one of five words. The words are the build spreadsheet's own states, not a scale we invented for the wiki: the Level 0 sheet records plasmid, glycerol stock, PCR product, TA cloning, enzyme digestion and sequencing as separate columns, the Level 1 sheet records an assembly status and a colony PCR length, and the sequencing file records a colony number and a date. A part gets the highest word it has earned, and the small tag after the word says at which assembly level it earned it.

  • Designed

    A sequence exists and in most cases a synthesis plasmid exists, and no bench step after that is recorded. In the Level 0 sheet these rows read not started across every column.

  • Cloned

    The part is in an entry plasmid. PCR, gel, clean-up and TA cloning are recorded, and either the digest or the sequencing read is still missing.

  • Assembled

    The transcription unit went into its destination vector and gave colonies. The Level 1 sheet writes this as Assembly, meaning under way and not yet confirmed.

  • Colony-PCR checked

    A colony PCR or a restriction digest returned the expected band. The construct is the right size and the junctions are where they should be. Nobody has read it.

  • Sequence-confirmed

    A named colony and a sequencing date, both recorded in sequencing positive clone.docx. This is the only word on the page that means somebody read the DNA back.

  • Unsuccessful

    The build record marks it failed. Three Level 1 assemblies carry this word, and all three are disputed by later gels; they are named under contested assemblies.

There is a sixth rung the collection does not reach in a way we can attribute. B. subtilis 168 takes the shuttle vector between 2.0 and 2.3 kV, and the transformation counts are on the Engineering page, but the archive nowhere records which cargo-bearing plasmid was in which transformant. So no individual part on this page is marked as being in B. subtilis, and the one protein-level signal we have, a single band at roughly 41 kDa on the 24 August blot, cannot be assigned to a named part either.

PCB production module

This module supplies the chromophore the light sensor needs. The ho1 and pcyA genes are carried as a single fused operon: heme oxygenase-1 opens the cell's endogenous heme ring, and phycocyanobilin:ferredoxin oxidoreductase reduces the product to phycocyanobilin. Without that molecule the CcaS photoreceptor is apoprotein and green light does nothing at all, which is why this module sits first in the chain even though it is not the part that senses anything.

Light sensing module

CcaS-m3 is a miniaturised variant of the cyanobacterial CcaS histidine kinase. With phycocyanobilin bound, green light drives it to autophosphorylate and to pass the phosphoryl group to CcaR. We built it behind two constitutive promoters of different strength, PliaG and PlepA, because the useful dynamic range of a two-component system depends on how much sensor is sitting in the cell, and we had no way to predict which level would work in our hands.

Transduction module

CcaR is the response regulator half of the pair, an OmpR-class protein that accepts the phosphoryl group from activated CcaS-m3 and then activates transcription from PcpcG2. It is the piece that turns a phosphorylation event into a promoter firing, and it is deliberately on a constitutive promoter of its own so that the amount of regulator in the cell does not itself depend on the light.

Green-light responsive expression

This is the output stage. PcpcG2-veg is a chimera: the cyanobacterial CcaR-responsive promoter with B. subtilis promoter features grafted on so that the host's own polymerase will read it. Behind it sits EEBD-RBS0, and behind that a secretion peptide fused to whichever protectant is being tested. Several cargoes went through the slot, and they are the reason the module table is long: each promoter, RBS and terminator is shared, and only the cassette changes.

Secretion reporter module

Same cargoes, constitutive promoter, shuttle vector. The point of this module is diagnostic. A construct that has to be induced by light before it makes anything gives a negative protectant result two possible causes, the switch or the cargo, and with no induction curve there is no way to tell them apart. Putting each cargo behind Pveg in pSTK-EXP-1-sfGFP, which replicates in both E. coli and B. subtilis, removes one of the two.

Build record

Level 2 junction check

Three consecutive Golden Gate rounds returned nothing with no design change between them. The insert-to-vector ratio was then swept at 1:5 and 1:7, and on 1 September one clone from each ratio was checked across all three internal junctions: junction A at 375 bp, junction B at 632 bp and junction C at 661 bp. Those two clones are X5 and X7, named after the ratios that produced them. Three junctions is what separates a complete circuit from a plasmid that happens to contain one module.

On 19 September the check was repeated and widened. Three independent ACDI clones, X1, X3 and X9, were run at all three junctions in a single nine-lane gel, and every lane carries a band. That is the strongest cloning evidence the project holds.

A second Level 2 device also exists and has never appeared on this wiki. ABDI is the same four modules with the PliaG version of the CcaS unit in place of the PlepA one, so it is A, B, D and I where the other is A, C, D and I. Its clone V7 was called positive on 1 September, and it was screened across August and again on 17 September. It has never had all three junctions checked in one gel, which is the difference between it and ACDI.

Protectant series

Most of the collection is borrowed. This section is the part that is not. Three protectant proteins were taken as starting points, ACC deaminase, LEA14 and the broccoli elicitor peptide BoPep4, and each was put through some combination of codon optimisation, point mutation and truncation, then fused to a secretion peptide. The variant suffixes follow a v-and-t convention of the team's own: v is the sequence version and t is the trial within it.

Truncation series

The truncation work goes the other way from the mutations. Instead of stabilising the whole peptide, it cuts it down, on the reasoning that a smaller peptide both crosses plant tissue more easily and secretes more efficiently. Two constructs were planned for direct assembly into the shuttle vector, with the originals held on a pTwist Amp backbone: the full-length peptide behind Pveg, and the truncated fragment behind Pveg. Neither has a colony number or a sequencing date. Both are Designed.

Two further single-substitution variants, S15A and G17A, sit alongside them at the same stage. What the substitutions were chosen to do, and on what evidence, belongs to Peptide Design, and that page also carries the claims the team retracted in writing. Nothing on this page should be read as a comparison between protectants: LEA14 and BoPep4 have never touched a plant, and a four-way protectant comparison cannot be made from this evidence.

Registry contributions

Being clear about what is ours is a condition of the collection being useful. Most of the regulatory parts came out of a kit, several of the coding sequences came from teams who submitted them years ago, and what we added is a set of fusions and variants that did not exist before this summer.

Reused parts

Eleven entries come straight from the SubtiToolKit [5], a Golden Gate kit written for B. subtilis and Gram-positive hosts, along with every destination vector we used: pSTK-0 for entry parts, pSTK-1A through pSTK-1D for transcription units, pSTK-2 for the joined circuit and pSTK-EXP-1 and pSTK-EXP-2 for the shuttle builds. The kit parts are Pveg, PlepA, PliaG, PtagC+RBS, RBS0, the EEBD blocking device, B0015, L3S2P21, and the Csn, AmyQ and GanA secretion tags.

Five coding and regulatory parts come from earlier iGEM teams: BBa_K1232004 for the Ho1:pcyA operon, BBa_K592002 for CcaR, BBa_K823000 and BBa_K823002 for PliaG and PlepA through BioBrick Box 2.0, and the Pveg accession in dispute under P1. The alternate promoter and RBS panel adds five more that were ordered and not used: BBa_K143013, BBa_K3317064, BBa_K2086002, BBa_K5178006 and BBa_K3317072.

Our own parts

What we would submit is the set with no accession in the tables above. In order of how much evidence sits behind each one:

  • Sequence-confirmed at Level 1. ccaS-m3 as a standardised entry part, PcpcG2-veg, the EEBD-RBS0 composite, Csn:ACCD, Csn:LEA, SamyQ:ACCD v2t1 and SamyQ:LEA v2t1. Seven parts with a named colony and a date behind them.
  • Sequence-confirmed at Level 0. SamyQ-BoPep4-v2t1 and SamyQ-BoPep4-v2t2, and, if P8 is settled in their favour, the three AmilCP reporter cassettes.
  • Designed only. SamyQ:ACCD wild type, SamyQ-ACCD-v3t1, SamyQ-LEA wild type, SamyQ-LEA-v2t2, SamyQ-BoPep4 wild type, the 9-23 truncation, S15A and G17A. Eight designs that would have to be submitted as design entries and labelled as such.

What has no measurement

Registry documentation is judged partly on characterisation, so it is worth saying exactly which measurements this collection has and which it does not.

What we hold is cloning evidence: plasmid sizes, colony PCR amplicons, restriction digests and Sanger reads, all listed above with their dates. We also hold a transformation efficiency curve for B. subtilis 168 on the Engineering page, which is a measurement about the host and not about any part in this collection.

What we do not hold is any output measurement for any part. No part here has a fluorescence, absorbance or enzyme activity number attached to it. PcpcG2-veg has never been shown to change output in response to green light, in either direction, so the promoter at the centre of the design is uncharacterised and so is everything downstream of it. The three Western blots of 2, 7 and 24 August are the only protein-level data, and the 24 August blot, the only one whose ladder resolves, carries a single band at roughly 41 kDa in the lane loaded with the 18 August culture; the lane holding the culture that was actually sprayed on plants is blank. That band cannot be tied to a named part in this collection.

Every registry entry we submit should therefore describe its characterisation as absent and say what measurement would close the gap. For PcpcG2-veg the missing measurement is a green-light induction curve against a dark control in strain 168.
