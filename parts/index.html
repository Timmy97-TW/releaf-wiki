Parts

Abstract

Parts is our collection of thirty-two entries in five modules: a green-light switch in Bacillus subtilis with a secreted plant protectant behind it.

Every evidence word in the table comes from the build record, a named colony and a date for sequence-confirmed; where gel and spreadsheet disagree the gel wins, and the three contested assemblies are named. Eighteen of the thirty-two entries are new this year, eight come from the SubtiToolKit and six reuse one of five distinct Registry accessions. Six entries are designs with no bench step recorded, two of them codon-optimised peptides confirmed at Level 0. The Level 2 join gave three clones with bands at all three junctions on 19 September, junction-checked and not sequenced. No part is characterised for light response: no induction curve, no dose response, no dark control.

No part here is characterised for light response

Every evidence word in the table above is a cloning measurement: an amplicon size, a restriction digest, a Sanger read. Nothing in the wet lab archive shows PcpcG2-veg output changing with green light, in either direction, so the promoter at the centre of the design is uncharacterised and so is everything downstream of it. There is no induction curve, no dose response and no dark control series. Every statement here about what the switch does is design intent, taken from the two CcaSR papers [1, 2]. Level 2, the joined circuit, is junction-checked and was never read end to end.

Build record

Every evidence word in the table above comes from here. This record is assembled from four files kept separately during the summer: the Level 0 progression sheet, the Level 1 assembly sheet with its cloning and colony PCR lengths, the sequencing file with colony numbers and dates, and the monthly gel decks.

They disagree, and the disagreements reach the main constructs. The spreadsheet called PARTS PROGRESSION FINAL!!.xlsx is the most organised of the four and also the most out of date: its Level 2 sheet contains no Level 2 work at all, and its Level 1 sheet still reads Not started for three constructs that were sequenced in August and September. It is treated here as one source among several and not as the record. Where a gel and a sheet disagree, the tables in the folds below follow the gel.

Level 2 is junction-checked and not sequence-verified

The joined construct has never been read end to end. Both Level 2 rows in sequencing positive clone.docx have a blank sequence date, which is the same fact stated a second way. Everything a junction check establishes is that the right modules are adjacent in the right order; it says nothing about what is inside them, and the modules were confirmed individually at Level 1 in a different plasmid. Nothing on this wiki should describe the Level 2 circuit as verified.

Level 2 junction checkThree internal junctions checked on two devices, ACDI and ABDI, and why neither counts as verified.

Three consecutive Golden Gate rounds returned nothing with no design change between them. The insert-to-vector ratio was then swept at 1:5 and 1:7, and on 1 September one clone from each ratio was checked across all three internal junctions: junction A at 375 bp, junction B at 632 bp and junction C at 661 bp. Those two clones are X5 and X7, named after the ratios that produced them. Three junctions is what separates a complete circuit from a plasmid that happens to contain one module.

On 19 September the check was repeated and widened. Three independent ACDI clones, X1, X3 and X9, were run at all three junctions in a single nine-lane gel, and every lane carries a band. That is the strongest cloning evidence the project holds.

A second Level 2 device also exists and has never appeared on this wiki. ABDI is the same four modules with the PliaG version of the CcaS unit in place of the PlepA one, so it is A, B, D and I where the other is A, C, D and I. Its clone V7 was called positive on 1 September, and it was screened across August and again on 17 September. It has never had all three junctions checked in one gel, which is the difference between it and ACDI.

Protectant series

Most of the collection is borrowed; the protectant series is not. Three protectant proteins were taken as starting points, ACC deaminase, LEA14 and the broccoli elicitor peptide BoPep4, and each was put through some combination of codon optimisation, point mutation and truncation, then fused to a secretion peptide. The variant suffixes follow a v-and-t convention of the team's own: v is the sequence version and t is the trial within it.

Truncation seriesWhy the peptide was cut down, and how far the two truncations and the two substitutions got.

The truncation work goes the other way from the mutations. Instead of stabilising the whole peptide, it cuts it down, on the reasoning that a smaller peptide both crosses plant tissue more easily and secretes more efficiently. Two constructs were planned for direct assembly into the shuttle vector, with the originals held on a pTwist Amp backbone: the full-length peptide behind Pveg, and the truncated fragment behind Pveg. Neither has a colony number or a sequencing date. Both are Designed.

Two further single-substitution variants, S15A and G17A, sit alongside them at the same stage. What the substitutions were chosen to do, and on what evidence, belongs to Peptide Design, and that page also carries the claims the team retracted in writing. Nothing on this page should be read as a comparison between protectants: LEA14 and BoPep4 have never touched a plant, and a four-way protectant comparison cannot be made from this evidence.

Part registry entriesWhat the team has written into each entry so far, in their own words, with the construct diagram for each.

Below is what the team has written for each part so far, in their words, with the construct diagram for each one.

The mRNA secondary structure around the ribosome binding site and start codon heavily influences the translation-initiation efficiency inside bacteria, independent of the encoded protein sequence. Synonymous codons, meaning codons that result in the same amino acids, in the 5′ end of a CDS can impact how the mRNA folds near the binding site. The folding can either hide or expose the Shine Dalgarno sequence and start codon, impacting the efficiency of ribosome binding and translation initiation. Following this concept suggested by Castillo-Hair [1], the design pipeline treated the first 15 codons of each CDS as an editable region. ViennaRNA 2.7.2 [6] was used to generate and fold tens of thousands of synonymous variants in the RS-inclusive 236 nt 5′ window (residual recombination site, MF001 RBS, spacer, SamyQ ORF and the first 90 nt of the CDS), determining the mean base-pair unpaired probability across the 15 nt following the ATG and across the MF001 Shine-Dalgarno core (AAGGAGG) and creating a balance score.

SamyQ-LEA-v2t1

Short description. SamyQ-LEA-v2t1 connects a Late Embryogenesis Abundant (LEA14) protein to SamyQ, a Sec-secretion signal peptide, directing the proteins through a Sec-dependent pathway in B. subtilis 168. LEA14 is an intrinsically disordered protein that can help plants stabilise cellular membranes under drought, salinity, heat and osmotic stress. In this variant, we synonymously recoded the first 15 codons of the coding sequence to improve the mRNA secondary structure of the translation start site.

Detailed description. LEA proteins, a family of stress-protective proteins, are small, partially folded and hydrophilic proteins that stabilise membranes and isolate ions to prevent water loss [8]. In our project, LEA14 is expressed as a secreted output under the PcpcG2-veg promoter of a CcaSR light-inducible expression system in B. subtilis 168, relieving plants from abiotic stresses.

SamyQ-LEA-v2t1 was determined to be the best candidate from the run: 6 of the first 15 LEA14 codons were changed (c1 GCG→GCA, c2 TCT→AGT, c3 TTA→CTC, c4 TTA→CTC, c10 TTT→TTC, c11 GTA→GTC). The predicted start+15 accessibility improved from 0.538 to 0.669 and SD-core accessibility decreased from 0.357 to 0.307 when compared to the wild type, resulting in an overall balance score gain of 0.895 to 0.976. The codon adaptation index against B. subtilis 168 usage [7] dropped from 0.85 to 0.81. SamyQ-AcdS-v2t1

Short description. SamyQ-AcdS-v2t1 connects 1-aminocyclopropane-1-carboxylate (ACC) deaminase (acdS), a plant-growth-promoting enzyme, to SamyQ, a Sec-secretion signal peptide, directing the protein through a Sec-dependent pathway in B. subtilis 168. ACC deaminase degrades ACC, the precursor of the plant stress hormone ethylene, helping relieve plants from ethylene-driven growth inhibition under abiotic stress once secreted into the rhizosphere. In this variant, we synonymously recoded 12 of the first 15 codons of the coding sequence to improve the mRNA secondary structure of the translation start site.

Detailed description. ACC deaminase is a plant-growth-promoting enzyme that hydrolyses ACC before it is converted into ethylene, reducing stress-induced ethylene accumulation in nearby plant roots and helping plants better tolerate drought, salinity, flooding and pathogen stress [9]. In our project, ACC deaminase is expressed as a secreted output under the PcpcG2-veg promoter of a CcaSR light-inducible expression system in B. subtilis 168, relieving plants from abiotic stresses.

SamyQ-AcdS-v2t1 was determined to be the best candidate from the run: 12 of the first 15 acdS codons were changed (c2 TTA→TTG, c3 AAT→AAC, c4 AGA→CGC, c7 AGA→CGC, c8 TAT→TAC, c9 CCG→CCC, c10 TTA→CTT, c11 ACA→ACT, c12 TTC→TTT, c13 GGA→GGG, c14 CCG→CCA, c15 AGC→AGT). The predicted start+15 accessibility improved from 0.563 to 0.773 and SD-core accessibility improved from 0.088 to 0.203 when compared to the wild type, resulting in an overall balance score gain of 0.651 to 0.976. The codon adaptation index against B. subtilis 168 usage [7] dropped from 0.85 to 0.75. Instead of following Castillo’s rule, which prioritises low GC content and A over U, the program individually folded and verified every candidate to verify improved accessibility, as the Castillo-rule-applied control produced a start+15 score of only 0.698 and an SD-core score of 0.186 (balance 0.884), which, although still an improvement over wild type, remained lower than the balance achieved through individual verification of every candidate.

SamyQ-BoPep4-v2t1

Short description. SamyQ-BoPep4-v2t1 connects BoPep4, a 23-residue plant elicitor peptide (PEP) from broccoli (Brassica oleracea var. italica), to SamyQ, a Sec-secretion signal peptide, directing the peptide through a Sec-dependent pathway in B. subtilis 168. BoPep4 is expressed as a secreted output under the PcpcG2-veg promoter of a CcaSR light-inducible expression system, targeting microbes in the extracellular environment once exported. In this variant, we synonymously recoded 9 out of the first 15 codons of the coding sequence to improve the mRNA secondary structure of the translation start site.

Detailed description. BoPep4 is a short, 23-amino-acid mature PEP (GILIGSKKRPREPHSSGKPGGHN) used as a secreted output. In our design, BoPep4 is fused C-terminally to the SamyQ signal peptide and expressed under the PcpcG2-veg promoter of the same CcaSR light-inducible system used for our other secreted outputs in B. subtilis 168. The peptide utilises an auxin and abscisic acid signalling crosstalk to enhance salt-stress tolerance in broccoli by reducing the Na+/K+ ratio, and by promoting leaf wax and cutin accumulation [10].

Wild-type BoPep4 was the weakest starting point of the whole construct series: start+15 accessibility was only 0.396 and SD-core accessibility only 0.067, both below the roughly 0.50 usability threshold and the Ho1 reference baseline (0.681). SamyQ-BoPep4-v2t1 was determined to be the best candidate from the run: 9 of the first 15 BoPep4 codons were changed (c2 ATT→ATC, c5 GGA→GGT, c6 TCA→AGT, c7 AAA→AAG, c10 CCG→CCT, c11 AGA→AGG, c13 CCG→CCT, c14 CAT→CAC, c15 TCA→TCC). The predicted start+15 accessibility improved from 0.396 to 0.497, and SD-core accessibility rose sharply from 0.067 to 0.634, roughly a 9.5-fold gain, resulting in an overall balance score gain of 0.462 to 1.131. The codon adaptation index in B. subtilis 168 [7] dropped from 0.87 to 0.77. We verified this variant against a Castillo-rule-applied control (low GC content, A over U): the control reached a start+15 of 0.677 and an SD-core of 0.425 (balance 1.102), an improvement over wild type, but still below the balance achieved by individually folding and verifying each candidate.

Sequence. 715 bp. Cassette: PcpcG2-veg promoter → EEBD (RS, rrnB T1, T7Te, RS) → MF001 RBS (SD core AAGGAGG) → SamyQ signal peptide (unedited) → BoPep4 CDS (codons 1 to 15 recoded) → 6×His tag → B0015 double terminator. CDS 1 to 45 nt, wild type: GGCATTTTAATTGGATCAAAAAAAAGACCGAGAGAACCGCATTCA. CDS 1 to 45 nt, recoded: GGCATCTTAATTGGTAGTAAGAAAAGACCTAGGGAACCTCACTCC.
