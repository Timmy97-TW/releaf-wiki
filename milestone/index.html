Milestone

8 August 2026, the whole year on one table. Every object here was drawn on that whiteboard first. The rig ran for the camera that afternoon; the growth curve on the laptop is real, and so is the four-fold calibration error it was still carrying.

Two hundred and sixty days from the first team meeting to the writing of this page. This is the order it happened in — the decisions, the first results, and the things the February plan did not survive.

Before the season

GEMS Taiwan is a school team, not a university lab with a rolling cohort. Every year the roster empties and refills, and the people who carry anything across that gap are the advisors and the instructors — former members, a plant PhD, an electronics advisor, teachers who stay after the building closes. Before December 2025 there was no ReLeaf. There was a room, a bench, a set of pipettes, and a handful of people who had done this before and were willing to do it again.

What carried over was not a project. It was a habit: photograph everything, file it to the week it happened in, and write the reason down while you still remember it. Four hundred and eighty-nine photographs later, that habit is the only reason this page can be written at all. Every date below is the date on a file, not a date somebody reconstructed in October.

Winter: choosing the problem

December to February is the part of an iGEM year with nothing to show for it. No results, no parts, no hardware — just a large number of people trying to find one question they can all work on. It took us about ten weeks, and the answer we arrived at in February was not the answer we finished with.

  1. 13 December 2025

    Day one, and somebody has already brought an Arduino

    The first meeting was a room, a slide reading DEC 13, and forty-seven people who mostly did not know each other. No project had been chosen. On the same afternoon one member turned up with a microcontroller and a bag of jumper wire, which turned out to predict the shape of the year fairly well: the hardware line started before the biology did, and never stopped being ahead of it.

  2. 27 December 2025

    The first proposal is taken apart

    Two weeks in, a member brought a full project proposal to four advisors. It did not survive the meeting, and that was the point of the meeting. What came out of it was the rule we used for the next three months: a proposal has to name the person who is worse off if nobody builds it. Ideas that could not answer that question were dropped quickly after this, which is why the winter looks so empty and the spring does not.

  3. 1–5 February 2026

    A pitch, and the plan we spent the year departing from

    The first Human Practices meeting put the pitch in front of somebody who had no stake in liking it. Four days later the team drew the roadmap below: four parallel tracks — biosecurity, modelling, a stress-response switch, and a protectant — each ending in a field test. Read it now and the interesting part is not what it got right. It is that the entire hardware programme, which ended up being half the project, appears on it as the words Collaborate with Drylab in a blue box.

Spring: first builds

March to May is when the project stopped being a document. Three things happened in parallel and only one of them was planned: the wet lab learned to work, the project changed shape entirely, and the dry lab started building instruments because the ones we needed cost more than the team had.

  1. 7 March 2026

    The lab learns to work

    Training ran through March: sterile technique, gels, plates, the ordinary competence that has to exist before anything interesting can be attempted. Nobody in the room had held a micropipette in December. The protocols that came out of this stretch are in Experiments, and the provision that made it safe to run them is in Safety.

  2. 27 March 2026

    An organism

    At Professor Cheng's molecular biochemistry lab at National Taiwan University the team learned the plant protocols it had been reading about, and left with Arabidopsis seed in a tube. A week later the Verslues lab at Academia Sinica showed us vertical agar plates and how plant stress is actually scored, which is a harder question than it sounds and one we were about to get wrong.

  3. 15 April 2026

    The pivot

    April is late to change a project. We changed it anyway. A meeting with Professor Chen Wen-liang, and a startup poster from the 2025 season that had been sitting in a folder since, made the same point from two directions: a protectant that is sprayed is a protectant that is mostly wasted, and the interesting problem is not what you spray but when and where you release it. The diagram below is the result. Stress switches a light on; the light activates one of several chambers of engineered B. subtilis; each chamber releases a different protectant through a selective membrane into the soil.

    Everything after this point in the year is that diagram being argued with. The Description page states it properly; what matters here is the date. Six weeks of February's roadmap were spent on a project that no longer existed by the time the seed arrived.

  4. 18–25 April 2026

    Drawing the machine

    Ten days after the pivot the device had a form. A hollow-fibre membrane cartridge does the one thing the diagram demands and nothing else does cheaply: it holds a dense bacterial culture on one side and lets small molecules cross to the other, without letting the organism itself out. Choosing it fixed the rest — a recirculation loop, a peristaltic pump, a medium reservoir, and somewhere to put the light. The reasoning is on Hardware and the volumes are in Bioreactor Calculations.

  5. 16 May 2026

    Saying it out loud to a farmer

    At the Taipei Expo farmers' market the team put the idea to people who grow things for a living, with a clipboard and no slides. This was the first external check on the pivot, and the answers were not the ones the pitch expected: the farmers we spoke to were less interested in a yield number than in whether a device would demand attention during the weeks they had none to give. That constraint runs through Human Practices and eventually through the hardware.

  6. 27–28 May 2026

    Two instruments we could not buy

    A light plate apparatus for optogenetic induction and an inline photometer for reading culture density are both standard equipment and both out of reach on a school budget. So the dry lab drew them on a whiteboard on 27 May and had a breadboard photometer producing its first readings the following evening, after school, built by two members of the wet lab who had not touched electronics before. Neither instrument was any good yet. Both existed, which was the milestone.

Summer: the long runs

June to August is the whole project compressed into eleven weeks. The school year ended, the team moved into the lab full time, and the record thickens accordingly — three hundred and sixty-nine of the year's photographs were taken in this stretch. It is also when most of the failures happened, because it is the first period in which there was enough built to fail.

  1. 6–10 June 2026

    The reactor holds a culture

    The first B. subtilis growth test in the hollow-fibre loop. No induction, no protectant, no measurement worth the name — just medium, cells, a peristaltic pump and the question of whether the thing leaked. It did not. Sampling from the loop and from a flask in parallel started the same week, and by 10 June there was a curve to argue with: the lumen of the cartridge is not a shake flask, and it does not behave like one.

  2. 17 June 2026

    The plates go on the rack the wrong way round

    Vertical agar plates work because gravity is a signal: roots grow down the plate and can be measured along its face. Racked the wrong way, the seedlings grow across the plate instead, and a fortnight of root-length data becomes a photograph of a mistake. This is that photograph. It is on this page rather than buried in the notebook because it changed the protocol — plate orientation is now checked and initialled by a second person, and no run has repeated it. The same week cost us a trehalose series to contamination.

  3. 16–19 June 2026

    The whole thing, on one whiteboard

    Somewhere in the middle of June the project stopped being four teams working on four things. Someone drew the entire system on a board — reactor, plasmid, promoter, ribosome binding site, secretion, membrane, diffusion, plant — and it fitted. Not because it was simple, but because by then everybody had enough of it in their head to check somebody else's half. The board was redrawn on the 19th with the team watching, and one member filmed it, which is the closest thing this project has to a design review record.

  4. 18–20 June 2026

    Prototype 1 meets two professors

    Within three days the bioreactor design was reviewed twice by people with no reason to be kind about it. Prototype 1 — a PES hollow-fibre membrane, a 300 mL medium reservoir, a peristaltic pump and a sampling valve — went up on the screen and came back down with a list. The design-build-test cycle recorded in Engineering as cycle 2 starts the day after this meeting.

  5. 24–29 June 2026

    The lesson meets its first classrooms

    The education programme ran its preliminary trial on 24 June and its first full trial the day after. Both used the leaf-disk experiment: spinach disks sink, then rise under a lamp as they photosynthesise, and stop rising in warm water. That is the ReLeaf premise in a beaker, and it worked. What did not work was the vocabulary — students told us the English terminology was the obstacle, not the biology, and the lesson was rebuilt around that before the next trial. Education carries all four rewrites and the survey data behind them.

  6. 2 July 2026

    The team learns git

    One afternoon, one whiteboard, four numbered items: show the main workflow, forking problems, marking the mistakes, and a starting task. Before this, the wiki was one person's folder. After it, it was a repository a dozen people could write into without destroying each other's work. Every page on this site, including this one, is downstream of that hour — the handover notes are in Contribution.

  7. 7 July 2026

    A hunch about small farms becomes a map

    The GIS group put the project to the creative director of the Berkeley Historical GIS Project, and came away with a method rather than an opinion. The claim we wanted to make — that Taiwanese agriculture is thousands of small plots rather than a few large ones, and that this changes what a device has to be — was until that afternoon a thing we believed. The mapping programme on Geospatial Analysis is what turned it into 2.79 million parcels and a colour scale.

  8. 9 July 2026

    A benchmark, in a bottle

    A day at CH Biotech (正瀚): a wall of the company's own research posters, a regulatory briefing from someone who files the paperwork for a living, and a bottle of their commercial plant protectant to take back to the lab. That bottle is why the efficacy work in Results has a benchmark instead of a baseline of zero, and the regulatory session is the spine of Laws and Regulations.

  9. 12 July 2026

    The reactor starts reporting on itself

    A pressure sensor on the loop reached an Arduino and the laptop drew a live trace. Until this afternoon every number about the reactor came from somebody reading a gauge and writing it down. After it, the rig was an instrument: it logged, it timestamped, and it could be left running overnight without a person in the room. The acquisition code is on Software.

  10. 17 July 2026

    The light plate, whole

    The 3D-printed parts arrived and the light plate apparatus was assembled whole for the first time, its wells lit amber and green. The unglamorous work was the week before it: every LED measured and sorted by intensity, taped out in rows on a table, because an optogenetic induction plate is only as good as its worst well. What the instrument is for is on Hardware; what it can and cannot claim is on Measurement.

  11. 19 July 2026

    The photometer works, and is wrong

    The inline photometer read a growing culture in real time, and then read it about four times too high against the bench Biodrop. This is a milestone in both directions, which is why it is here. The instrument worked: it sat in the loop, it sampled continuously, it produced a curve with the right shape. The numbers on that curve could not be used. Everything on Measurement that carries a caveat carries it because of this reading, and 23 August was spent chasing it.

  12. 21 July 2026

    A day on Farmer Chen's smallholding

    Not an interview. A day of work — cutting leaves, harvesting okra, kneeling in her soil while she explained what her plot does in August, and then being fed. The maps on Geospatial Analysis say that Taiwanese agriculture is thousands of small plots rather than a few large ones; this was the day that stopped being a statistic. A device for her farm cannot need mains power, a technician, or a spare hour a day, and the case in Entrepreneurship is written to those limits because of this visit.

  13. 23 July 2026

    The hydroponic line is abandoned

    The hydroponic setup had been running since May and the plants in it kept dying in ways the team could not attribute. On 23 July it was taken apart and the failures were examined one seedling at a time rather than in aggregate, which is the only way we found the pattern. The decision was to stop: soil, from here on, for every experiment that matters. It cost a whole line of work and it is the reason the protocols in Plants are soil-based despite hydroponics being far easier to instrument.

  14. 30 July 2026

    Everything that had gone wrong, written on a board

    A week later the team put the year's failures on a whiteboard in three columns — agar, hydroponics, soil — with the problems listed down the right in red: 3D print, troubleshooting, contamination, seeds disappearing, agar disappearing, plant stress, medium renewal. It is the most useful hour of the summer and the least photogenic. Almost every entry on that list has a section somewhere on this wiki; the ones that do not are the ones we ran out of time to solve, and they are named as such in Engineering.

  15. 3 August 2026

    One cabinet, one system

    The reactor, the inline photometer, the control electronics and the plants were running in the same incubator at the same time, and the growth curve on the screen was being produced by instruments the team had built. It is the first day the project was one system rather than three, and the first long unattended run — the thing a school team almost never gets to do, because it requires trusting your own hardware overnight.

  16. 6 August 2026

    A chassis that will not eat its own product

    A visit to National Chung Hsing University ended with a plate of B. subtilis WB800 — a strain with eight extracellular proteases knocked out. That is the difference between a secreted peptide that survives in the medium and one that is digested by the organism producing it, and it is the single most consequential reagent anybody handed us all year. The strain work is on Parts; the peptide it has to keep intact is on Peptide Design.

  17. 8–11 August 2026

    The argument, in two minutes

    The promotion video was shot over one long day on a set built in the team's own room — the reactor lit green, the whiteboard behind it carrying the claim in marker, two members in front of the camera and a dozen behind it. It went up on 11 August. Writing two minutes that survive a stranger's attention is a harder edit than writing this page, and it forced a decision the wiki had been avoiding: what is the single sentence this project is for.

  18. 11 August 2026

    What the alternative costs

    A commercial vertical farm answered a question the project has to answer honestly: controlled-environment agriculture already solves crop stress, at a capital cost no smallholder will ever meet. Standing under six storeys of LED-lit lettuce is the clearest possible statement of the gap ReLeaf is aimed at. The comparison, with numbers rather than impressions, is drawn out on Sustainability.

  19. 14 August 2026

    The third classroom

    The high school session: the third age band, the third rewrite of the lesson, and the first audience old enough to argue back about regulation. The order the programme was rebuilt into is visible in the photograph — the microscopes are out before the slides start, because mechanism first and argument last is the only sequence that produced a change in the survey data.

  20. 22 August 2026

    Safety on the record

    The lab's safety provision was photographed item by item — biosafety cabinet, eye wash, fire blanket, spill kit, first aid, signage — because a safety form that asserts things is worth less than one that shows them. It is filed on Safety. The same afternoon produced the ordinary bench photographs that go with it: the point of the record is that the cabinet is in use, not that it exists.

  21. 23 August 2026

    The reactor moves house, and the photometer is put on trial

    The bioreactor was rebuilt into a computer case: rigid, ventilated, closable, and carryable — the first time the hardware was designed for somewhere other than the bench it was born on. The same day was spent recalibrating the photometer against the Biodrop with a TiO₂ standard, chasing the four-fold error from 19 July. The standard was chosen because it scatters rather than absorbs, which is what a bacterial suspension does; whether that closed the gap is stated, with the residual, on Measurement.

  22. 29 August 2026

    Saying it out loud, again

    Booth material and presentation rehearsals began nine weeks out. The first run-throughs were in Chinese, in front of the team's own poster, to an audience of one. Explaining the project to somebody standing in front of you finds the holes in it faster than rereading the wiki does, which is the same lesson as 27 December 2025 and 20 June 2026, learned for the third time.

Autumn: the argument

This page is being written on 30 August 2026, so what follows is a plan rather than a record. The hollow dots are the honest part: these are dated commitments, not achievements, and each one will be rewritten in the past tense or explained away when it arrives.

  1. September 2026

    Characterisation, and the calibration that is still open

    The switch and protectant lines have to produce data that survives being plotted next to the CH Biotech benchmark, and the photometer has to agree with a reference instrument before any of that data means anything. Whatever the calibration ends up being, it will be stated on Measurement with the residual error attached rather than smoothed out.

  2. October 2026

    The wiki freeze

    Every page written, every image and the typeface re-hosted on static.igem.wiki, every scaffold note deleted, and the parts submitted to the registry. The publishing checklist this team is working from is in the repository as notes/publishing.md.

  3. 1–7 November 2026

    Grand Jamboree, Biomanufacturing Village

    Where the argument gets made to people whose job is to find the hole in it. The photograph folder already has a week reserved for it.

How this page was assembled

Nothing on this timeline was reconstructed from memory. Since December 2025 the team has filed every photograph under the month, sub-team and week it was taken in, named YYYYMMDD_Subteam_Type_Description, with the date being the day of the shot rather than the day of the upload. That gives 489 images across 38 weeks, and it is the primary source for every entry above — the date on a milestone is the date on a file.

Two consequences worth stating. First, the record is uneven in a way that reflects the year rather than the project's importance: December has three photographs and June has one hundred and thirty-seven, because in December there was nothing to photograph. Second, several entries here are failures, and they are here because the filing system made them impossible to quietly lose. A photograph named ...GetsPutOnTheRackWrongDirection... is not a photograph anybody deletes.
