On this page

The year in four phases

  1. Idea Formation, March to April

    We began by defining the biological problem and testing our original concept. Feedback from Dr. Lin, Prof. Cheng, and Dr. Verslues helped us narrow our focus from a broad PGPR system targeting multiple stresses to a more controlled and testable biological concept towards plants.

  2. System Design, April to May

    We changed the biological concept into a practical bioreactor system. Prof. Chen’s feedback shifted us toward a controlled bioreactor, whereas Dr. Brophy led us to implement physical containment and membrane-based delivery.

  3. System Optimization, June to August

    With the overall system design structured, we focused on refining its individual components. We started to interview experts for feedback that lead to improvements to the bioreactor, protectant validation, dosage and delivery, and plant-testing conditions.

  4. Real-World Implementation, September

    In the final stage, we connected our development to real-world application. We focused on system reliability, farmer usability, regulation, and whether our experiments clearly demonstrated our proposed mechanism.

From our Integrated Human Practices timeline. December to February, before the first phase, went on choosing a project; October and November are still ahead.

April 2026

  1. 3 Apr

    Dr. Verslues, Academia Sinica

    During our meeting with Dr. Verslues, we gained valuable insights into the bacterial aspects of our project. More specifically, he consulted us on the mechanisms of drought and the proline delivery system.

  2. 10 to 15 Apr

    Professor Chen, and the pivot to a bioreactor

    In April, the meeting with Prof. Chen, also known as the founder of Agritalk, provided us with feedback that shaped the direction of both our hardware development and business plan, which allowed us to gain more understanding of the strategy for setting up a business plan and advised and suggested us on the hardware design. By 15 April the project had its present shape: stress switches a light, the light activates engineered B. subtilis, and protectants pass through a selective membrane to the plant.

  3. 18 Apr

    Dr. Brophy, and lab training with Ian

    Through this exchange, we received many valuable pieces of advice regarding circuit design for B. subtilis and hardware design. This meeting influenced the direction of our project by highlighting several potential problems of the kill-switch design.

  4. 25 Apr

    Drawing the machine

    A hollow-fibre membrane cartridge became the core of the device: it holds a dense culture on one side and lets small molecules cross to the other. A pump, a reservoir and a light followed from that choice.

June 2026

  1. 6 to 10 Jun

    The reactor holds a culture

    The first B. subtilis growth test in the hollow-fibre loop, sampled alongside flask cultures. The loop did not leak, and the culture in the lumen did not grow like the one in the flask.

  2. 16 to 19 Jun

    The whole system on one whiteboard

    Reactor, organism, construct, secretion, membrane, diffusion and plant were drawn on one board for the first time, and redrawn on the 19th with the team watching.

  3. 17 Jun

    Plates the wrong way round

    A set of vertical plates was racked the wrong way, so the seedlings grew across the plate instead of down it, and a trehalose series was lost to contamination the same week.

  4. 18 Jun

    Professor Chang reviews the bioreactor

    In June, Prof. Chang visited our team and discussed our bioreactor design with us while also providing valuable insights for the B. subtilis challenges. The main problem we wanted to consult was the design of our hollow-fiber membrane bioreactor.

  5. 20 Jun

    Professor Chen visits

    During Prof. Chen’s visit, he provided feedback on different aspects, including the math model, plant model, protectant, hydroponics, gene circuit, and bioreactor.

    2 more photographs

  6. 24 to 29 Jun

    The first classrooms

    This year, our team visited 3 elementary schools spanning over the months of June and July. During these lessons, we delivered an interactive Synbio workshop titled “Little Plant Guardians: Combating Environmental Stress”. Yongping Elementary School on 24 June, Dingxi Elementary School on 25 and 26 June, and the Affiliated Experimental Elementary School of University of Taipei on 29 June.
