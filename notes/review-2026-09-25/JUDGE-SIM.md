# Judge simulation, ReLeaf wiki, 25 September 2026

Written by reading the wiki at `http://localhost:8792/` as four judges would: a
two minute pass and a twenty minute pass on every page that carries a medal
criterion or a special award, plus one whole-wiki pass for Best Wiki. Review
notes (`aside.review-note`) and the rule check tray were stripped before
reading, as instructed, so nothing below counts them against the team. Team is
GEMS Taiwan, iGEM 2026, Biomanufacturing Village, high school section (roster
is Freshman/Sophomore/Junior at nine schools, so this is judged in the High
School Competition).

Everything here is written to be useful, not to be nice. The work is well above
what a high school team usually brings. The gap between what the team has done
and what a judge can *credit* is the whole subject of this document.

## 1. What I verified against iGEM, and what I inferred

Verified live on `competition.igem.org` on 25 September 2026, and cross-checked
against the local copy of the 2026 Judge Handbook at
`/Users/timmy/Documents/Claude/Projects/Releaf_Wiki/Past drafts/2026-judge-handbook-digital.md`:

- **Bronze has three criteria in 2026, not four.** They are Competition
  Deliverables ("Safety Forms, Wiki, Judging Form, Presentation Video, and
  Judging Session"), Project Attributions, and Contribution. **There is no
  Project Description medal criterion in 2026.** The brief I was given assumed
  one. `/description` is still worth its place, but no ballot line depends on
  it.
- **Bronze #2 no longer requires the embedded iframe.** Handbook, Bronze row 2:
  "Teams must still complete and submit the standard Attributions Form through
  the Deliverables Dashboard, but they are no longer required to embed an
  iframe of the form on their Wiki."
- **Silver #1** Engineering Success, standard URL `/engineering`. **Silver #2**
  Human Practices, standard URL `/human-practices`. Wording as quoted in
  section 3 below.
- **Gold is new in 2026.** Judges cast no separate Gold vote. The team selects
  exactly three Special Awards on the Judging Form, at least one from General
  Bio Engineering (Measurement, Model, New Basic Part, New Composite Part, New
  Improved Part, Part Collection) and at least one from Specializations
  (Education, Entrepreneurship, Hardware, Inclusivity, Integrated Human
  Practices, Alternative Platform, Safety and Security, Software, Sustainable
  Development). Each judge's yes/no on each of those three awards is inferred
  from their rubric score. **All three must come back yes for Gold.**
- **The cap is three, full stop.** "Teams cannot apply for more than three
  Special Awards total, whether they are seeking a gold medal or not."
- **Best Wiki and Best Presentation are "All Teams" awards.** Every team
  competes for them automatically and they do not use up any of the three
  elections.
- **High school is scored as its own section** for Special Awards and Village
  Awards. Judges are told to judge high school teams "just as you would a
  standard collegiate team" but to weigh smaller budgets, less lab access and
  shorter hours, and to be careful in their comments.
- **Exact ballot questions** for all eleven awards below are quoted from
  `competition.igem.org/judging/awards/special`, read live. They differ in
  places from the handbook prose, and the ballot is what judges click.
- **Dates**: Project Safety Form final version due 7 October 23:00 GMT+8. Wiki,
  Attributions Form, Judging Form, Registry Contributions and Software all
  freeze 21 October 23:00 GMT+8. That is 26 days from today.

Inferred, not verified: the per-question 1 to 6 rubric wording is deliberately
unpublished by iGEM, so "meets the performance standard" is my judgement of
where a 4 (good work, complete) turns into a 5 (great work with impressive
components). Where I say "at risk" I mean I would score 3 or 4 rather than 5.

### 1.1 The strategic finding that outranks everything else

`notes/structure.md` lists nine special awards as targets, and nine pages carry
an award callout: Measurement, Model, Hardware, Software, Education,
Entrepreneurship, Sustainability, Inclusivity, Safety and Security, plus Best
IHP on Human Practices. **The team may elect three.** The Judging Form is the
only place the election happens, it freezes 21 October, and no election means
no Gold.

This is not a wiki problem, it is a decision the team has to make in the next
two weeks, and the wiki should then be aimed at it. Keeping all nine pages is
fine and costs nothing. Spreading the last 26 days of effort across all nine is
what costs the medal. My recommendation is in section 6.

## 2. Bronze

### Bronze #1, Competition Deliverables

**Two minute pass.** Not a wiki judgement. The wiki itself exists and is far
past the bar. What a judge sees on their dashboard is whether the Safety Forms,
Judging Form, Presentation Video and Judging Session are done.

**Twenty minute pass.** Three of the five are outside this repository and I

Safety Form is in hand, and the 7 October PI deadline is before the freeze.

**Verdict: at risk, for reasons outside the wiki.** Disqualification language
is attached to the Project Safety Form ("Teams that do not submit the Project
Safety Form may be disqualified"), and no Judging Form means no medals at all.

**Three actions, best payoff first.**
1. Confirm with the PI, in writing, that the final Project Safety Form is

   entire season. (Deliverables Dashboard, not the wiki.)
2. Fill the `/safety-and-security` §1 risk table with the rows the rest of the
   page already argues (escape, leak at the pump head, chemicals, sharps,

   empty frame into evidence for Bronze #1 and the Safety award at once.
3. Submit the Judging Form early, with the three award elections on it, rather
   than on 21 October. An hour.

**What already impresses:** the wiki is done to a standard most collegiate
teams do not reach, and the safety page is honest enough to say containment is
"a design intent" rather than a result.

### Bronze #3, Contribution

Standard URL `/contribution`. Criterion: "Make a useful contribution for future
iGEM teams."

**Two minute pass.** Excellent. The page opens with one sentence that says
exactly what is on offer ("the set of measurement methods behind the two
instruments we built, written with the mistakes that produced them still
attached"), then a four row table: thirteen measurement methods, twenty-one
protocols, two rebuildable instruments, a troubleshooting record. Every row
says who it is for and links to where it lives. A judge can vote yes in ninety
seconds.

**Twenty minute pass.** This is the strongest short page on the wiki. Six
methods with the failure that produced each one ("Blank on distilled water, not
on medium. Blanking on medium put a roughly twenty per cent offset into the
readings"). A "How to reuse this" section written as a three week plan for a
team with a printer and an Arduino. A closing section that names five things
nobody should build on. That closing section is what turns a contribution page
into a credible one.

One structural risk, and the page raises it itself: "Eight Registry entries have
been opened and all eight are empty ... Until they carry the sequence, the
assembly and the screening evidence, no part on this wiki can carry Bronze
criterion 3." That is correct and correctly stated. It does not sink the
criterion, because the contribution being offered is methods and hardware, not
a part. But Registry Contributions freeze on 21 October too, and eight empty
entries are visible to any judge who follows the link.

**Verdict: likely met.** The methods-and-hardware contribution stands on its
own.

**Three actions.**
1. Change the page status from "Draft" and from "drafted 25 September 2026 from
   the wiki's own pages; team to check" to a checked, dated byline. A judge who
   reads "team to check" on a medal page wonders whether the team believes it.
   One hour of checking, and it is the single cheapest credibility gain on the
   wiki.
2. Put the four deliverables behind download links or explicit anchors, not
   only page names: the thirteen methods, the twenty-one protocols, the STL
   set, the six cloning cycles. A judge should be able to take the thing, not
   read about it. Two hours.
3. Fill at least the two or three Registry entries with the most evidence
   behind them (sequence, assembly route, screening gel) before 21 October, and
   say on this page which entries carry what. Half a day, and it also feeds any
   part award.

**What already impresses:** "Three of our written protocols carry errors we
have found and not yet corrected", with the three named. Very few teams at any
level publish that.

## 3. Silver

### Silver #1, Engineering Success

Standard URL `/engineering`. Criterion: "Demonstrate engineering success in a
technical aspect of your project by going through at least one iteration of the
engineering design cycle."

**Two minute pass.** Mixed, and this is the most important fix on the wiki. The
good news first: a boxed callout near the top reads "Silver Medal Criterion #1
· Engineering Success", names one complete iteration (cycle 3, the pGEM-T Easy
intermediate), spells out Design, Build, Test and Learn in one paragraph, and
offers four jump links straight to them. That is exactly what a judge needs and
it was not there two days ago.

The bad news: it sits inside section 01, which is titled "Gaps in this record",
and the meta block above it reads "Status: Draft, 12 fixes open". Immediately
below the callout is a twelve entry schedule of unwritten work, with student
names attached: "Test and learn are still missing", "The build sentence stops
at 'using', and the learn field reads 'We learned that'", "The text says four
constructs were designed and then lists three." A judge's first ninety seconds
on the page currently consist of the team listing what it has not written.

The page itself notes the schedule "comes off the page before the wiki freeze".
It has to.

**Twenty minute pass.** Once past the top, this is the best engineering page I
have read from a high school team. Six cloning cycles, each ending in the
change it caused. Real failure reporting: MoClo 2.0 giving background, the
ambiguity between a failed assembly and a PCR artefact, the marker clash where
pGEM-T Easy and the Level 1 destination vector both carry ampicillin, a module
whose "recombination premise did not survive". Twelve places where the team's
own records disagree, each boxed with both sources named and neither settled,
because settling them is the experimenters' decision. A "Claims and evidence"
table with four claims the evidence does not support. The links to Parts,
Results and Measurement work in both directions.

Two things a judge will hold against it. The criterion says documentation of a
part used for this criterion "must be on the Part's entry on the Registry", and
the eight Registry entries are empty. And the page is enormous: at phone width
it is 114,792 pixels tall, roughly 130 screens.

**Verdict: likely met, if the top of the page is fixed.** The evidence is
there. Presentation is what puts it at risk.

**Three actions.**
1. **Move the twelve entry schedule off the top and into a closing section, or
   off the page entirely, and raise the Silver #1 callout to the first thing
   under the title.** Two hours of cutting and pasting, and it is worth more
   than any other two hours available between now and the freeze. Keep the
   declared gaps and the twelve fix boxes, which are the page's credibility.
   What has to go is the to-do list with the students' names on it, because a
   judge reads it as unfinished work rather than as honesty.
2. Give cycle 3 a self-contained box at the top holding the actual evidence,
   not only prose: the colony PCR gel, the seven expected sizes, the date. A
   judge who wants one complete cycle should never have to scroll. Three hours.
3. Change "Status: Draft, 12 fixes open" to something that describes the page
   rather than its worklist, and do the same on the other ten pages carrying
   "Draft, N fixes open". The fix boxes stay; the status line changes. One
   hour across the wiki.

**What already impresses:** "Every light-gated cargo was also built
constitutively, so a negative result has one explanation fewer." That is an
engineering sentence, and it is the students'.

**Two minute pass.** Yes, immediately. Hero photograph of both halves of the

**Verdict: likely met.** This is the safest criterion on the wiki.

**Three actions.**

2. Put dates on the conclusion's claims the way the map does. A judge skimming

3. Resolve the "Pending" line in Open items (forum survey tally, booth practice
   questions, 5 September farm interview notes) or say plainly that the tally

**What already impresses:** "We went back to seven stakeholders, one of them

**Two minute pass.** Contender. "Five schools, three age groups, one lesson we

**Twenty minute pass.** Pre and post knowledge, attitude and practice surveys

**Verdict: contender.**

**What already impresses:** "An outreach event that ends when we leave the

Ballot: first customers and unmet needs; possible, scalable and inventive;
product development plans with realistic milestones, timelines, resources and
risks; skills, capabilities and stakeholders needed; positive and negative
long-term impacts.

**Two minute pass.** Plausible to contender. The opening paragraph names the

**Twenty minute pass.** Business model canvas, SWOT, PESTEL, market sizing,

revenue model, regulation moving from a checklist to a barrier to entry,
organic certification treated as a market they are excluded from).

What holds it back is ballot question 2. The MVP is described but not
demonstrated: there is no working unit with a cost, and the team's own box says
the bill of materials disagrees with itself three ways. A judge reading the
handbook line about "created a minimum viable product that customers want to
use" will mark that down.

**Verdict: plausible.** Strong for high school, short of the MVP the rubric
asks for.

**Three actions.**
1. Settle the bill of materials to one number with sources, and say what it

2. Put the MVP claim on its own: one photograph of the assembled unit, what it

3. Compress sections 5 to 9 by a third. Fifteen named sections is more

**What already impresses:** the team wrote a rule forbidding itself three

### Best Model (General Bio Engineering)

has been measured on the team's construct, so the middle model is literature

**Three actions.**

## 6. Overall

### The three elections that give the best Gold shot

Rules: at least one from General Bio Engineering, at least one from
Specializations, exactly three.

**My recommendation: Measurement (GBE), Integrated Human Practices
(Specialization), Education (Specialization).**

- **Measurement** is the strongest General Bio Engineering option by a wide
  margin. Thirteen measurements with state, unit, control and anchor, and a
  real external calibration. Model is the alternative and it is good, but two
  of its three models are unvalidated and the page is currently damaged by the
  deleted-page references.
- **Integrated Human Practices** is the safest yes on the wiki. It also carries
  Silver #2, so the work is required anyway.
- **Education** has documented instruments, pre and post data, released
  materials under an open licence, and a reflective failure (base pairing at
  junior high). It is the specialization where a high school team competes best
  against collegiate practice.

Hardware is the painful omission. It is visually the most impressive work and
the documentation is award standard, but two of four ballot questions (user
testing, demonstrated functionality) are currently unanswerable, and I do not
think they can be fully answered in 26 days. If the team runs the user testing
session in the first week and it goes well, swap Hardware in for Education and
reconsider. Entrepreneurship is the next alternative and is held back by the
unsettled bill of materials. Do not elect Software (eligibility and validation)
or Inclusivity (investigation depth).

### The single biggest risk to each medal

- **Bronze**: the Attributions Form has not been started, and the Project
  Safety Form is due 7 October. Both are off-wiki, both are yes/no, and either
  one alone stops a Bronze. This is a risk of omission, not of quality.

  and the callout is correct, but a judge's first ninety seconds are spent
  reading a twelve item list of what the team has not written, under a status
  line that says Draft. The criterion is met by the content and endangered by
  the layout.
- **Gold**: electing fewer than three awards, or electing three the wiki cannot
  support. Gold in 2026 requires three yes results out of three, so the
  elections are the medal. Spreading effort across nine award pages instead of
  three is the failure mode to avoid.
