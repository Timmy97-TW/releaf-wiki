# Writing on this wiki that is worth keeping

Eight reviewers read the whole site overnight. This file collects the passages
they praised, plus some found while checking their work, with every quote
verified against the live page. It is grouped by page, with the author where the
page header's "Written by" line names one. Quotes are short, twenty-five words
at most, so you can find them with your editor's search.

Read as a whole, the writing here does one thing far better than most iGEM
wikis: it reports what happened rather than what was hoped for. Failures are
kept with their dates and their causes, negative results are given the same
space as positive ones, and when a number is doubtful the page usually says
which number, why, and what would settle it. The sentences are short and the
verbs are concrete. Several pages set a rule for themselves in public, such as
not calling the device cheap until a bill of materials exists, or not claiming
an SDG without pointing at something built, and then keep it. Judges notice this
quickly, because it means everything else on the page can be trusted at face
value.

## Home page
*No "Written by" line*

- The integrated human practices lede: "Human practices here is a change log, not
  an attendance record." It sets a standard a judge can test on every card below
  it, which is exactly what the rubric asks.
- The synthetic biology lede: "light is the only signal that crosses into a
  sealed vessel without opening it." A design reason in one clause, stated as
  physics rather than as preference.
- The bioreactor's open items: fouling over a multi-day run, protectant transfer
  rate and growth at field temperature "are still open", printed next to the
  claims rather than after them.
- The scale figure: "A share of land, not a count of machines." Scale shown
  without inventing a number.
- The journey's closing card: "It goes on sale after 730 five-day windows. A
  flash drought needs one." Arithmetic a reader can check, landing as an
  argument.
- The big-picture alt text, on the reactor and the plants: "The two have never
  been connected." Four words that stop a reader over-reading a photograph, and
  most of the tiles do the same.

## Plants
*Written by: Wet lab, plant sub-team*

- Hydroponics, prototype 5: "Germination came back to 7 of 10 and so did the
  contamination." The prototype series deliberately reverses the one change that
  worked, so the diagnosis is confirmed instead of asserted. That is a control
  experiment inside a build log, and it is the best piece of experimental
  reasoning on the wiki.
- The ACC deaminase run: "The pretest was not a failed test of a protectant.
  There was no protectant in it." A distinction a judge is specifically checking
  for, made by the team before anyone asked.
- Overview: "Too little salt and there is no gap. Too much and the plant is dead
  before the protectant matters." It explains a whole assay design in two
  sentences, with no jargon.
- Verification: "A protectant assay that cannot tell you the protectant was
  absent is not an assay." Short, quotable, and it is the reason the rest of the
  page is believable.
- The contamination paragraph: "Our own reading, and that of the professors we
  asked, is the filtering step." Names the likely cause as an opinion, says
  whose, and does not dress it up as a finding.

## Measurement
*Written by: Wet lab, with dry lab and reactor*

- Chlorophyll extraction: "Normalising chlorophyll to fresh weight can invert
  the ranking of your own experiment." A warning written for another team,
  proved two paragraphs later with the team's own numbers, with a remedy that
  costs nothing. This is the strongest measurement writing on the wiki.
- Measurement map: "a spray that contains no protein is a spray of medium."
  Twenty words that explain why protein measurements sit beside plant ones.
- Physical measurements: "A dead seedling has to be a decision, and the decision
  has to be written down." The paragraph then shows a published mean of 54.98 mm
  that is seven measured values divided by nine. Naming your own summarisation
  fault, with the arithmetic, is rare and it reads as confidence.

## Experiments

## Results
*Written by: Wet lab and dry lab*

- Protectant function: "A three and a half fold effect with a broken control is
  a reason to run the experiment again." The largest effect in the plant data is
  reported and dismantled in the same breath. This is the paragraph a judge will
  remember.
- Culture monitoring: "We had expected that comparison to run the other way."
  One clause, and it buys the whole calibration section credibility.
- Containment: "A safety claim should not rest on an n of one."
- Light delivery: "No induction curve exists." Four words in bold, in the
  section that most needs them.
- What the chain does not close: "The parts that are open are the couplings",
  and a coupling cannot be tested until both halves work. That is an insight
  about the project rather than a summary of it.

## Protein Design

## Hardware, Bioreactor
*No "Written by" line; the notebook names the builders*

- 1.1: "The protectant has to get into the soil; the bacteria that make it must
  not." The whole design constraint in one sentence, followed by the admission
  that the biology cannot solve it alone. The best statement of need in the
  section.
- 5.1: the comparison ends "a promising difference, not a measured one", and
  earlier says identical medium and temperature "is true, and it is not the same

- 6.2: "the honest version of the specification is that three of its mechanical
  choices are being replaced." Few teams tell a judge that the machine described
  is not the one on the bench.
- 5.2: withdrawing the 50 kDa cut-off claim in public, with the week it happened.
