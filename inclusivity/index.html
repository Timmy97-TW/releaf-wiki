Who this project is for

ReLeaf is built for the people who farm Taiwan’s land, and most of that land is small. On our geospatial layer of rural regeneration communities, almost every community sits under the national average holding of 1.1 hectares, and only three sit above it. The average Taiwanese farmer is 63.5 years old, and more than half are over 65 (Geospatial Analysis). Those are the growers least able to absorb a lost crop, and the ones with the least market power (Entrepreneurship).

The second group is students. Synthetic biology reaches Taiwanese classrooms mostly as an argument about genetic modification, before students have seen how any of it works (Education). Our lessons were built for elementary, junior high and high school students who meet the field through a second language.

Review · Who this project is for

Works. Naming two concrete groups, with a number behind the first (holdings under 1.1 hectares, a mean farmer age of 63.5), is the right start for an Inclusivity case: judges look for a named group, not a general wish to include everyone.

Fix. This section was drafted overnight from Geospatial Analysis, Entrepreneurship and Education; a team member has to check it reads the way the team would say it. The Inclusivity rubric asks who is kept out of iGEM and synthetic biology, so say which of these groups has no voice in the field today and how you know, not only that they would benefit from ReLeaf. Barriers we found

Four barriers came up in our own work. Each is recorded on the page where we met it.

  • Language. After the second elementary trial, students told us the English was the hard part rather than the biology. Much of the vocabulary in this field only exists in English (Education, Sustainability).
  • Scattered advice. At the Farmer Expo and at Tamsui Happy Farm we saw that farm advice exists on government sites, research pages and social media, but it is scattered, and many smallholders are not comfortable searching for it (Human Practices).
  • Age. Young farmers are the likely first users; older farmers need a different case (Human Practices, Entrepreneurship).
  • Cost. A large single payment at the start of a season is the wrong instrument for growers who are paid after the harvest, and the price has not yet been tested with the growers it is meant for (Entrepreneurship, Sustainability).

Review · Barriers we found

Works. Every barrier here came from a named encounter (a trial, a farm visit, a market), which is the evidence judges ask for. The language finding is the strongest: students said it themselves, and the team changed the lesson because of it.

Fix. Drafted overnight from Education, Human Practices, Entrepreneurship and Sustainability; check each item against its source page. The original scaffold also listed land tenure, literacy, connectivity and gender; either add evidence for them or leave them out, and say so. Give the number of students or farmers behind each finding, for example how many survey comments named English as the problem on Education.

What we changed

  • Bilingual teaching. Every slide deck became bilingual, Chinese and English, and vocabulary became its own section of the lesson, taught through pictures and word prefixes instead of definitions (Education).
  • A booking route for any teacher. Cold email only reached schools we already knew, so a form on the education website lets any teacher book a workshop without an introduction (Education).
  • Advice where farmers already are. We built 農友助手 (Farmers’ Assistant), an official account on LINE, which is on almost every phone in Taiwan: a six-button menu and nothing to install (Human Practices).
  • Data you can hear. At the market booth, stress data was rebuilt as a lit map and as the slowed-down clicks of a plant short of water. Every age group took the headphones, including visitors too young to read the poster behind them (Data Physicalization).

One change is still a plan, not a result: a cross-subsidy in which commercial buyers help put a unit within reach of a smallholder (Entrepreneurship).

Review · What we changed

Works. These are design changes, not intentions, which is exactly what the scaffold asked for. Saying that the cross-subsidy is still a plan is honest and a judge will trust the rest more for it.

Fix. Drafted overnight from Education, Human Practices, Data Physicalization and Entrepreneurship; a team member must check it. For each change, add what happened next: did the bilingual deck change the survey result in a later trial, how many teachers used the booking form, how many farmers joined the LINE account. Language and access

This wiki is written in English, with Chinese kept where a name, a quote or a term is Chinese. The work that faces the public mostly runs the other way.

  • The lesson slide decks are bilingual, Chinese and English, and free to download (Education).
  • The education website has every one of its eight pages in English and in Traditional Chinese (繁體中文).
  • The LINE account for farmers is in Chinese (Human Practices).
  • At the market booth, the blue poster was headed in Chinese (作物的感官警報), and the visitors’ written answers are shown on the page in Chinese with an English translation beside them (Data Physicalization).
  • Interviews with farmers were held in Mandarin and are quoted in translation (Human Practices, Sustainability).

Review · Language and access

Works. A plain list of what exists in which language is easy to check, and the Chinese version of the whole education website is a real piece of access work, not a promise.

Fix. Drafted overnight from Education, the education website, Human Practices, Data Physicalization and Sustainability; check it. Say whether any farmer-facing material is in Taiwanese Hokkien or Hakka, since many older farmers speak those first; if nothing is, say that too. The wiki itself has no accessibility statement (screen readers, captions on videos, alt text); if the team checked any of that, it belongs here.

Inside the team

The Members page lists students from nine schools, from freshman to junior year, alongside student advisors and instructors. It lists nobody as a lead: each person is shown with the tasks they worked on, not a rank. Work at the bench without supervision was open to anyone who passed the wet lab training and the molecular cloning exam, on paper and at the bench, and the dry lab and human practices tracks have their own published requirements (Members).

Review · Inside the team

Works. Publishing the requirements for each track, so a student can see how to earn unsupervised bench work, is a concrete way of making access inside the team fair.

Fix. Drafted overnight from the Members page; a team member has to check every claim. The scaffold asked who got to do what and how that was decided: say who set the tasks and how students chose them. Say whether the team charged fees, and whether any student was supported to join who otherwise could not have; the Inclusivity Award rewards opportunities created for people who would not otherwise have them, and this is where that evidence would go.

What we learned

Two findings changed how we worked. The first came from students: the barrier in our lessons was English, not biology, which is why every deck is now bilingual (Education). The second came from farmers and industry: young farmers are likely to adopt first and older farmers need a different argument, so one pitch cannot serve everyone we are building for (Entrepreneurship).
