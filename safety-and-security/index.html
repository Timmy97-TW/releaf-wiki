Biological risk

Containment is a design intent

Retention has not been demonstrated in our hands, and the second containment layer has not been built. Contained use is a classification we are pursuing, not one we hold.

Chassis

Every organism number on this wiki is Bacillus subtilis strain 168. The protease-deficient strain WB800 was written into the electroporation protocol as the intended production host and never run, so the competent-cell method and the pulser settings for it are a plan and not a record (Experiments, fix E3). Cloning and storage are in Escherichia coli DH5α, which never leaves the bench (Experiments).

The risk group of the strain is not recorded anywhere on this wiki, and this page will not assert one until the safety officer has written the assessment down. Sporulation is not discussed in any of our records either, although it bears directly on whether an escaped cell persists. Both are marked below as work for a person.

Payload

Three cargoes have been built or designed, and they are described part by part on Parts. ACC deaminase degrades 1-aminocyclopropane-1-carboxylate, the immediate precursor of ethylene, so a stressed plant makes less of the hormone that tells it to stop growing. LEA14 is a late embryogenesis abundant protein that holds cellular structures together under dehydration. BoPep4 is a plant elicitor peptide from broccoli. None of the three is a toxin, an antibiotic resistance determinant or a virulence factor, and the only selection markers in the collection are the ampicillin, chloramphenicol and erythromycin resistances carried by the cloning and shuttle vectors (Experiments).

What the payload does in a non-target organism has not been tested by us and is not claimed here. Nothing on this wiki shows the light-responsive promoter changing output in either direction, so the expression level of any cargo is also unmeasured (Parts).

Escape

The containment claim is one membrane and one plate. The hollow fibre cartridge is rated at 0.2 µm and a B. subtilis cell is a rod of micrometre scale, so whole cells are expected to stay on the lumen side while a protein of tens of kilodaltons crosses (Hardware → Bioreactor). The test was done once: during the version 1 run on 6 June, 2 mL of shell-side liquid was spread on LB agar and no colonies grew. Replicate count, incubation time and incubation temperature were not recorded, and no photograph of the plate exists (Results, R8).

No log reduction value has been measured, and none should be published until it is. A plate with no colonies on it gives no bound on escape, because the detection limit of that plate was never stated (Measurement).

The membrane is also not the whole boundary. Version 1 of the reactor ended early when medium leaked at the pump head, and the tubing at the pump head carries lumen-side culture, so that event was a loss of containment as well as a loss of flow. The fix was procedural, BPT tubing in the pump head, and version 2 then ran a full cycle at 94 mL/min with nothing leaking (Measurement). The Luer sampling valves are the other place where procedure, not geometry, is what holds.

Chemical and physical risk

The reagents that need naming are the ones used most and the ones that bite hardest. Chlorophyll extraction uses 80% acetone by the millilitre, on an open bench and in a plate reader room, and it is also the step the team demonstrates to children (Experiments, Plants). Medium preparation uses KOH to set pH and an autoclave at 127 °C (Measurement). Selection plates carry ampicillin, chloramphenicol or erythromycin. Gels are cast with BioGreen Safe DNA gel dye rather than ethidium bromide, which removes the single worst reagent hazard a cloning bench usually carries (Experiments).

Three physical hazards sit in the instruments the team built. The electroporator delivers pulses up to 2.5 kV across a 0.2 cm cuvette, and our own record contains two arcing events at the start of the first session (Measurement). The DiOPAL array is a dual-wavelength LED panel switched in six columns of four, bright enough to be uncomfortable to look into (Hardware → DiOPAL). The reactor loop runs a pump at pressures the team has measured: 0.197 bar of loop pressure drop at 263 mL/min, against a transducer rated to 1 MPa (Measurement). Mains power sits next to a water-filled loop on the same bench, which is a hazard the hardware pages describe the build of but no page assesses.

Security and dual use

The design's best feature is a slot. The green-light responsive expression module keeps its promoter, ribosome binding site and terminator fixed and changes only the cassette behind the secretion peptide, and three different coding sequences have already been put through it (Parts). Anything that can be expressed and secreted by B. subtilis could in principle be put in that slot by somebody else, which is the dual-use question this project has to answer rather than avoid.

Four things limit it as built. The cargo has to be secreted, so it has to tolerate a signal peptide and the host's secretion machinery. The switch is uncharacterised, so nobody, including us, can currently state what the slot produces under light (Parts). The device is a benchtop reactor with a hand-assembled tubing loop, not a production line. And the parts we would submit are fusions and variants of plant-protective proteins, with no toxin, no virulence factor and no resistance determinant among them.

What that adds up to is not a security argument yet. It is a description of the attack surface. The judgement of whether these parts should be submitted with the slot described as retaskable, and what an entry should and should not say about it, belongs to the team and its PI.

Containment design

Engineered in, today, is one thing: the membrane. Culture circulates on the lumen side and never leaves it; what crosses the wall and goes to the field is the protectant together with anything else below the 0.2 µm pore, which includes medium components, vesicles and debris (Hardware → Bioreactor). Everything else that holds the organism in is tubing, connectors, Luer valves and a pump head, and that envelope is maintained by procedure.

Three instruments were designed to watch that envelope and none was built: a leakage detector, a membrane breach detector and a turbidity meter reading the shell-side stream for cells that should never be there. Each has a written principle, no threshold and no calibration, and Measurement gives the reason plainly, that testing a safety instrument means creating the failure it is built to catch.

There is no kill switch, no auxotrophy and no second containment layer on this project. Experiments points at this page for the kill switch argument; the argument is that we do not have one, and that the membrane and the enclosure are therefore doing all of the work.

Lab practice, training and supervision

This is a high-school team working in a host laboratory, and the record of how it was supervised is partial. What the wiki does establish: the plant protocols were learned in person at Prof. Cheng Mei-Chun's laboratory at NTU and at the Verslues laboratory at Academia Sinica before any of them were run here (Plants). From July, every plate-to-plate transfer was done inside a biosafety cabinet with an instructor present, and that single change is the one the team credits with cutting the contamination rate most (Plants). Dr. Kyle set standing rules weekly from June, including the rule that a contaminated plate contaminates its own data and the rule never to top up evaporated medium.

One failure of technique is in the record and worth keeping there. The ACC deaminase preparation for experiment set 8 was passed through its 0.22 µm filter on the open bench instead of inside the laminar flow hood, and the plates grew bacteria (Plants). The one sterile step in that protocol was done in the wrong place, and it cost the trial.

Who was trained on what, by whom, and who was permitted to work unsupervised is not written down on this wiki. That list has to come from the team.

Shipping, disposal and incidents

The disposal route is not documented on this wiki. Autoclaving appears in our protocols only as a preparation step, 20 min at 127 °C for medium, and a 1:100 bleach soak appears once, as an optional sanitising step for pots and trays (Experiments). Neither of those is a waste route for cultures, plates or reactor fluid. Nothing on this wiki says how the host laboratory disposes of them, and nothing here says anything about shipping.

Two incidents are in the record, both reported by the pages that own them. The version 1 reactor run ended early when medium leaked at the pump head, which was a loss of containment as well as a loss of flow, and the fix was BPT tubing in the pump head (Measurement). The filtration on the open bench described in the section above cost experiment set 8 its first trial. Neither was written up as a safety incident at the time, and neither has a date, a person or a corrective action recorded in a form.
