Protein Design

Abstract

The dry lab work that chose ReLEAF’s three protectants and decided what to change in each before any DNA was ordered.

They are BoPEP4, a Brassica oleracea plant elicitor peptide, LEA14, a late embryogenesis abundant protein, and ACC (1-aminocyclopropane-1-carboxylate) deaminase. The binding sites themselves turned out to have almost no headroom, so the engineering moved to the construct and the delivery.

It is a record of what we got wrong and caught in time. A purification tag every earlier check had approved turned out, once simulated, to abolish the one contact the peptide depends on. The largest gain available on ACC deaminase, an enzyme, is a change to the medium it works in.

Findings

  • The Asn23 clampFive contacts hold the C-terminus, not one salt bridge
  • Truncation ladder9–23 is the production length; it gives up the Lys7 anchor
  • The purification tagSix histidines on the C-terminus abolish the salt bridge
  • Apoplast pHAt pH 5.5 the Glu12–His227 contact holds in 99.9% of frames
  • Codon optimizationThe 5′ leader swap opened the start site, not the recoding

What went to the wet lab

Three protectants, three sends. Each lane holds two boxes: what went out, with the reason protein design chose it and a link to the run it came from, and below it what was considered and rejected. The second box matters as much as the first, because what was ruled out, and on what evidence, is the other half of the result.

BoPEP4

Peptide · the ladder, sent whole

A 23-residue peptide that switches on the plant’s own stress response by binding a receptor, PEPR1. A shorter peptide is cheaper for the bioreactor to make, so the question was how much could be cut from the front before it stopped working. Nothing computational separated the best candidates, so the whole ladder went out and the concentration decides.

Sent to the wet lab

  • D-03 / D-05BoPEP4 1–23

    Full length. The reference every shorter rung is measured against.

    Truncation ladder →
  • N-HisBoPEP4 7–23

    Best of the series once simulated: the grip on the receptor held 98.5% of the time against 77.1% for the shorter rung below.

    Truncation ladder →
  • D-01 / D-04BoPEP4 9–23

    Cheapest to make, at 33% less mass, with the contacts that anchor it still intact.

    Truncation ladder →
  • N-HisBoPEP4 15–23

    Cut past the point where the modeling says it breaks. Nine residues is too short to reach along the receptor groove, so this is the ladder’s own negative.

    Truncation ladder →
  • D-02G17A

    A deliberate failure. Published at more than 4000-fold weaker, it calibrates the assay every other activity claim rests on.

    G17A →
  • D-06S15A

    The second control, published at roughly 100-fold weaker. No substitution meant to improve the peptide was ordered, because none was found worth ordering.

    S15A →
  • all of themN-terminal 6×His

    The purification tag goes on the front. On the back it caps the one chemical group that forms the peptide’s strongest contact with the receptor, which would have given a molecule that purifies well and cannot work.

    N-His vs C-His →

Considered and rejected

  • K18R

    The contact it was built on held only half the time once simulated, so the reason for making it went away.

  • K8Q + K18R

    The two prediction methods disagreed on this one more sharply than on anything else in the project.

  • Δ1-2, Δ1-3

    The strongest binders of the whole panel. Trimming two residues saves too little to be worth making.

  • Tandem repeats

    Ruled out on chemistry alone: only the last copy in a chain keeps the end group the peptide needs.

LEA14

Folded protein · five N-His constructs

A 151-residue plant protein that helps cells survive drying and salt. It is not an enzyme: one copy protects one thing and is not reused, so the only ways to get more effect are to deliver more copies or make each copy do more. These five test both, by trimming the loose ends and by bolting on a membrane-binding motif it does not naturally have.

Sent to the wet lab

  • + K-segmentK–GGGGS–WT

    Adds an ability LEA14 lacks. The K-segment sticks to membranes as a cell dries; LEA14 cannot do this on its own, so this asks whether borrowing it makes each copy count for more.

  • + K-segmentK–GGGGS–T1-16

    The same addition on a trimmed background, so the pair below isolates exactly what the K-segment contributes.

    LEA14 runs →

    The matched control: the same trim with no K-segment. Comparing these two is the experiment.

  • −1–23T1-23

    The deeper front trim. The first 23 residues have no fixed shape in the solved structure, and loose ends are where protein-chewing enzymes start.

    Conservation alignment →
  • −146–151T146-151

    The back end instead. Those last six residues are loose and water-repelling, which is the usual starting point for copies clumping together into something useless.

Considered and rejected

  • Tighter partner binding

    Skipped. The published work disagrees on whether LEA14 protects its partner or blocks it, so tighter binding could help or harm.

  • BP100 cell-entry tag

    Held. There is no assay here that can tell protein inside a plant cell from protein stuck to the outside, so the result would be unreadable.

  • Chloroplast entry tag

    Held. A plausible mechanism with no published test of an applied protein behind it.

ACC deaminase

Enzyme · no mutants sent

A bacterial enzyme that lowers plant stress by eating ACC, the molecule plants turn into the stress hormone ethylene. Unlike the other two it is a true catalyst, so one copy works over and over. That is why the decisive result here is not a mutation.

Sent to the wet lab

  • WT onlyACC deaminase wild type

    No mutant was sent. The enzyme meets far less ACC than it could handle, so how much working enzyme is present matters more than how good the enzyme is. On the modeling, the largest gain available is a change to the conditions it works in instead of to the protein, which is why no variant was ordered.

    Conservation and stability screen →
  • designedC41V, C67A, C162A, C282A

    A version with four of its six reactive sulfur groups removed, which should keep longer in storage. Designed and held for a season with bench capacity. Two of the six stay, both buried where they cannot cause trouble.

    Unpaired cysteines removed →

Considered and rejected

  • F156C

    Would staple the fold together with a new cysteine. The family keeps an aromatic here in 97% of cases and the stability screen fails it. It was also designed against the wrong organism’s sequence, which is how the naming error was caught.

  • V293T

    The only route to shifting the working pH. Self-rated at 25% before any calculation, and conservation and stability both came back against it.

  • E295D

    Shifts the pH as intended but costs tenfold activity to do it, which is worse than where it started.

  • Arginine clamp

    Every related enzyme grips the substrate with an arginine; this family does not. There is nowhere in the crowded pocket to put one.

K-segment
A short stretch, EKKGIMDKIKEKLPG, found in related plant proteins. It sticks to membranes and protects them as a cell dries out. LEA14 has no such stretch of its own.
GGGGS
A flexible linker of five small residues. It joins the K-segment to LEA14 without either half interfering with how the other folds.
6×His
Six histidines added to one end so the protein can be pulled out of a mixture and purified. Which end it goes on is a design decision, not a formality.
T1-16, T146-151
Truncations. The number range is the piece removed, counted from the start of the protein.
