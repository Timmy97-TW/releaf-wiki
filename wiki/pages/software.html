Digital Twin

Abstract

An interactive software that integrates real-time field sensing, bioreactor monitoring, and automated control to make ReLEAF accessible to farmers.

To make ReLEAF easier to monitor and operate, we developed a digital twin that connects field sensors, bioreactor measurements, and automated controls in one interface. The system tracks crop conditions alongside bioreactor data, including optical density, pH, pressure, and flow, allowing users to monitor both plant health and production status. By integrating real-time measurements with predictive monitoring, the platform helps farmers receive early warnings, track system performance, and manage ReLEAF without specialized biomanufacturing knowledge.

Digital Twin and ReLEAF's System

ReLEAF's bioreactor is built to stand beside a rice field and run for weeks, looked after by a rice grower with no laboratory training. The software therefore has five jobs: show what the culture is doing, raise an alarm when a reading or the culture drifts, predict the biomass a few hours ahead, judge from the weather forecast and the crop whether the protectant will be ready when it is needed, and give the answer in plain language. Live readings cover only the first job. The others need the batch simulated ahead of the vessel and the result returned to it. That return is what separates a digital twin from a digital model, which is updated by hand, and a digital shadow, which receives live data but needs a person to act on it [1, 2].

ReLEAF is an AIoT system: the bioreactor's sensors and valves are wired to a board that logs every reading, a sensor beside the crop reads leaf temperature and leaf wetness, and models learn from those readings. An autoencoder trained on the healthy first 120 hours of the 434-hour run flagged a failing photometer 26.7 hours before its reading became unusable (R2). The amount of protectant cannot be read in-line; it comes from samples drawn by hand and assayed by ELISA and DNPH (section 4). So far the models have run on recorded data after each run and a person makes every decision; the next Digital Twin joins them to the live loop.

Leaf wetness and the rice blast rule

Leaf wetness is not itself a stress for rice. It matters because blast fungus needs a wet leaf to infect, and infection depends on how long the leaf stays wet and how warm it is, so one sensor reads both channels. The twin applies the BLASTAM rule for leaf blast [3], which counts a wet period as an infection condition when the leaf stays wet for at least 10 hours at a mean of 22 to 25 °C, asks for longer as it gets cooler, and allows no infection below 15 °C or above 25 °C [4]; a dry hour ends the period. Hours already past come from the sensor, and hours ahead are estimated from the forecast, counted as wet when relative humidity reaches 90% [5]. When a period meets the rule, the interface tells the grower to look for lesions five to ten days later.

The sensor has only been on a bench (R3) and its wet threshold is a default. The rule has not been checked against Taiwanese disease records, and a met condition means only that the weather allowed infection. ACC deaminase has not been tested against blast, so blast risk is shown to the grower but does not switch the green light.

Run 7 is the longest clean run: 77 hours in one file, OD to 3.34, no window lost to low signal. Two changes made it possible. Run 5's reading broke down after ten hours, with bubbles in a sixth of its windows and the reference drifting 4.8-fold, so run 6 halved the permeate flow to 12 mL/min and added an acceptance gate that holds the last good value when a window fails its checks. Run 6 then read cleanly to 2.71 at 37 h before its sample beam was lost; run 7 passed that point at 44 h and kept reading.

Fouling stayed far from a cleaning point: the extra pressure across the membrane rose from 2.0 to 3.8 kPa over run 6 and from 1.9 to 4.4 kPa over run 7, as permeability halved from 2520 to 1210 LMH/bar. Each short spike in that trace coincides with zero permeate pressure, which is the permeate side being opened. On run 7 the pH loop logged 53 base doses against a 7.00 target, yet pH still ranged from 6.34 to 7.97.

Photometer calibration against TiO₂ and SiO₂

The photometer follows the BioDrop with slope 1.018 (R² 0.995) up to OD 1.2, and a threefold dilution reads back within 0.2%.

TiO₂, August 23

45 paired points from cumulative TiO₂ additions to a stirred 400 mL loop, read in-line and on the BioDrop spectrophotometer.

SiO₂, October 10

iGEM's silica protocol on the same loop: 2.5 mg steps from 2.5 to 100 mg in 500 mL of 1% SDS, read at each step on the BioDrop and both photometer builds, and logged in-line every 5 s for 4 h 27 min.

At 137.6 min the suspension was diluted and then restored, which tests proportionality without a known dose: 2.3610 before, 0.7943 diluted, 2.3665 restored (SD at most 0.016). The ratio is 2.97 down and 2.98 back, and the restored reading is within 0.2% of the original.

On a plant, leaf minus air temperature is a water-stress signal, and a 1.2 to 1.9 °C bias can hide or invent it, so the bias must be removed before the twin acts on it.

Module pressure drop

Wall shear calculated from the pressure sensors and from the flow meter agrees to 2.8%, so each sensor checks the other. On August 16, 2026 water at 22 °C was pumped at eight speeds with the hollow fiber module fitted and with a plain tube in its place. At our running flow of 263 mL/min the module loses 0.096 bar, 1.03 times the prediction for 11 open fibers, so none are blocked.

Batch forecasting

The Kalman filter beat persistence on both runs scored: 41% less error at 2 h in September, 24% at 8 and 12 h on run 7. On the 102-hour perfusion run of 2 to September 6, 2026 (Bacillus subtilis 168 secreting ACC deaminase, OD 0.84 to 3.77), four models forecast every hour from 12 h using only past data, against persistence, which predicts no change.

Global linear weighs old data as much as new, and exponential assumes growth keeps accelerating. Local linear came close; the Kalman filter also weighs each reading against the noise, and it is the model in the twin.

Registered forecast

Registered on October 1, before any WB800 bioreactor run, and scored on run 6: the growth rate landed inside its interval, 0.286 against 0.2705 to 0.3300 h⁻¹. The prior came from the light plate (R6). Run 5 broke down after ten hours, so run 6 is the scored run. Run 7 is not scored: its three-hour lag at OD 0.19 has no term in the registered logistic, and its fitted rate swings from 0.16 to 0.46 h⁻¹ with the choice of window.

The twin times induction from both ends. From the field, the forecast and the crop-stress rule give the hour the crop comes under stress, and the light must come on earlier by the time protectant takes to make. From the vessel, the prior sets a target time at hour zero, the Kalman filter updates it, the sensor alarm decides which readings to trust, and the fouling trace shows whether the membrane will still pass permeate. If the culture is ready in time, the twin names the hour to induce; if not, it warns days ahead that the batch cannot meet the demand.

The assays cannot yet set how long to induce. The ELISA figure is flat from hour 1 to hour 41, as expected for a constitutive strain without light; both assays are single samples, and the activity reading is below the quantitation limit with its two readouts 22-fold apart. A person still decides the induction length until the assays are repeated on an induced batch and green light is fitted to the bioreactor.

The Software

The Digital Twin

Run 7 ran on this version for 77 hours: 53 automatic base doses, 90 shell-valve collections, and the 0.70 bar pressure trip armed but never needed (peak 0.57 bar). Firmware v4.2.2 (sketch/sketch.ino) on the Uno Q’s microcontroller samples the instruments and holds every interlock. Backend v6.2.2 (python/main.py) on the board’s Linux side computes optical density, growth rate, membrane values and alarms, runs pH control, logs to disk and serves the interface. Interface v6.2.2 (assets/index.html) shows the live readings and sends pump and valve commands; it holds no interlock.

The next Digital Twin

The next version brings leaf wetness and the weather forecast into the green-light decision. Its seven tabs are what to do now, a live dashboard, growth against its forecast band, weather and blast risk, the batch record, Ask, and setup. It stores every run, so its predictions improve as runs accumulate.

In the Ask tab a farmer pastes their own AI provider’s API key (Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini) and asks about the screen in plain language, for example why the green light is still off. The assistant sees the readings, warnings and advice on screen; it can explain them but cannot move a valve or the light. The key is not saved. On the iGEM wiki the chat is off, because the wiki allows no outside requests.

Current limits

Five things are not yet in the twin.

  • Loop temperature: the probe is fitted but not responding, so the runs used a temperature entered by the operator.
  • Dissolved oxygen: calibration of both probes did not hold, and neither probe can be autoclaved.
  • Green light: induction was tested on the light-programmable array only and is not built into the bioreactor.
  • Light inside the culture: not measured; a dense culture dims it by an unknown amount.
  • Photometer upper limit: run 6 lost its sample beam above OD 2.7, while run 7 read to 3.34. Above wherever the beam fails, samples are drawn by hand.
