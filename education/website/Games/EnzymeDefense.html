Enzyme-Substrate Match Defense

Enzyme Defense is an educational mini-game designed to illustrate the concept of enzyme specificity and our project’s application in degrading PET plastic via TfCut2. Players control an enzyme with shape-specific active sites, catching matching substrates (representing PET molecules) to degrade them and prevent landfill buildup.

This game aims to:

  • Demonstrate enzyme-substrate specificity through the lock-and-key mechanism.
  • Simulate real-world PET degradation by showing the accumulation of plastic waste if not degraded efficiently.
  • Engage players interactively to understand how engineered enzymes like TfCut2 work in breaking down PET for recycling.

Gameplay Mechanics:

  • Controls: Use ← and → arrow keys to move.
  • Objective: Catch only matching substrates to degrade them.
  • Challenge: Missing substrates increases landfill buildup; catching the wrong shape stuns the enzyme temporarily.
  • End Condition: The game ends after 3 levels (20 seconds each).

Educational Impact:

By visualizing how enzymatic degradation works in an entertaining way, Enzyme-Substrate Match Defense educates the general public on:

  • The importance of enzyme engineering in environmental biotechnology
  • The urgency of PET pollution and landfill overflow
  • The potential of synthetic biology in creating circular solutions for plastic waste
