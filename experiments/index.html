Overview

ReLeaf makes plant protectants in Bacillus subtilis and sprays them onto a plant that is under abiotic stress. Four families of bench work stand between that sentence and a number, and each one hands its product to the next.

Molecular cloning builds the constructs. Parts are amplified, cut, assembled by Golden Gate and moved into E. coli for storage and screening, then into B. subtilis by electroporation, which is where the protein has to be made. Recombinant protein production gets the protectant out of the culture: the cells are grown up, the secreted fraction and the lysate are taken separately, and what comes out is quantified and identified on a gel and a membrane. The plant protocols grow the test organism, Arabidopsis thaliana, on plates, in soil and in hydroponics, and apply a measured salt or heat dose to it. The functional assays read out what the stress did, by chlorophyll and by the phenotype the plate photographs record.

The chain matters more than any one link. A chlorophyll number means nothing unless the plants that produced it were stressed identically, which is why the plate protocols are written down to the seed spacing and the camera platform. A blot means nothing unless the lane came from a culture whose construct is verified, which is why the cloning protocols are written down to the digest that checks the assembly.

The twenty-one protocols below are the ones with a written sheet. Where a protocol was written but no result on this wiki came out of it, its own entry says so, in the line marked Ran for.

Molecular cloning

Ten protocols, and they run as a loop. A part is amplified, assembled, transformed, screened by colony PCR, grown up overnight, miniprepped and then checked by digest on a gel. A failure at any of those steps sends the work back to the assembly reaction, and over the summer it did so often. The vectors are the pSTK set; which construct went into which backbone is on Parts.

Culture agar preparation

Pour the LB agar selection plates that every transformation, electroporation and colony PCR in this section is plated onto.

Bench time30 min, plus a 30 min autoclave and cooling
OutputRoughly ten 90 mm plates per 250 mL batch
Ran forEvery plate on this wiki

Materials

  • LB agar powder
  • ddH2O
  • Glycerol
  • 500 mL flask, stir bar, magnetic stirrer
  • Antibiotic stock, one of:
    • Ampicillin, for level 1 constructs in E. coli
    • Chloramphenicol, for level 0 and level 2 constructs
    • Erythromycin, for B. subtilis

Procedure

  1. Add 250 mL ddH2O, 9.25 g LB agar powder and 2 mL glycerol to a 500 mL flask.
  2. Add a stir bar and stir until no powder is left undissolved.
  3. Cover the flask with aluminium foil and stick an autoclave indicator on top.
  4. Autoclave for 30 min.
  5. Move the flask into the biosafety cabinet and let it cool to hand temperature. Stirring while it cools keeps it even.
  6. Add 250 µL of antibiotic stock, which is a 1:1,000 dilution into the batch, and mix gently without raising bubbles.
  7. Pour the plates and let them set flat in the cabinet.
Notes

Add the antibiotic too early and it is cooked; too late and the agar sets in the flask. Hand temperature on the outside of the glass is the usable signal.

To fix · E1

Two numbers in the agar sheet are wrong or unitless.

  • The sheet writes 9.25 LB agar powder with no unit. Grams is the only reading that works: 9.25 g in 250 mL is 37 g/L, the standard rate. Written as grams here.
  • The sheet's last step says distribute 250 µL of medium in plate. A 90 mm plate takes about 25 mL, and 250 µL of the 250 mL batch would fill a thousand plates. The step is left unquantified above until somebody who poured them confirms the volume.

DNA gel electrophoresis

Separate DNA by size so a PCR product, a colony PCR screen or a digest can be confirmed against a ladder.

Bench time25 min to cast and set, 30 min to run
OutputOne imaged gel
Ran forEvery gel figure on Engineering and Parts

Materials

  • Agarose powder
  • 1× TAE buffer
  • BioGreen Safe DNA gel dye, 6×
  • Gel mould, tray, comb
  • Plastic wrap, microwave
  • DNA ladder, 1 kb or 3 kb

Casting the gel

  1. Assemble the tray, comb and mould.
  2. Weigh agarose powder for the concentration the fragment sizes call for.
  3. Add 20 mL of 1× TAE to the flask and mix.
  4. Cover with plastic wrap and poke four or five holes in it with a 1000 µL tip.
  5. Microwave for 30 s, then swirl carefully.
  6. Repeat until the agarose is fully dissolved and the solution is clear.
  7. Remove the wrap and wait until it stops steaming.
  8. Add the safe stain at 1:20,000 and pipette to mix.
  9. Pour into the mould.
  10. Pop surface bubbles with the pointed end of a 1000 µL tip, and sweep bubbles off the floor of the tray with the flat end.
  11. Let it set at room temperature.

Loading and running

  1. Prepare samples. A PCR product needs no added dye. A miniprep needs 1 µL of 6× dye to every 5 µL of DNA, mixed on parafilm.
  2. Pour 1× TAE into the tank until it covers the gel.
  3. Seat the gel in the tank.
  4. Load 5 µL of ladder or sample per well. The ladder has to run past the largest fragment you expect.
  5. Run at 100 V for 30 min.
Notes

The sheet says the appropriate amount of agarose without naming a percentage, and the gels actually run were not logged by percentage either. Anyone repeating a specific figure should read the percentage off the notebook entry for that date rather than assuming one. Stain goes in after the flask stops steaming; added to boiling agarose it degrades.

The molar volume formula is garbled in the source. The sheet gives the vector and insert volumes as Vector: 0.02 = {(X sequence length(bp) × 650)/1000/ DNA concentration} and the same shape for the insert with 0.01. E. coli transformation

Get assembled plasmid into chemically competent DH5α, where it is replicated, stored and screened before it goes anywhere near B. subtilis.

Bench time1 h 45 min at the bench, then 16 h overnight
OutputOne selection plate of colonies
Ran forEvery cloning step on Parts

Materials

  • DH5α chemically competent cells
  • Plasmid or assembly reaction
  • SOC medium, made from LB with 0.5 M sorbitol and 0.38 M mannitol
  • LB selection plates: ampicillin for level 1, chloramphenicol for levels 0 and 2
  • Water bath at 42 °C, ice bath, spreader, Bunsen burner

Procedure

  1. Set the water bath to 42 °C and prepare an ice bath.
  2. Pre-warm the selection plates at 37 °C.
  3. Thaw competent cells on ice until half thawed, then add 10 µL of plasmid to 100 µL of cells.
  4. Incubate on ice for 20 min.
  5. Heat shock at 42 °C for 45 s.
  6. Return to ice for 3 min.
  7. Add 1 mL of SOC.
  8. Recover for 1 h at 37 °C.
  9. Centrifuge at 5,000 × g for 5 min.
  10. Remove about 1 mL of supernatant and resuspend the pellet in what is left.
  11. Spread on the plate.
  12. Incubate at 37 °C for 16 h.
Notes

The 45 s heat shock is the step that punishes inattention. Cells that sit at 42 °C for a minute give a visibly worse plate. Concentrating the recovery into the last 100 µL is what makes a low-efficiency assembly readable, and it costs nothing.

Electroporation into B. subtilis

Move a verified construct into the production organism, which does not take up plasmid by heat shock.

Bench timeCompetent cell prep 3 h; electroporation 30 min plus 3 h recovery
OutputSelection plate on erythromycin or chloramphenicol
Ran forStrain 168 only

Materials

  • B. subtilis 168 or WB800 overnight culture
  • Electroporation (EP) buffer, ice-cold; the WB800 version carries 0.5 mol/L trehalose
  • SOC recovery medium
  • 0.2 cm electroporation cuvettes, pre-chilled
  • Bio-Rad Gene Pulser Xcell [6]
  • LB plates with erythromycin or chloramphenicol

Making electrocompetent cells, strain 168

  1. Dilute the overnight culture 16-fold into growth medium.
  2. Shake at 37 °C, 200 rpm, until OD600 reaches 0.85 to 0.95.
  3. Chill on ice for 5 min.
  4. Centrifuge at 5,000 × g for 10 min.
  5. Discard the supernatant and wash the pellet twice with ice-cold EP buffer.
  6. Resuspend in one twentieth of the original culture volume of EP buffer.
  7. Aliquot into pre-chilled microcentrifuge tubes.
  8. Store at −80 °C.

Making electrocompetent cells, strain WB800

  1. Dilute the overnight culture 16-fold into growth medium.
  2. Shake at 37 °C, 200 rpm, until OD600 reaches 2.2.
  3. Chill on ice for 5 min.
  4. Centrifuge at 5,000 × g for 10 min.
  5. Wash the pellet four times with ice-cold EP buffer containing 0.5 mol/L trehalose.
  6. Resuspend in one fortieth of the original culture volume.
  7. Aliquot into pre-chilled tubes and store at −80 °C.

Electroporation

  1. Pre-warm SOC recovery medium at 37 °C and pre-chill the 0.2 cm cuvettes.
  2. Mix plasmid with 100 µL of electrocompetent cells in a 15 mL tube.
  3. Set the pulser to the parameters in the table below.
  4. Transfer the cell and DNA mixture to a cold cuvette and flick the suspension to the bottom.
  5. Place the cuvette in the shock pod, close the lid, and wait for the beep, about one to two seconds.
  6. Remove immediately and add 2 mL of SOC.
  7. Record the delivered pulse parameters from the instrument.
  8. Transfer to a culture tube and recover for 3 h at 37 °C, shaking at 200 rpm.
  9. Centrifuge at 5,000 × g for 5 min.
  10. Plate on selective LB and incubate at 37 °C overnight.

Liquid bacterial culture

Grow a verified colony overnight, which is the input to every miniprep and every protein extraction on this page.

Bench time10 min, then 16 h overnight
Output3 mL of saturated culture per tube
Ran forEvery miniprep and extraction

Materials

  • Plate of screened colonies
  • TB medium
  • Antibiotic stock: ampicillin at 25 mg/mL, chloramphenicol, or erythromycin
  • Culture tubes, Bunsen burner, shaking incubator

Procedure

  1. Label the culture tubes.
  2. Add 3 mL of TB medium and antibiotic at 1:1,000 to each tube.
  3. Touch a pipette tip to the chosen colony.
  4. Drop the tip into the tube.
  5. Incubate 16 h with the tube at a slant, which keeps the culture aerated.

DNA isolation

Pull plasmid out of an overnight culture, measure it, and hand it to a digest, a sequencing submission or the next assembly.

Bench time1 h including the spectrophotometer
Output50 µL of eluted plasmid with concentration and purity ratios
Ran forEvery sequencing submission on Parts

Materials

  • Overnight bacterial culture
  • Viogene Mini Plus Plasmid DNA Extraction Kit [3]: MX1, MX2, MX3, WN and WS buffers, columns, elution buffer
  • Microcentrifuge
  • BioDrop spectrophotometer

Procedure

  1. Transfer 1.8 mL of overnight culture into a 2 mL tube.
  2. Centrifuge at 16,000 × g for 1 min and discard the supernatant.
  3. Repeat until the whole culture has been pelleted into the same tube.
  4. Add 200 µL of MX1 and resuspend the pellet completely by pipetting or vortexing.
  5. Add 250 µL of MX2. Mix by inverting 8 to 10 times, never by vortexing.
  6. Stand at room temperature for 2 min.
  7. Add 350 µL of MX3 and invert immediately to mix.
  8. Centrifuge at 16,000 × g for 5 min. Label the columns and collection tubes while it spins.
  9. Transfer about 900 µL of supernatant to the column and spin at 7,000 × g for 30 s. Discard the flow-through.
  10. Add 500 µL of WN, spin at 7,000 × g for 30 s, discard the flow-through.
  11. Add 700 µL of WS, spin at 7,000 × g for 30 s, discard the flow-through.
  12. Spin the empty column at 10,000 × g for 3 min to drive off residual ethanol.
  13. Move the column into a clean 1.5 mL tube.
  14. Add 50 µL of elution buffer to the centre of the membrane and stand for 3 min.
  15. Centrifuge at 10,000 × g for 3 min.
  16. On the BioDrop, clean the port with ddH2O, then ethanol, then ddH2O.
  17. Blank on elution buffer.
  18. Read concentration, A260/230 and A260/280, and write all three on the tube and in the notebook.
  19. Clean the port again the same way.
Notes

The 3 min dry spin at step 12 is the step people skip, and carried ethanol is the commonest reason a clean-looking miniprep will not cut. The two purity ratios go on the tube, not only in the notebook: a sequencing reaction that comes back as noise is usually explained by a number that was already written down.

Recombinant protein production

Six protocols that take a verified B. subtilis strain and try to end up with an identified protein. The design is secretion: the cargo carries a SamyQ signal peptide so the protectant leaves the cell, which is why the supernatant and the pellet are harvested and analysed separately at both scales. The trial scale settles the lysis conditions; the large scale runs them at volume.

Bradford protein assay

Put a concentration on a protein sample against a BSA standard curve, so that a gel lane can be loaded to a known mass.

Bench time40 min including the 5 to 10 min development
OutputConcentration in µg/mL from a fitted line
Ran forNo result on this wiki

Materials

  • Bradford reagent, Coomassie Brilliant Blue G-250 based
  • BSA stock at 2 mg/mL
  • ddH2O
  • Protein samples
  • 96-well microplate and plate reader at 595 nm, or cuvettes and a spectrophotometer

Procedure

  1. Dilute the 2 mg/mL BSA stock with ddH2O into the six standards above.
  2. Dilute the protein samples with ddH2O so the expected concentration lands inside the 100 to 600 µg/mL range of the curve.
  3. Add 100 µL of each standard to its own well, and include one well of 100 µL ddH2O as the blank.
  4. Add 196 µL of Bradford reagent plus 4 µL of sample to each sample well. The blank gets Bradford reagent only.
  5. Mix gently without raising bubbles, which read as absorbance.
  6. Stand at room temperature for 5 to 10 min in the dark while the colour turns from brown to blue.
  7. Read at 595 nm, zeroed on the water-only well.
  8. Plot absorbance against known BSA concentration for the six standards and fit a straight line.
  9. Read the unknowns off the fitted line.
  10. Run standards and samples in duplicate or triplicate and average.
To fix · E5

The Bradford well volumes do not add up. Step 3 puts 100 µL of standard in a well. Step 4 then says to add 196 µL of reagent and 4 µL of sample to every well. Taken literally a standard well receives a sample too, and the well holds 300 µL, which is at or over the working volume of a flat-bottom 96-well plate. Either the standards are 4 µL into 196 µL of reagent like the samples, in which case the 100 µL table is a dilution series made in tubes first, or the sample wells are something other than 200 µL. Somebody who ran the plate should say which.

Trial scale protein extraction

Settle the lysis and clarification conditions on 5 to 50 mL before spending a 500 mL culture on them, and find out whether the cargo is in the medium or still inside the cell.

Bench time1 h 30 min, cold throughout
OutputClarified supernatant and a soluble lysate fraction
Ran forThe cultures behind the August blots

Materials

  • B. subtilis liquid culture, 5 to 50 mL
  • CelLytic B 10× concentrate
  • EDTA-free protease inhibitor
  • Centrifuge tubes sized to the culture, refrigerated centrifuge, vortex
  • Ice bucket or 4 °C access

Harvesting the supernatant

  1. Centrifuge at 4,000 to 8,000 × g for 10 to 20 min at 4 °C to separate pellet from supernatant.
  2. Keep both fractions.
  3. Add EDTA-free protease inhibitor to the supernatant immediately.
  4. Clarify again at 15,000 × g for 15 to 20 min at 4 °C.

Harvesting the cell pellet

  1. Prepare a 10-fold dilution of CelLytic B 10× into the chosen buffer.
  2. Grow a 10 mL culture under the normal induction conditions.
  3. Take 1.5 mL of culture at OD600 0.5 to 1.0 per dilution condition and spin at full speed for 2 min. One tube per CelLytic B dilution.
  4. Discard the medium and resuspend each pellet in 0.4 mL of its own CelLytic B dilution.
  5. Vortex briefly, then mix for 5 to 10 min at room temperature so the detergent can pull out soluble protein.
  6. Spin at full speed for 5 min to pellet insoluble material.
  7. Separate the supernatant, which is the soluble fraction, from the pellet, which is the insoluble or inclusion body fraction.
Notes

Protease inhibitor goes in as soon as the supernatant is off the pellet, and the sample stays cold from there to the freezer. The whole point of running both halves is that the answer to where is the protein is a gel and not an assumption, and on this project the answer changed the plan more than once.

  • DNase I, 20 mg/mL stock diluted 1:100 to a 200 µg/mL working stock, at 25 µL per 1 mL of lysis buffer

Harvesting the supernatant

  1. Pellet the induced culture at 4,000 to 8,000 × g for 10 to 20 min at 4 °C.
  2. Keep the supernatant and discard the pellet.
  3. Add EDTA-free protease inhibitor immediately.
  4. Clarify at 15,000 × g for 15 to 20 min at 4 °C.

Harvesting the cell pellet

  1. Harvest cells at 5,000 × g for 10 min and remove the spent medium.
  2. Prepare a 10-fold dilution of CelLytic B 10× into the chosen buffer.
  3. Add the diluted CelLytic B at 10 to 20 mL per gram of cell paste, pre-mixed with lysozyme and protease inhibitor at the ratios above, and resuspend fully. One gram of wet paste is about 250 mL of culture at OD600 2.0.
  4. Shake at room temperature for 10 to 15 min.
  5. Add DNase I working stock at the ratio above and mix briefly to cut the viscosity before spinning.

  7. Collect the supernatant as the soluble fraction.

Skip the DNase and the lysate becomes a string that will not pipette and will not pellet cleanly. No blot or purified protein on this wiki traces back to a large scale run.

Plant protocols

Three ways to grow Arabidopsis thaliana and put a measured dose of stress on it. Plates are the workhorse, because a vertical square plate lets root length be photographed every day without touching the plant. Soil is closer to a field and much harder to dose evenly. Hydroponics sits between the two and was built so that the medium, and therefore the salt, can be changed under a growing plant without disturbing it.

Every plate protocol on this page uses the same seed template, so plants from different experiments are spaced and imaged identically.

Arabidopsis in soil

Raise plants from sterilised seed to a treatable rosette in a 3-inch pot, and keep untreated plants going for seed.

Bench time10 days to a transplantable seedling, about 3 weeks more to treatment
OutputPotted plants, and seed from the untreated ones
Ran forThe soil planting progression record

Materials

  • 75% and 95% ethanol
  • ½ MS square plates
  • Bleach, for optional pot sanitisation
  • Peat soil, autoclaved
  • 3-inch pots and water trays, transparent lids
  • Hyponex No. 2 fertiliser (花宝2號)
  • Bamboo stakes, 200 µL tips, orchid clips
  • Sterile ddH2O

Sterilisation and germination

  1. Sterilise seed in 75% ethanol for 2 min, then add 95% ethanol and let the seed air-dry under sterile conditions.
  2. Sow the dry seed on square plates of ½ MS.
  3. Cold treat at 4 °C in the dark for 3 days.
  4. Move to white light and grow the plates vertically for 7 days.

Soil and pots

  1. Optionally sanitise pots and trays by soaking in 1:100 bleach for 10 min, then rinsing repeatedly until no bleach smell is left. Do this in a hood.
  2. Mix peat soil and autoclave it.
  3. Fill each 3-inch pot to about 80% full, pressing lightly, then water the surface until the whole soil body is wet.

Transplanting

  1. Make a hole about one knuckle deep in the centre of the soil with a finger. For more than one seedling per pot, space the holes evenly.
  2. Select one uniform, healthy, 7-day-old seedling and lift it with tweezers from beneath the cotyledons. Never clamp the seedling itself.
  3. Lay the root against the sidewall of the hole.
  4. Wash soil over the roots with a wash bottle so they are covered naturally. Do not touch the root system with tweezers or fingers.

Acclimatisation and care

  1. Stand the pots in a tray with about 0.5 cm of water, so the pot bottoms are submerged.
  2. Cover with a transparent lid and step the humidity down over three days: day 1 fully sealed, day 2 slightly open, day 3 uncovered for air circulation.
  3. Water on a dry and wet cycle set by the chamber humidity. The surface should dry, but never for more than a day.
  4. Alternate water and fertiliser with each watering. Dilute Hyponex No. 2 at 1:1,000.
  5. Water and fertilise from the tray. Water the surface only when the soil is too dry to take up water from below.

Treatment, support and seed

  1. Apply protectant or stress treatment at about 3 weeks after transplanting. Two to four weeks is workable depending on growth, and treatment should start no later than four weeks to stay ahead of bolting and growth arrest.
  2. Photograph daily to find the right starting point.
  3. Once the inflorescence reaches 20 cm, push a bamboo stake into the soil. Cap the sharp end with a 200 µL tip first so it does not sit against wet soil and grow mould.
  4. Clip the inflorescence to the stake with orchid clips to stop it falling over, without damaging the stem.
  5. For untreated plants kept for seed: siliques start drying about 1.5 months after transplanting, so cut back the watering then.
  6. When 80% of the siliques are dry, stop watering entirely and let the plant dry fully before collecting seed.

Functional assays

Two protocols that turn a treated plant into a number. Chlorophyll extraction is the destructive endpoint for every plate run on this page. The heat stress test is a second stress axis, used with a commercial peptide to ask whether the timing of a spray matters at all before asking whether our own protectants work.

Chlorophyll extraction

Convert a harvested plate of seedlings into total chlorophyll, which is the readout most sensitive to salt and heat damage in this system.

Bench time1 h 30 min including freezing and extraction
OutputTotal chlorophyll a plus b, in mg per g fresh weight
Ran forAgar sets 4 to 8 and both hydroponic runs

Materials

  • Agar plates with treated plants
  • Tweezers, tissue paper, weighing plate
  • 15 mL centrifuge tubes, marker
  • Stainless steel beads, ten per tube
  • Acetone, diluted to 80%
  • Vortex
  • 96-well plate and an ELISA plate reader

Procedure

  1. Lift every plant off the plate at once with tweezers, in a single motion, holding below the leaf area and without squeezing.
  2. Lay the plants on tissue paper and press lightly to take off the agar. Do not press the leaves hard.
  3. Put the plants on a weighing plate and record the fresh weight, one plate of plants at a time.
  4. Move them into a 15 mL centrifuge tube, roots included.
  5. Label the tube with the group concentration and its fresh weight.
  6. Repeat for every condition.
  7. Add ten stainless steel beads to each tube and hold at −80 °C for 10 min.
  8. Dilute acetone to 80% and add 4 mL to each tube.
  9. Let the chlorophyll extract, vortexing or shaking hard to break the tissue up. It is done when the plants are no longer green.
  10. Write down which sample goes in which well before loading anything. This is the step that loses runs.
  11. Load 200 µL per well, three technical replicates per sample.
  12. Blank on 200 µL of 80% acetone.
  13. Read the plate at 663 nm and 645 nm.

Calculation

Total chlorophyll, in mg per g fresh weight, from Arnon's coefficients for 80% acetone [1]:

Chl a+b = (8.02 × A663 + 20.20 × A645) × V / (1000 × fresh weight)

where A663 and A645 are the blank-subtracted absorbances, V is the extraction volume in mL and fresh weight is in g.

Notes

Heat stress on agar

Ask whether spraying a protectant before, during or after a heat event changes the outcome, using a commercial peptide as the test article.

Bench time17 days: 4 vernalisation, 7 germination, 6 observation
OutputDaily plate photographs, fresh weight, chlorophyll
Ran for15 August 2026 and the 13 August horizontal-plate trial

Materials

  • ½ MS medium powder, MES buffer, sucrose, agar powder, distilled water
  • CH Biotech peptide (富肽二號)
  • Sterile ddH2O, 50 mL centrifuge tube, hand sprayer
  • 500 mL flask, stir bar, pH meter, aluminium foil
  • Square petri dishes, breathable tape, tweezers
  • Incubator at 37 °C under white light, rack, thermometer

Vernalisation plates, 200 mL for 4 plates

IngredientAmount
½ MS medium powder0.46 g
MES, 0.5 g/L0.1 g
Distilled water200 mL
Sucrose, 1%2.0 g
Agar powder, 1%2.0 g
  1. Combine in a 500 mL flask and stir on a magnetic stirrer until nothing is left undissolved.
  2. Bring the pH to 5.7 to 5.8 with KOH.
  3. Foil the neck, add indicator tape, autoclave at 127 °C for 20 min, and wait for zero pressure.
  4. Label the dishes during the autoclave: date and an up arrow in the top right.
  5. Take half the flasks into the cabinet and hold the rest in a hot bath.
  6. Dry each flask with a paper towel before spraying it with 70% ethanol.
  7. Pour about 50 mL per dish, then bring in and pour the second half.
  8. Let them set flat in the cabinet.

Sowing and germination

  1. Sow 18 seeds per plate in two rows of nine, using the template in Figure 20.
  2. Seal with breathable tape and label the vernalisation date.
  3. Wrap the plates in foil and vernalise at 4 °C for 4 days.
  4. Move to the growth chamber for 7 days to germinate.

Transferring seedlings

  1. Spray the plates with alcohol before they go into the cabinet.
  2. Rule a line 5.5 cm from the top of the plate using the template.
  3. Stand the plates vertically at a slight angle.
  4. Pick 12 germinated seedlings of similar root length and line their root tips up on the ruled line. Four plates, one per group: control, pre-treatment one day before, co-treatment on day 0, and after-treatment one day after.
  5. Seal with breathable tape, label the transfer date, and rack the plates in the growth chamber, tilted slightly back.

Applying treatments

  1. One day before the heat event, dilute the CH Biotech peptide 1:500 in a 50 mL tube: 19.96 mL of sterile ddH2O and 40 µL of peptide.
  2. Fit a sprayer to the tube.
  3. Take out the pre-treatment plate, stand it vertically, and spray the plants from leaves to roots from 15 cm away, about two sprays, so every plant is wetted.
  4. Reseal with breathable tape and return it to the growth chamber.
  5. On day 0, spray the co-treatment plate the same way from the same distance.
  6. Reseal it.
  7. Rack all four plates, tilted slightly, and put the whole rack into the incubator at 37 °C under white light for thermopriming.
  8. Leave the seedlings in the incubator for another 5 days.
  9. One day after thermopriming, take out the after-treatment plate, spray it the same way, reseal, and put it back in the incubator.

Recording

  1. Observe for 6 days from pre-treatment.
  2. Set up the black-background platform under white light.
  3. Bring the plates out one at a time and photograph them, keeping labels legible and avoiding reflections and droplets.
  4. Return each plate to the chamber, tilted slightly back.
  5. After 6 days, harvest and take fresh weight and chlorophyll.
Notes
  • The test article is a commercial product, not a ReLeaf protectant. The experiment asks whether timing matters at all for a foliar spray under heat, which is a question worth answering with material that definitely works before spending our own on it. LEA14 and BoPep4 have never been applied to a plant in any experiment on this project.
  • The dilution is written as 19.96 mL plus 40 µL, which is 1:500 in a round 20 mL. Make it in the tube you will spray from.
  • Spray distance and count are part of the dose. Two sprays from 15 cm is what was run; changing either changes the treatment.
  • This run exists in the archive as five slides of photographs, 16 to 21 August. Its only numbers are in the chlorophyll sheet named below, which carries both the problems in fixes E12 and E15.
