Experiments

Abstract

Experiments collects the protocol sheets behind the wet lab work, grouped into four stages, each sheet with its materials, its procedure and what it was run for.

The sheets follow four stages in the order one hands its product to the next: ten for cloning, six for protein production, five plant assays and two for the bioreactor. Each one names what it was run for, and all but one carry the full procedure as a downloadable PDF. Four produced no result on this wiki: the Bradford assay, large scale extraction, purification and dialysis, and the silica calibration. Electroporation ran on strain 168 only, although the sheet also carries a WB800 method.

Making electrocompetent cells for B. subtilis 168

  1. Dilute the overnight bacteria culture to 16-fold in growth medium
  2. Incubate the diluted culture at 37 °C with shaking at 200 rpm until the OD₆₀₀ reaches 0.85 to 0.95.
  3. Cool the culture on ice for 5 min.
  4. Centrifuge the culture at 5,000 × g for 10 min

  8. Store the electrocompetent cells at −80 °C until use.

  18. Add 700 μL WS buffer and centrifuge at 7,000 x g for 30 seconds. Discard flow-through.

Procedure

  1. Prepare the BSA standard curve by diluting 2 mg/mL BSA stock with ddH₂O into six tubes/wells, per the table below (100 µL total each):
    µL600 μg/mL500 μg/mL400 μg/mL300 μg/mL200 μg/mL100 μg/mL
    BSA30252015105
    ddH2O707580859095
  2. Dilute your protein sample(s) with ddH₂O if needed, so the expected concentration falls within the 100 to 600 µg/mL range of your standard curve.
  3. Add 100 µL of each standard into separate wells of the microplate. Include one well with 100 µL ddH₂O only as a blank.
  4. Add 196 µL of Bradford reagent + 4 µL of samples to every well, only add Bradford reagent to blank. Mix gently (e.g., by pipetting up and down or gentle shaking); avoid making bubbles, since they interfere with the absorbance reading.
  5. Incubate at room temperature for 5 to 10 minutes, protected from light. Color development (brown to blue) happens during this window.
  6. Measure absorbance at 595 nm using a microplate reader, zeroing/blanking against the ddH₂O-only well.
  7. Plot the standard curve: absorbance (y-axis) vs. known BSA concentration (x-axis) for your six standards, and fit a linear trendline.
  8. Use the trendline equation to calculate your unknown sample concentrations from their measured absorbance values.
  9. Run standards and samples in duplicate or triplicate if possible, and average the results for better accuracy.

General formula

Total volume needed = (number of wells) × (volume per well) × 1.1. The ×1.1 (10% extra) accounts for pipetting loss, dead volume in tubes, and reagent that sticks to pipette tips.

  1. Bradford reagent. Volume needed = (total number of wells: standards + blank + samples) × 100 µL × 1.1. Example: 6 standards + 1 blank + 3 samples = 10 wells → 10 × 100 µL × 1.1 = 1,100 µL (1.1 mL) of Bradford reagent
  2. BSA stock (2 mg/mL) for the standard curve. This is fixed regardless of how many samples you run, since it only depends on your 6-point standard curve: volume needed = (30 + 25 + 20 + 15 + 10 + 5) µL × 1.1 = 105 × 1.1 = ~116 µL, round up to ~120 µL of 2 mg/mL BSA stock. If running standards in duplicate or triplicate, multiply by 2 or 3 first, then apply the ×1.1.
  3. ddH₂O. Two separate uses, add them together: for standard dilutions, (70 + 75 + 80 + 85 + 90 + 95) µL × 1.1 = 495 × 1.1 = ~545 µL; for the blank well(s), 100 µL × (number of blank wells) × 1.1; for diluting samples (if needed), depends on your sample's starting concentration: use C₁V₁ = C₂V₂ to figure out how much water dilutes your sample into the 100 to 600 µg/mL range, then add that volume too.
  4. Protein sample. Volume needed = 100 µL × (number of replicate wells for that sample) × 1.1. If you're testing 3 samples in triplicate: 100 µL × 3 replicates × 3 samples × 1.1 = ~990 µL total sample volume needed (split across your 3 actual samples, so ~330 µL each before replicating).

1. Purpose

This document provides a standard operating procedure for building a “particle concentration vs. OD600” regression curve by cumulative titration of a known-concentration monodisperse silica (SiO2) microsphere suspension. The goal is to simultaneously calibrate two inline photometers installed in a recirculating loop, using OD600 values measured on a benchtop spectrophotometer (standard 1 cm pathlength) as an external reference anchor, so that the resulting conversion factors can be applied to real-time optical monitoring in a high-cell-density perfusion bioreactor (HFM) system.

3. Principle

For a solid sphere of diameter 1 µm and density 2.3 g/cm³, one particle weighs about 1.2 pg, so the 25 mg/mL stock holds about 2.08×10¹⁰ particles/mL. The van de Hulst anomalous diffraction approximation gives an extinction efficiency Qext ≈ 0.81 at 600 nm, a theoretical estimate of 1 mg/mL SiO2 ≈ OD600 2.3 (1 cm pathlength, single scattering). As OD approaches or exceeds 1.0, multiple scattering makes the measured value fall below the linear extrapolation.

Each cycle adds 1 mL of working stock, mixes, and withdraws a 1 mL sample, keeping the system volume constant at V = 50 mL:

\[ C_n = \frac{V\,C_{n-1} + C_{\text{stock}}}{V + 1}, \qquad C_n = C_{\text{stock}}\left[1 - \left(\tfrac{V}{V+1}\right)^{n}\right] \]

With V = 50 and Cstock = 2.5 mg/mL: cycle 30, C ≈ 1.10 mg/mL (theoretical OD600 ≈ 2.5); cycle 45, 1.48 mg/mL (≈ 3.4); cycle 60, 1.74 mg/mL (≈ 4.0).

4. Safety precautions

  • Wear gloves and eye protection when handling the SiO2 suspension to avoid splash contact with eyes and skin.
  • If weighing out raw powder is ever required (outside the scope of this protocol, which uses only the liquid stock), work inside a fume hood to avoid inhaling dust.
  • Before starting the peristaltic pump, confirm all tubing connections are securely fastened to prevent leaks or spray during operation.
  • Follow the manufacturer's electrical safety guidelines when operating the spectrophotometer and inline photometers.

5. Reagent preparation

Combine 6 mL of the original stock (25 mg/mL) with 54 mL of DI water and mix thoroughly to obtain 60 mL of SiO2 working stock at 2.5 mg/mL. This uses 6 mL (150 mg) of the 10 mL original stock, leaving 4 mL (100 mg) in reserve, and supports approximately 60 cycles. Vortex or shake both the original stock and the working stock thoroughly before use, as the microspheres will slowly settle on standing.

6. Hardware assembly and system initialization

  1. Assemble the recirculating loop. Connect, in order: 100 mL beaker → silicone tubing → peristaltic pump → inline photometer 1 → inline photometer 2 → back to the beaker. Place a magnetic stir bar in the beaker on a stir plate.
  2. Establish the blank on both inline photometers. Add 50 mL of DI water to the beaker and start the pump (~60 mL/min) to fill the entire loop and both photometers, confirming no residual air bubbles remain. Allow 2 to 3 full loop turnovers (about 2 to 3 minutes) until the signal stabilizes, then record the blank readings from inline photometer 1 and 2.
  3. Establish the blank on the benchtop spectrophotometer. Withdraw 1 mL of DI water from the beaker and measure it as the blank, then replace it with 1 mL of fresh DI water so the beaker returns to 50 mL.

7. Experimental procedure

  1. (Optional) Preliminary mini-scan. Cumulatively add 2, 5, 10 and 20 mg of SiO2 (80, 200, 400 and 800 µL of working stock), recording the inline photometer reading after each addition, to confirm the measured slope is on the same order as the theoretical ~2.3 OD·mL/mg.
  2. Each cycle. With the pump running, pipette 1 mL of working stock into the beaker. Keep stirring and pumping for 2 to 3 loop turnovers. Record inline photometers 1 and 2 simultaneously. Withdraw a 1 mL sample, mix briefly, and immediately measure OD600 on the benchtop spectrophotometer. The beaker is back at 50 mL and the next cycle can begin.
  3. Repeat for a recommended total of 45 to 60 cycles. OD600 should approach 2.5 around cycle 30; the remaining cycles show the saturation behavior. The reserve 4 mL of stock can be diluted on the spot to continue the run.

9. Quality control and troubleshooting

  • Keep the beaker stirring the whole time and measure withdrawn samples promptly, so the microspheres do not settle.
  • If the two inline photometers diverge persistently, flag those points (possible sedimentation in the tubing or a positional effect) rather than discarding them.
  • Microbubbles cause strong scattering artifacts, so confirm the loop is fully degassed at startup.
  • Use only the points in the linear range for the formal regression, and flag the non-linear, high-concentration points as the instrument's effective upper limit.
  • Re-run this protocol if the microsphere batch or the instrument hardware changes.

10. Recommended data analysis

Plot the theoretical concentration Cn against inline photometer 1, inline photometer 2 and benchtop OD600; regress each within its linear range; use the benchtop regression as the external reference to build an “inline reading → standard OD600” conversion; record R² and residual standard deviation; and keep the raw readings for every cycle. The data recording template and the full derivation are in the PDF.
