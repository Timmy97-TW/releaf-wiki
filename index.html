01The threat

The weather swings hardest where Taiwan farms most.

Since 1991 the island has warmed 0.29 °C per decade, nearly triple its earlier rate. Rain falls on fewer days and close to 30 % harder.

  1. 1

    Western plain

    Volatility is highest from Taichung to Tainan, where most of the island’s farmland and rice are.

  2. 2

    Alishan

    Sunshine in the high-stress months rose from 152.5 to 196.7 hours a month between 1991 and 2021. The high-mountain tea and vegetables grown there have nowhere cooler to move.

  3. 3

    Kaohsiung

    July nights average 26.7 °C, inside the 25 to 33 °C band that damages rice at grain-setting. The field still looks healthy at dawn.

Most of the farmers we met had lost crops to environmental stress.

So an answer has to be

  • Protective It has to protect the crop against the heat and drought stress this weather brings.

The damage starts before anyone can see it.

One spell of heat and drought on one farm, day by day.

The forecast is the only warning that comes before the damage. The first visible sign comes days after the damage starts, and the next check can be weeks away. The farmer can only judge, mix and spray after seeing the problem.

So an answer has to be

  • On demand The protection has to be in the field when the forecast turns, before day 0.
  • Automatic It cannot wait for someone to notice, mix and spray.

Each tool covers part of what a crop needs.

Farms already have good tools, each good at what it was built for. A crop under heat and drought stress needs five things at once, and each tool covers some of them.

  • Ready-made protectants

    Chemical or biological products bought at a shop. Some are proteins, made in bacteria that are killed before the protein is sold. They protect the crop, but someone has to notice the stress, buy the product, mix it and spray it.

    Covers Protective, Cell-free and Monitored.

  • Live microbial inoculants

    Living bacteria or fungi, applied to seed or soil. Taiwan’s microbial fertilisers exclude genetically modified microbes, so an engineered strain cannot be used this way.

    Covers Protective, On demand and Monitored.

  • On-farm biofactories

    Farms multiply microbes in their own tanks and spray the fermented broth. The broth is a live culture, with no required quality check.

    Covers Protective, On demand and Automatic.

  • Alert and dosing platforms

    Soil sensors and weather forecasts that open automated valves. They open a valve on time, but they dose the fertiliser or pesticide someone bought and loaded.

    Covers On demand, Automatic, Cell-free and Monitored.

An answer has to meet five demands.

  • Protective
  • On demand
  • Automatic
  • Cell-free
  • Monitored

The voices

We didn’t want to design ReLEAF in isolation.

So we listened to the people who would shape whether it could work in the real world.

  1. The farmer

    Grounded us in real agricultural needs.

    → A dose that leaves the crop its own resilience.

  2. The experts

    Helped us refine the science and engineering.

    → Containment first, and a replaceable bacterial capsule.

  3. The protectant manufacturer

    Showed us the barriers between production and access.

    → Root, leaf and drone delivery compared, with cost per area.

  4. The forum

    Challenged us to explain, question, and rethink our design.

    → Two open questions: organic certification, and reactors per farm.
