The weather swings hardest where Taiwan farms most.

  1. Tamsui, the north coast

    Farmer Chen farms in Tamsui, on Taiwan’s north coast. Her field is one of the island’s many, and the weather over all of them is changing.

  2. Taiwan

    0.29 °Cof warming per decade since 1991

    That is nearly triple the island’s earlier rate, and 2024 was its hottest year on record. Our climate volatility index measures how far a place swings within a year: day against night, summer against winter, and how unevenly its rain falls.

    99.2%of Taiwan’s farms are under 2 hectares

    Together they work 85.3% of its farmland. They cluster along the western plain and in the south, where the volatility index is highest.

  4. Pingtung, the far south

    ~10%of a rice yield lost for each degree of night warming

    Warm nights keep a rice plant burning the sugar it made during the day. Pingtung’s farmland is in the warmest night band on the island. Across southern Taiwan, a heat spike in the flowering season that was once rare is now routine.

How ReLEAF works

From a reading in the field to a protectant for the crop, in one machine.

  1. A leaf sensor reads leaf temperature and wetness. With the weather forecast, our model turns them into a stress index.
  2. The index sets how bright the green lamp should be. Green light (520 nm) switches on the CcaSR circuit in the engineered B. subtilis.
  3. The cells grow in a stirred 300 mL culture and secrete ACC deaminase into the medium. An in-line photometer reads their density.
  4. The culture runs round a loop through a hollow-fibre membrane. The cells stay in the loop; the protectant passes through.
  5. The protectant leaves in the permeate, to join hydroponic circulation or soil irrigation.
  6. Red light (660 nm) switches the circuit off again.

Green light switches on the CcaSR circuit in B. subtilis, and the cells secrete the protectant
