01The threat

The weather swings hardest where Taiwan farms most.

Since 1991 the island has warmed 0.29 °C per decade, nearly triple its earlier rate.

Where the weather swings hardest

  1. 1

    Tainan

    Tainan’s plain sits in the top band of an index that runs from 0.38 to 0.93.

  2. 2

    Kaohsiung

    A three-hour drive from Kaohsiung to Jade Mountain crosses about 19 °C.

  3. 3

    Taipei

    In 2024, the hottest year on record, Taipei logged 63 days above 35 °C.

Where Taiwan farms

Most of the farmers we met had lost crops to environmental stress.

So an answer has to be

  • Protective It has to protect the crop against abiotic or biotic stress this weather brings.

The damage starts before anyone can see it.

The forecast is the only warning that comes before the damage. By the time anyone can see the problem, the damage is days old.

So an answer has to be

  • On demand The protection has to be in the field when the forecast turns, before day 0.
  • Automatic It cannot wait for someone to notice, mix and spray.

It has to be safe to use, and checked while it is made.

A protectant grown by engineered bacteria raises two more questions. Taiwan’s microbial fertilisers must be free of genetically modified microbes, farmers at our forum asked whether a treated crop could still be certified organic, and nobody on a farm can see inside a culture.

So an answer has to be

  • Biosafe No living engineered cell can reach the field, only the protectant it makes.
  • Monitored The product has to be checked while it is made, with no sample sent to a lab.

Each tool covers part of what a crop needs.

Farms already have good tools, each built for its own job. A crop under heat and drought needs all five things at once, and each tool covers some of them.

  • Ready-made protectants

    Chemical and biological products bought at a shop, some of them proteins made in bacteria that are killed before sale. They protect once someone has seen the risk, bought the product, mixed it and sprayed it.

    • Protective
    • On demand
    • Automatic
    • Biosafe
    • Monitored

    Covers Protective, Biosafe and Monitored.

  • Live microbial inoculants

    Living bacteria or fungi put on the seed or into the soil at planting, working from then on. Taiwan’s microbial fertilisers must be free of genetically modified microbes, which closes this route to an engineered strain.

    • Protective
    • On demand
    • Automatic
    • Biosafe
    • Monitored

    Covers Protective and Monitored.

  • On-farm biofactories

    Farms grow microbes in their own tanks and spray the broth when they need it. The tank is tended by hand, and the broth is a live culture whose quality rules are still being written.

    • Protective
    • On demand
    • Automatic
    • Biosafe
    • Monitored

    Covers Protective and On demand.

  • Alert and dosing platforms

    Soil sensors and forecasts that open a valve at the right hour, with nobody there. What the valve lets out is still the product someone bought and loaded.

    • Protective
    • On demand
    • Automatic
    • Biosafe
    • Monitored

    Covers On demand, Automatic, Biosafe and Monitored.

Stress-tolerant seed protects from the day it is planted, at a level fixed that day, and cannot be raised the week a heatwave is forecast.

Each covers part of it. None covers all five.

An answer has to meet five demands.

  • Protective
  • On demand
  • Automatic
  • Biosafe
  • Monitored

The voices

We didn’t want to design ReLeaf in isolation.

So we listened to the people who would shape whether it could work in the real world.

  1. The farmer

    Grounded us in real agricultural needs.

    → A dose that leaves the crop its own resilience.

  2. The experts

    Helped us refine the science and engineering.

    → Containment first, and a replaceable bacterial capsule.

  3. The protectant manufacturer

    Showed us the barriers between production and access.

    → Root, leaf and drone delivery compared, with cost per area.

  4. The forum

    Challenged us to explain, question, and rethink our design.

    → Two open questions: organic certification, and reactors per farm.
