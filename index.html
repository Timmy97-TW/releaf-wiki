The weather swings hardest where Taiwan farms most.

Since 1991 the island has warmed 0.29 °C per decade, nearly triple its earlier rate. Our climate volatility index measures how far a place swings within a year: day against night, summer against winter, and how unevenly its rain falls.

Climate volatility index

Taiwan’s agricultural regions are increasingly exposed to more extreme and less predictable climate conditions.

Small-scale farms

With 99% of farms classified as small‑scale, much of Taiwan’s farmland is managed by farmers with limited capacity to absorb repeated losses.

Convergence

Where small farms and climate stress overlap, the same farmers face the greatest risk of losing a season’s work.

The damage starts before anyone can see it.

Days of warning only help if the coming stress is predicted early and precisely.

Researchers are finding new protectants, but the cells that make them must stay out of the field.

Protectants are proteins and peptides that help a crop withstand heat, drought and salinity.

The microbes engineered to make them cannot go with them. Taiwan’s organic standards prohibit genetically modified preparations, and a living culture has to be watched as it grows, because what it makes cannot be seen from outside.

Today’s tools were made for a steadier climate.

What a farmer can buy was developed against the conditions of years ago, while each season arrives hotter, drier or less predictable than the last.
