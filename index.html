02The gap

Each route in use today gives up speed, containment or quality.

A microbial protectant can be bought from a central plant, applied as living cells, or multiplied on the farm. Brazil has taken the third route furthest: since Law 15.070/2024, farms may produce bioinputs for their own use without product registration.7

  1. Central biomanufacturing

    Industrial fermentation, then purification, formulation, registration and distribution.

    • SpeedRegistration alone takes 1–3 years in Brazil, 2–3 in the US and up to 7–9 in the EU.4
    • ContainmentEngineered cells stay inside a licensed facility.2
    • QualityRegistered, batch-tested product.

  2. Live microbial inoculants

    The living cells are the product, applied to seed or soil. Made and registered centrally.

    • SpeedSame registration and supply chain as a central product.4
    • ContainmentAn engineered strain in this route enters the environment by design.3
    • QualityRegistered strain, checked before sale.

  3. On-farm biofactories Brazil

    Starter cultures multiplied beside the field, in units down to a shipping container that plugs into a farm socket.6

    • SpeedProduced on site, for the farm’s own use, without product registration.7
    • ContainmentFarm hygiene, not contained use. An engineered strain here would be released.3
    • QualityAll 18 farm-made inoculants sampled carried non-target microbes; 44% of bacterial isolates were potential human pathogens.5

03The distance

A commercial biological can take a decade to reach a farmer.

Between 34% and 46% of flash droughts reach drought within a single five-day window.8 Pivot Bio’s nitrogen-fixing microbes grew from university research begun around 2008, the company was founded in 2011, and its first product reached farms in 2019.9 In the EU, registration alone can take 7–9 years,4 and only four groups of organisms are admitted as microbial biostimulants.10

Synthetic biology

A cell that reads a colour and answers with a protein

The controller decides when the crop is in trouble. The cell has to be told, and light is the only signal that crosses into a sealed vessel without opening it. Four modules carry that signal from the wall of the reactor to a secreted protectant.

Module 01PCB synthesis

ho1 and pcyA turn the cell’s own heme into phycocyanobilin. Without that pigment the light sensor is blind, and B. subtilis does not make it on its own.

Module 02Light sensing

ccaS sits in the membrane holding the pigment. Green light flips it and it autophosphorylates. Red light flips it back, which is what makes the switch a dial rather than a fuse.

Module 03Transcriptional output

ccaS hands the phosphate to ccaR. Phosphorylated ccaR dimerises and binds the output promoter, so the state of one protein becomes the state of one gene.

Module 04Output

The protectant is transcribed, translated and secreted into the medium, where the membrane is waiting to let it out and keep the cell in.

Green, 520 nm

The controller has seen a stress condition coming. Transcription on. Design intent: no forecast model is trained yet.

Red, 660 nm

Nothing to answer. Transcription off, and the culture goes back to growing.

Cloning is modularised, so any gene B. subtilis can express drops into the output position without redesigning the circuit around it, including protectants we have not designed yet. How we chose the first one →

Product

What a farm would actually receive

One housing, three channels, one controller: the packaged arrangement the single-channel prototype is a step toward. Five properties decide its shape, and each one has a page behind it that says what is measured and what is still design intent.

  1. On-site

    Made beside the crop, so nothing is shipped, stored or held as inventory between the fermenter and the field.

  2. On-demand

    A run starts when the stress forecast calls for one, so quiet weeks cost nothing and nothing is made ahead to be thrown away.

  3. Precision agriculture

    Brightness sets the expression rate, so the dose follows the week the crop is having. Design intent: no induction curve has been measured yet.

  4. Biosecurity

    Engineered B. subtilis stays on its side of a 0.2 µm membrane. Shell-side medium plated on LB agar grew no colonies, in a single plating.

  5. Open-source

    Every drawing, model and part list is published under CC BY 4.0, so a farm, a school or another team can build one.

How we see farmers using ReLeaf

High-value protectants within every farmer’s reach

We see ReLeaf as more than a bioreactor. The goal is to give farmers more control over how and when they protect their crops, by moving biological production closer to them instead of asking them to wait for it to arrive and worry about storage.

Every farm looks different. Responsive protection should meet farmers where they already grow.
