01The threat

The weather swings hardest where Taiwan farms most.

Since 1991 the island has warmed 0.29 °C per decade, nearly triple its earlier rate. Rain falls on fewer days and close to 30 % harder.

  1. 1

    Western plain

    Volatility is highest from Taichung to Tainan, where most of the island’s farmland and rice are.

  2. 2

    Alishan

    Sunshine in the high-stress months rose from 152.5 to 196.7 hours a month between 1991 and 2021. The high-mountain tea and vegetables grown there have nowhere cooler to move.

  3. 3

    Kaohsiung

    July nights average 26.7 °C, inside the 25–33 °C band that damages rice at grain-setting. The field still looks healthy at dawn.

Most of the farmers we met had lost crops to environmental stress.

So an answer has to be

  • Protective It has to protect the crop against the heat and drought stress this weather brings.

02The hour

The damage starts before anyone can see it.

One spell of heat and drought on one farm, day by day.

So an answer has to be

  • On demand The protection has to be in the field when the forecast turns, before day 0.
  • Automatic It cannot wait for someone to notice, mix and spray.

03The gap

Every option on the market misses at least one.

Four kinds of product reach a field today. Each one leaves something out.

What farms use todayWhat goes wrongHow ReLeaf answers it

Ready-made protectants

Chemical or biological products bought at a shop. Some are proteins, made in bacteria that are killed before the protein is sold.2

  • On demand
  • Automatic

Someone has to notice the stress, buy the product, mix it and spray it. By then the damage has started.

The forecast switches production on, and the protectant goes out in the irrigation water without anyone there.

Live microbial inoculants

Living bacteria or fungi, made in a factory and applied to seed or soil. They work only if the cells colonise the roots.

  • Cell-free
  • Automatic

The product is alive and is released into the field. Taiwan’s microbial fertilisers exclude genetically modified microbes,3 so an engineered strain cannot be used this way.

The engineered cells stay behind a 0.2 µm hollow fibre. Only the protein they secrete leaves the reactor.

On-farm biofactories

Farms in Brazil multiply microbes in their own tanks and spray the fermented broth, without registration since 2024.4

  • Cell-free
  • Monitored

The broth is a live culture, often grown in non-sterile containers,5 with no required quality check.6 All 18 sampled batches carried other microbes, some of them potential human pathogens.7

The culture runs in a sealed loop that an in-line photometer reads while it runs. Only the protein leaves.

Alert and dosing platforms

Soil sensors and weather forecasts that open automated valves.

  • Protective

They open a valve on time, but they dose the fertiliser or pesticide someone bought and loaded. They make nothing.

The same forecast starts production, so what goes through the valve is the protectant itself.

The competitors and what each one leaves to the farmer are on our Entrepreneurship page.

Two misses that are demands in their own right

  • Cell-free Nothing living reaches the field. The crop gets the protein.
  • Monitored What is made is measured while it is made.
