This page holds all twenty-three cycles, across the genetic circuit, the protectant, the bioreactor, the photometer, the light plate apparatus and the plant assays, with what we designed, what we built, what the test said and what it changed.

The one cycle that has not run

Nothing anywhere in our records shows PcpcG2 output changing with green light. Six cloning cycles put four modules into one plasmid and none of them tested whether the circuit does what it was designed to do. Every sentence on this page about the switch is a statement about the design, and it is written as one.

The circuit we had to build

ReLeaf senses stress in a plant, drought or salinity, and turns that signal into a pulse of green light aimed at a Bacillus subtilis culture in a bioreactor. The bacteria carry an engineered CcaS/CcaR circuit that answers green light by making and secreting a protectant, which goes back to the stressed plant. Making the protectant on demand instead of continuously conserves the culture and keeps the reactor from overgrowing while nothing is wrong with the crop.

The design follows the CcaS/CcaR system that Castillo-Hair and colleagues ported into B. subtilis [1] and that Haller, Castillo-Hair and Tabor later wrote up as a protocol [2], split into four transcription units so each part of the signal path can be built, screened and swapped on its own. CcaS is a photoreceptor and it cannot see anything without a chromophore. In its ground state it absorbs green light near 535 nm and has low autokinase activity; a green photon switches it to a red-absorbing state near 672 nm with high autokinase activity, in which it phosphorylates itself and passes the phosphoryl group to CcaR. Phosphorylated CcaR drives transcription from PcpcG2. Red light returns CcaS to the ground state and transcription stops.

Show details: the kit, the primer tail and the assembly levels Why the build moved into the SubtiToolKit, and the four-element tail every part we added carries.

The circuit was first designed to the MoClo 2.0 standard [3] as whole-gene synthesis. That attempt produced a screen our own records describe three different ways (see cycle 1), and what every version agrees on is that Golden Gate efficiency stayed low. A professor supplied the SubtiToolKit [4], a Golden Gate kit written for B. subtilis and other Gram-positive bacteria, and every subsequent cycle is inside that kit. The MoClo constructs did not go to waste: they became the templates the STK-compatible parts were amplified off.

The kit ships promoters, ribosome binding sites and terminators. It does not ship ho1:pcyA, ccaSm3, ccaR, the secretion peptides or any of the protectants. Nine parts had to be turned into Level 0 entries, and every primer that did it is built the same way, so one explanation covers all of them.

BsaI is used only at Level 1, and then BpiI again joins four Level 1 modules into one Level 2 plasmid.

Show details The full design and build, and the three ways our own records describe this screen.

The coding sequences for the green and red light components, ccaR, ccaSm3 and the ho1:pcyA operon, were taken from the CcaS/CcaR characterisation of Castillo-Hair and colleagues [1] and paired with the cargo sequences the project needed, LEA, ACCD and BoPep4. To get those into MoClo 2.0 as transcription units, each module was given a standard set of regulatory parts, and every part got a custom primer pair carrying type IIS sites: BbsI and BpiI for Level 0 entry, BsaI for Level 1, each flanked by the standard overhangs. That hierarchy is the one the build kept, even after the kit under it was swapped.

The mixtures were transformed into E. coli DH5α and plated on selective media, screened by colony PCR and read on agarose for insert size. Colonies with a band near an expected size were marked for miniprep and a confirming digest. Colony PCR and restriction digest both came back with high background: empty vectors and off-target bands that could not be told apart from an assembly failure or a PCR artefact.

The other half of the fix came from outside the team: a professor connected to the original paper supplied the SubtiToolKit [4]. That was luck rather than planning, and it is recorded here as luck.

Our records disagree: no colonies, barely any colonies, or colonies that were screened

Three statements about this cycle sit in the same document. A, the DBTL 1 test field: Transformant colonies were screened by colony PCR, which then yielded inconsistent and low-efficiency cloning. B, the DBTL 2 design field: transformation didn’t reveal any colonies. C, the light-sensing module: barely any colonies transformed. Source for all three: Engineering_.docx, 21 September 2026. The three cannot all be true of the same plates, and which one is depends on notebook pages we do not have.

Show details The seven sets and their expected sizes, two more screens, and what the sequencing added.

A plasmid is also easier to work with than a linear fragment. A pGEM-T Easy clone can be purified reliably, screened by colony PCR and sequence-confirmed before it ever meets the BpiI and BsaI digestion-ligation reactions, which keeps an uncharacterised PCR error from propagating into every downstream assembly. Three outcomes were scored negative and excluded: no band at all, a band at the size of the empty vector, meaning the vector self-ligated, and a band at any other size, meaning a truncated or mis-primed amplicon.

Show details The two BoPep4 screens from the same day, and the redesign this cycle handed to cycle 6.

Only the coding sequences that had not already been cloned in cycle 3 were affected; the ones already in pGEM-T Easy were still usable at Level 1.

Once all four modules sat in one cell, long stretches of identical sequence could give recombination something to act on, and the CcaS/CcaR signal path is exactly what would be damaged. The redesign written at the end of this cycle is one line: diversify the promoter, ribosome binding site and terminator across the modules so that no two of them share a long identical stretch. That is what cycle 6 was run on.

Every cargo built behind PcpcG2-veg in module 4 was also built this way, which is the second reason the series exists. A vector we built, and then found we did not need

In April, before the kit arrived, we built our own Golden Gate shuttle vector by moving a dropout cassette into pNW33N. The kit’s own library sheet describes pSTK-EXP-1-sfGFP as produced from pNW33N adapted for STK. The instinct was right and the work turned out redundant.

A colony PCR cannot confirm integration

The DBTL 5 test field says the gel bands matched the sequence-verification data, confirming intact genomic/plasmid integration in B. subtilis (Engineering_.docx, 21 September 2026). A colony PCR band says a template of the right length is present in that colony. It does not say where the construct sits, whether it is intact outside the amplified stretch, or whether the plasmid is carried at all. This is a wording fix, not a data fix: the bands are real and the sentence claims more than they can.

Level 2, and a stoichiometry change

With module 1 rebuilt, the four modules went into Level 2. Three consecutive Golden Gate rounds returned nothing with no design change between them. On 28 August the insert-to-vector ratio was swept at 1:5 and 1:7 and both returned positives; on 1 September two clones were confirmed across three internal junctions at once. Module 2 was built on two promoters and both survived, so the Level 2 assembly was run twice: ACDI carries the PlepA photoreceptor and ABDI the PliaG one. Until an induction curve exists there is no way to choose between them, so both were carried.

ABDI is the weaker of the two: clone V7 has been checked one junction at a time on separate gels and never all three at once. No Level 2 construct has been sequenced end to end, and the build record’s Level 2 sequencing cell is empty for both devices.

Our records disagree: recombination is both the reason and not the reason

The cloning-progress notes say the terminator went back to B0015 because recombination is not the issue. The learn field of cycle 4 and the entire design of this cycle say repeated Pveg, RBS0 and B0015 across the modules carry a high risk of triggering homologous recombination. Source for both: Engineering_.docx, 21 September 2026. What is missing is the date and the substance of the advice. If recombination was ruled out before the module 1 rebuild, this cycle needs a different stated motive; if after, the order should be said plainly.

Two failures, one experiment

This set failed twice over. The plates were contaminated, and the Western blot of the culture that was sprayed found no ACC deaminase in it, which is the 24 August result in the protectant track. So this experiment cannot be used as evidence that ACC deaminase protects a plant, in either direction, and the page does not use it as one.

What we learned across cycles

Across twenty-three cycles in seven subsystems, what stopped us was usually not what we had designed against.

  1. Verify the donor, not the product. Four of the six cloning cycles are variations on one problem: a Golden Gate reaction fed unverified DNA gives a screen where a failed assembly and a PCR artefact look the same. The day pGEM-T Easy cost us bought the end of that ambiguity.
  2. Build the control alongside the thing you want. Every light-gated cargo was also built constitutively. Because of that the protein work, the reactor run and the plant spray could all start before the switch existed, and a negative result has one explanation fewer.
  3. A measurement can be wrong in a way the instrument cannot see. The photometer read 20 % out and then four times high, and both times the optics were fine and the blank was wrong. The fix that mattered was not optical, it was a developer mode that streams both raw channels so the optics can be ruled out in twenty-five minutes instead of a rebuild.
  4. Parts sold as identical are not. Twenty green LEDs spread by 26 %. Sorting them into matched fours got each group inside about 1 %, and told us that the intensity tiers had to come from software rather than from selection.
  5. Score a design on its weakest term. A codon optimiser ranking on a sum bought a large gain at the start codon with a total loss at the ribosome binding site. Changing the objective to the weaker of the two moved the bottleneck 7.9-fold on the same sequence.
  6. Audit the method that chose the design, not only the design. The peptide audit retracted five claims, one of them after the DNA had been ordered, and the measurement that caught it was a flood fill that needed no docking and no simulation.
  7. Ask somebody who knows before you rebuild. Half the motive for the module 1 rebuild, the recombination worry, was retired by one conversation with Professor Huang. The other half stood, and the rebuilt module is the one in the circuit.
  8. Change one thing. Prototype II changed three at once and worked, and we still cannot say which change did it. It is recorded here so nobody reads it as proof that stirring alone fixed the reactor.
  9. The hard part of autonomy was sterility. We went into the instrumented prototype expecting the difficulty to be driving a pump from a microcontroller. It was getting a non-autoclavable probe into a sealed vessel.
  10. Record what a failure cost, in writing. Twelve places where our own documents disagree are boxed on this page instead of being settled here, because settling them is a decision for the people who ran the experiment.

What is still open

Does green light switch it on?
A light plate run on the Level 2 strain, green against dark, reading the output cargo. The apparatus is built and the strain exists. Until that run happens, every sentence here about the switch is a statement about the design.
Which promoter does module 2 want?
PliaG and PlepA both exist, and so do the two Level 2 devices built on them. A dose-response curve is what picks between them, and it is the same experiment as the one above.
Are the Level 2 constructs clean?
Nine correct junctions across three clones say four modules are joined in the right order. Sequencing a joined plasmid end to end is what would let us say it carries no mutation, and that cell of the build record is empty for both devices.
What does the reactor blot show?
Nine time points, three sampling sites, a named antibody, and three different readings of the result in our own documents. Reading it once, properly, is the shortest path from this page to a claim.
How does the model get its constants?
The model prescribes a light dose from Hill parameters measured on a chemically induced version of this circuit. Ours runs on constitutive promoters, so those parameters have to be measured again on the final construct before the dose it prescribes means anything. That is the same induction run as the first question here.
WB800N or 168?
The plan calls for the comparison and it has never been run. Every number on this page is strain 168.
