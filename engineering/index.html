    03

    The circuit

    ReLeaf senses stress in a plant, drought or salinity, and turns that signal into a pulse of green light aimed at a Bacillus subtilis culture in a bioreactor. The bacteria carry an engineered CcaS/CcaR circuit that answers green light by making and secreting a protectant, which goes back to the stressed plant. Making the protectant on demand instead of continuously is the whole point: it conserves the culture and keeps the reactor from overgrowing while nothing is wrong with the crop.

    CcaS is blind until ho1 and pcyA have built its chromophore

    The design follows the CcaS/CcaR system that Castillo-Hair and colleagues ported into B. subtilis [1] and that Haller, Castillo-Hair and Tabor later wrote up as a protocol [2], split into four transcription units so each part of the signal path can be built, screened and swapped on its own.

    CcaS is a photoreceptor and it cannot see anything without a chromophore. The first unit makes one. Heme oxygenase, ho1, oxidises heme to biliverdin; phycocyanobilin:ferredoxin oxidoreductase, pcyA, reduces biliverdin to phycocyanobilin. In its ground state CcaS absorbs green light near 535 nm and has low autokinase activity. Absorbing a green photon switches it to a red-absorbing state near 672 nm with high autokinase activity, in which it phosphorylates itself on a conserved histidine and passes the phosphoryl group to CcaR. Phosphorylated CcaR drives transcription from PcpcG2. Red light returns CcaS to the ground state and transcription stops.

    The move to the SubtiToolKit

    The circuit was first designed to the MoClo 2.0 standard [3] as whole-gene synthesis. That attempt produced a screen our own records describe three different ways, and the open question is set out at F8; what every version agrees on is that Golden Gate efficiency stayed low. A professor supplied the SubtiToolKit [4], a Golden Gate kit written for B. subtilis and other Gram-positive bacteria, and every subsequent cycle on this page is inside that kit. The MoClo constructs did not go to waste: they became the templates the STK-compatible parts were amplified off.

    Every part we added to the kit carries the same four-element primer tail

    The kit ships promoters, ribosome binding sites and terminators. It does not ship ho1:pcyA, ccaSm3, ccaR, the secretion peptides or any of the protectants. Each of those had to be turned into a Level 0 part, and every primer that did it is built the same way, so one explanation covers all of them.

    DesignThe circuit as whole-gene synthesis

    The coding sequences for the green and red light components, ccaR, ccaSm3 and the ho1:pcyA operon, were taken from the CcaS/CcaR characterisation of Castillo-Hair and colleagues [1] and paired with the cargo sequences the project needed, LEA, ACCD and BoPep4. To get those into the MoClo 2.0 kit [3] as transcription units, each module was given a standard set of regulatory parts, and every part got a custom primer pair carrying type IIS sites: BbsI and BpiI for Level 0 entry, BsaI for Level 1, each flanked by the standard overhangs. Promoter, ribosome binding site, coding sequence and terminator would go together into a single Level 1 unit, and the units would then combine directionally into a multi-gene circuit at Level 2. That hierarchy is the one the build kept, even after the kit under it was swapped.

    BuildRaw PCR product straight into pJUMP-29

    Each part was amplified to add its sites and overhangs. The products were purified and taken directly into a Golden Gate digestion-ligation with the Level 1 destination vector pJUMP-29, with no verified Level 0 stage in between, and the mixtures were transformed into E. coli DH5α and plated on selective media.

    TestColony PCR at low and inconsistent efficiency

    Transformants were screened by colony PCR and the products run on agarose to read insert size. Cloning efficiency was low and the screens were inconsistent. Colonies with a band near an expected size were the ones marked for miniprep and a confirming digest.

    DesignOptimise the workflow, and leave the genetics alone

    The part composition of every module stayed exactly as it was. Only the route into the vector changed.

    After cycle 2 the focus moved off the genetic architecture of the modules and onto the SubtiToolKit workflow itself. The BsaI-only Level 1 entry had produced colonies whose screens could not be resolved. The cloning strategy went back to the PCR and vector-entry stage, ahead of the Level 1 Golden Gate.

    BuildTA cloning into pGEM-T Easy, then sequence, then assemble

    Each module’s coding sequence was amplified by PCR and the amplicon cloned into pGEM-T Easy [5]. The vector is supplied pre-linearised with single 3′-thymidine overhangs, which pair with the single 3′-adenosine overhangs that Taq and other non-proofreading polymerases leave on a PCR product. The amplicon therefore ligates straight in, with no end-polishing, no restriction digest and no blunting.

    A plasmid is also easier to work with than a linear fragment. A pGEM-T Easy clone can be purified reliably, screened by colony PCR and sequence-confirmed before it ever meets the BpiI and BsaI digestion-ligation reactions, which keeps an uncharacterised PCR error from propagating into every downstream assembly.

    TestColony PCR between the T7 and SP6 promoters

    Colonies were screened by colony PCR and run against a ladder. The correct band is the distance between the T7 and SP6 promoters that flank the insertion site, so a single clean band at that size says a fragment of the right length went in at the right place. Three outcomes were scored negative and excluded: no band at all, a band at the size of the empty vector, meaning the vector self-ligated, and a band at any other size, meaning a truncated or mis-primed amplicon.

    TA cloning proved a fast and reliable way to generate verified Level 0 material. Colony PCR alone can only say that an insert of about the right length is present; it cannot rule out a point mutation or a small indel introduced during amplification. So fidelity was confirmed by sequencing, and no restriction digest was run at this stage, because correct band size plus full-length sequencing already answers both questions the digest would have answered.

    The cost was a day: colonies had to be picked, screened and miniprepped before anything could be sequenced. What that day bought was the end of the cycle 2 problem, where a failed assembly and a PCR artefact looked the same on a gel.

    DesignOne format, five cargoes, no choice made in advance

    Into B. subtilis

    B. subtilis is not easy to electroporate. Cells were grown in LB with 0.5 M sorbitol to OD600 0.85 to 0.95, washed four times in ice-cold sorbitol and glycerol buffer, and concentrated fortyfold to 1 to 1.3 × 1010 cfu/mL. Pulses were delivered across a 0.2 cm gap at 25 µF.

    The first attempt did not survive its first two pulses. At 2.0 and 1.8 kV with the resistance set to infinity the cuvette arced and the time constant read zero. Putting 300 Ω in parallel fixed it and gave a 6.7 ms time constant, and every later run used that setting.

    Where the protein goes

    The whole bioreactor argument rests on the protectant leaving the cell. If ACC deaminase stays inside, the culture has to be lysed and the shell side carries nothing, and the perfusion design has no purpose. One blot addresses that directly. On 2 August a culture carrying the constitutive ACCD construct was split into three fractions and run side by side: total pellet, soluble pellet and supernatant.

    The bands sit in the supernatant lanes, around 35 to 40 kDa. Both pellet lanes are effectively blank.

    This is the only fractionation evidence in the whole archive that the Csn secretion tag does what it was chosen to do, which makes it load-bearing, and it is one blot. There is no loading control, so a blank pellet lane could be a pellet that was underloaded.     08

    What is next

    Nothing in our records shows PcpcG2 output changing with green light. Six cycles of cloning produced four sequence-confirmed modules and two junction-checked Level 2 devices. Every one of those cycles was about getting DNA into the right vector, and none of them tested whether the circuit does what it was designed to do. That is the whole of what comes next.

    Does green light switch it on?
    A light-plate apparatus run on the Level 2 strain, green against dark, reading the output cargo. Until that exists, every sentence on this page about the switch is a statement about the design.
    Which promoter does module 2 want?
    PliaG and PlepA both exist, and so do the two Level 2 devices built on them. A dose-response curve is what picks between them, and it is the same experiment as the one above.
    Are the Level 2 constructs clean?
    Nine correct junctions across three ACDI clones say four modules are joined in the right order. Sequencing a joined plasmid end to end is what would let us say it carries no mutation, and the build record’s sequencing cell is empty for both devices.
    Does the strain actually make protectant?
    One blot lane out of three carried a band, and it was not the lane that went onto plants. Every batch that touches a plant should be blotted first, and that gate has been stated and not yet applied.
    What does the reactor blot show?
    The 44-hour run is the best protein experiment the project has: nine time points, three sampling sites, a named antibody. Our own records describe its result three different ways, and the expected mass of the fusion as built has never been computed. Reading it once, properly, is the shortest path from this page to a claim. F1.
    WB800N or 168?
    The plan calls for the comparison and it has never been run. All existing numbers are strain 168.
