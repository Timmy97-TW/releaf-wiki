Biomanufacturing

Abstract

ReLEAF grows a secreted protein protectant in a 1 L perfusion reactor beside the plants that need it, and works out what breaks when that unit is replicated.

Cells circulate on the lumen side of a 0.2 µm hollow-fibre cartridge and the protein leaves on the shell side, so one cartridge retains and recovers and the process has no downstream train. The loop has run 102.4 hours unattended at OD600 0.8 to 3.8, with transmembrane pressure within about ±0.1 bar. Green light is the command path, so production can be commanded from software without opening the vessel. Replicating the module holds wall shear and permeate flux fixed, so medium, pumping and power bind before the membrane does.

Synthetic biology for agricultural biomanufacturing

Agriculture increasingly depends on biological solutions that can help a crop survive environmental stress it cannot escape. Green biotechnology applies biological systems and engineering principles to improve crop production and resilience. ReLEAF works on agricultural biologicals: plant-protective molecules that support crops under heat, drought and salinity.

Finding a molecule that protects a plant is only part of the challenge. Biomanufacturing determines whether that molecule can be made reliably and delivered where it is needed, and cost, process control and containment are what usually stop a protective protein from reaching a field. ReLEAF answers them with a localised, responsive production model: the molecule is made at the point of use, on the day it is needed, by a machine small enough to stand beside the crop.

ReLEAF detects environmental stress through electronic sensors and activates engineered B. subtilis using green light. The bacteria produce plant-protective molecules inside a hollow-fibre bioreactor, which releases the protectants while retaining the engineered cells.

ACC deaminase is the protectant every bench result on this wiki was produced with. It interrupts the ethylene route: abiotic stress raises ACC, ACC oxidase turns ACC into ethylene, and ethylene is what reduces nodulation and growth. Cutting the chain at ACC leaves the plant its own signalling and takes away the part that costs it yield.

Optogenetics for bioproduction

We wanted production that could be commanded, not a cell locked into one output. Green light activates the CcaS/CcaR system in engineered B. subtilis, which switches on production of ACC deaminase, LEA14 or BoPEP4.

Four of the collection's five modules build that command chain: a ho1:pcyA operon supplying the chromophore without which the photoreceptor is apoprotein, CcaS‑m3 as the sensor, CcaR as the response regulator, and PcpcG2-veg driving a secretion tag fused to whichever protectant sits in the cargo slot. The fifth carries the same cargoes behind a constitutive promoter, so a negative protectant result has one fewer explanation to rule out.

For a process, light is an actuator with no mass. A chemical inducer has to be bought, stored, metered into a sterile vessel and accounted for in the product stream, and IPTG costs money on every batch. Light adds no line into the loop, leaves no residue in the permeate, and can be re-dosed from software without anyone opening the vessel. That is what lets a forecast drive production: the command path ends at an LED. It also answers the growth–production tradeoff, since a culture can build biomass first and switch to protectant when the forecast says the stress is coming.

Across six arms on 19 September, growth rate spans 0.279 to 0.317 h−1, all within one standard error of each other, and the highest-green arm reaches the highest plateau. Green induction therefore carries no measurable growth penalty at these intensities, so inducing does not trade against biomass the way a metabolic burden would.

Full record: the thirty-five parts in five modules on Parts, the logistic fit and its held-out test on Digital Twin, the builds and the gels on Results.

Protein engineering, secretion and recovery

The product is one of three plant-protective proteins: ACC deaminase, which lowers stress-induced ethylene; LEA14, a late embryogenesis abundant protein that stabilises other proteins under osmotic stress; and BoPEP4, a twenty-three residue elicitor peptide that signals drought through the PEPR1 receptor. Sequence analysis, structural modelling and targeted substitutions compared the candidates before any DNA was ordered, against two constraints at once: the receptor has to recognise the molecule, and B. subtilis has to translate it, push it through the Sec pathway and leave it whole in a protease-rich supernatant.

Two of those results changed a construct. BoPEP4 opens with an uncharged GILIGS segment that makes no receptor contact and is conserved nowhere; removing residues 1 to 6 leaves the docking score flat, so the peptide gets shorter at no cost. And the six-histidine purification tag was ordered on the wrong end — fused after Asn23 it spends the free carboxylate that salt-bridges Arg487, and under an atom-resolved criterion that bond is present in no frame of the tagged trajectory against every frame of the wild type.

Secretion and recovery are in the design, not a purification train bolted onto the end. The cell exports the protectant, it crosses the membrane, and it leaves the machine on the shell side, separated from the producer by the same component that contains it.

Full record: selection, alignments, docking and the molecular dynamics on Protein Design.

The bioreactor system

ReLEAF's vessel is a hollow-fibre module run as a perfusion culture, where containment and product recovery are the same operation.

Cells circulate on the lumen side of a cartridge rated at 0.2 µm, the protein crosses to the shell side, and the shell-side liquid is what leaves the machine. One cartridge does retention and recovery at once, so the process has no downstream train. Cells are never harvested, so the culture is a standing asset, not a batch to be consumed.

A reactor beside a field has nobody to sample it, so measurement is the part of this process we had to build ourselves. The inline photometer is the project's Process Analytical Technology tool: it reads culture turbidity continuously, in the loop, without drawing a sample, and a reference beam lets the system tell an instrument fault from a process event. The light plate apparatus is the rig that defines what a light dose is. The digital twin forecasts when stress will cross a critical threshold and triggers green light ahead of the peak. Together they make this a process that can be monitored and regulated while it runs, not only grown.

Regulation and biosafety

Farmers want to be able to say their crops are organic, and the bioreactor was built around that. The hollow-fibre membrane keeps the engineered B. subtilis inside the culture loop while the protectant passes into the product stream, so only the protectant leaves the machine.

Containment here is not a safeguard added around the unit operation; it is the unit operation. There is no filtration or kill step between culture and product, so the membrane both recovers the protein and holds the organism, and if the module fails the two fail together. That is why pressure and flow are logged continuously, why the microcontroller opens the pinch valve above 0.50 bar, and why a droplet sensor sits under the rig.

For on-site biomanufacturing deployed directly in agricultural settings, that physical separation is also what keeps the regulatory picture tractable, because ReLEAF would be regulated as three components, each following a different route.

The bioreactor is equipment

Pressure integrity, leak prevention, material compatibility, electrical safety, cleanability and routine maintenance of fluid-contact parts. The membrane is both the recovery step and the containment barrier, which is why those obligations matter here more than on an ordinary machine.

The culture is a contained GMM

The engineered B. subtilis sits in a replaceable cartridge and would be regulated separately as a genetically modified microorganism for contained use, with controls on handling, transport, storage and cartridge replacement. In the EU that is Directive 2009/41/EC.

The permeate is the product

Cell-free, so it is classified by composition, intended function and product claim rather than by how it was made. In the EU a non-microbial product whose claimed function is improving tolerance to abiotic stress can fall under Regulation 2019/1009, PFC 6(B) plant biostimulant.

In Taiwan and the United States classification is less straightforward, and can turn on whether the product is read as supporting nutrient use, altering plant physiology or acting as a plant regulator. ReLEAF therefore evaluates the biostimulant, biofertiliser and biopesticide routes separately for each protectant and each market.

Keeping the organism inside the machine is what lets that separation hold: it gives the production strain and the agricultural product two clean regulatory paths instead of one tangled one. Two themes hold in all three markets, engineered-microorganism biosafety and containment, and a plant-biostimulant or fertiliser route for the output. The core claim, improving tolerance to abiotic stress, is what keeps ReLEAF out of the pesticide frame.

Full record: the three-market comparison on Laws and Regulations, the risk assessment and the interlocks on Safety.

Market

Shipping a biological to a farm means a cold chain: refrigerated storage, refrigerated transport and a shelf life to manage. A protectant made on the farm never enters that chain, but every farm needs its own machine, and the case for ReLEAF rests on that trade.

Feasibility depends on whether the protectant a unit produces justifies the cost of running the system. The prototype build is the only cost figure we have, and it is a build cost for one hand-made unit, not a unit cost at volume: NT$52,578 for the bacterial kit and NT$30,450 for the bioreactor, NT$83,028 together, roughly US$2,800. A techno-economic analysis would set production performance and operating cost against that number to give a cost per unit of protectant, a break-even yield, and a comparison with what a farmer buys today.

A market with a large yield opportunity and a settled regulatory route is worth a unit before one with more upside and an unsettled classification, because the second spends the pilot season on paperwork rather than on the field data the business case needs.

Scaling

A process scales along one of two routes. Scale-up grows the vessel, so everything that depends on a length scale has to be re-established at the new size: mass transfer, oxygen supply, heat removal, mixing time, membrane area per unit volume. Scale-out replicates a fixed unit and changes nothing about the physics, trading a re-engineering problem for a logistics one. ReLEAF scales out [12].

Tangential-flow filtration is what makes that a decision instead of a convenience, and it is the reason hollow fibre was chosen over a stirred tank with a filter on the side. It has a formal linear scale-up method: hold every scale-independent hydrodynamic quantity constant and increase only the channel count, with transmembrane pressure controlled so the wall concentration does not move [3]. Hollow fibre suits it best, because a scale-down module can be built with the same fibre length as an industrial one. Our fixed-length module is already the replicable unit cell, not a prototype of a bigger vessel.

The membrane is last on that list, which is the point of the design and also the warning: everything above it is a deployment problem the reactor arithmetic cannot solve. Oxygen and energy bind per unit; medium and pumping bind in aggregate.

One change answers three of them. Running the recirculation pump on a duty cycle instead of continuously cuts the dominant electrical load, the cumulative shear exposure and the medium draw at once, and the reactor can afford it, because it already delivers more medium per day than a field deployment can carry and the biology responds over hours. The cost is settling in the fibres during the off phase, which is why it has not simply been adopted. It is the highest-value experiment left on the hardware side.

Dead time is what fixes the device's job. The CcaSR activation half-time of 105.1 ± 1.5 min [2] dominates every other lag in the loop by an order of magnitude, so ReLEAF is tuned for drought and salinisation developing over hours to days.

The arithmetic behind the table wall shear, critical flux, cell-specific perfusion, oxygen, energy and dead time

These numbers come from the dry lab's reactor calculation sheet of 23 September 2026. They are computed from the module geometry, not measured, and they inherit whatever the geometry gets wrong.

Wall shear is the master variable

It sweeps the membrane, it sets the critical flux, and holding it constant is what makes the scale-up linear. For laminar flow in a round lumen it falls out of the flow per fibre and nothing else:

\[ Q_1 = \frac{Q_{xf}}{N} \qquad \dot\gamma_w = \frac{32\,Q_1}{\pi d_i^{3}} \qquad \tau_w = \mu\,\dot\gamma_w \qquad \mathrm{Re} = \frac{4\rho Q_1}{\pi \mu d_i} \]

Dividing by fibre count first is the step that matters. Feeding the pump total into the single-fibre expression overstates shear by a factor of N, and that error has appeared in more than one reactor write-up. With the division done, and taking the module as eight fibres of 1.0 mm bore, the operating envelope is this.

Conventional hollow-fibre microfiltration of bacterial cultures runs at 2,000 to 8,000 s−1, and Maiorella's crossflow work puts the balance between sweeping the membrane and damaging the cells nearer 1,500 to 3,000 s−1 [8]. The pump's vendor floor of 230 mL/min [9] already sits at 4,881 s−1, in the upper half of the band; the band runs out at 377 mL/min, which is 6% of a dial that goes to 2,600. The usable setting is the stop at the bottom of the range, which is a design constraint, not a preference.

A walled bacterium is not a CHO cell, so the 3 to 5 Pa ceiling quoted in mammalian perfusion does not apply, and single-pass exposure is safe by a wide margin. Cumulative exposure is the open question: at 230 mL/min a 100 mL loop passes the fibres about 138 times an hour, and van Reis attributes protein aggregation to pump passages and air entrainment rather than to shear itself [3].

The fouling margin, and the headroom it buys

Below a critical permeate flux, deposition is negligible and transmembrane pressure does not drift; above it, cells build a cake and the membrane fouls [5]. For a particulate suspension in laminar cross-flow the shear-induced diffusion model gives the threshold [6][7]:

\[ J_{crit} = 0.072 \left( \frac{a^{4}}{L} \right)^{1/3} \dot\gamma_w \ln\!\left( \frac{\phi_w}{\phi_b} \right) \]

With a = 0.71 µm as the equivalent-sphere radius of a 0.8 × 3 µm rod, the wall fraction pinned at random close packing (0.60) and the bulk fraction computed from cell density (OD600 1.454 at 109 cells per mL per OD unit), the threshold at the 263 mL/min operating point with eleven fibres, wall shear 4064 s−1, is 46 LMH against a design flux of 2. The ratio J/Jcrit is 0.04.

The membrane is oversized for the duty by more than an order of magnitude, a side effect of choosing the module for its fibre geometry and taking the area that came with it. Flux can be raised about 23 times before fouling binds. That is still not the room needed to bring cell-specific perfusion into its conventional band, which starts at about 60 LMH for a litre of culture (below), so on this module the membrane and the medium bill bind together. Two caveats belong with that number: local flux is not uniform across the surface, so low-level fouling can occur below the nominal threshold and no operating point should be quoted as sitting exactly at Jcrit [4]; and the model treats the feed as cells in water, where secreted protein, media components and cell debris all add resistance the expression does not carry.

Feeding the culture

The invariant for this reactor is cell-specific perfusion rate, the medium delivered per cell per day, not the chemostat washout inequality D < µmax, which a 0.2 µm membrane against 1 to 2 µm rods makes inapplicable. Permeate leaves and biomass does not, so the dilution rate can exceed the growth rate without washing anything out. That is the defining property of perfusion.

Modern perfusion practice sits between 0.02 and 0.5 nL; at 2 LMH, with OD600 1.454 in 1 L and 109 cells per mL per OD unit, ours is 0.0007, about thirty times under the band, so the culture is being fed very lean per cell. Reaching the bottom of the band, 0.02, needs about 60 LMH, above the 46 LMH fouling threshold, and 0.05 needs about 150. The medium arithmetic points the same way: at 2 LMH the module draws 0.67 mL/min, which is 0.96 L of medium a day and about ten turnovers of the 100 mL loop; at 60 LMH it is about 29 L a day. At the design flux, 0.96 L a day is unremarkable for a bench run over a shift. For a device meant to sit unattended in a field for a week, the medium it would take to feed the cells conventionally is the binding constraint, and more membrane alone does not remove it.

Oxygen, energy and dead time

Oxygen caps the density before anything else does. At the carrying capacity of OD600 1.454, taking 0.4 g dry cell weight per litre per OD unit and a conservative 3 mmol g−1h−1 uptake, the culture consumes 1.74 mmol L−1h−1 and needs a kLa of 12.5 h−1. That is modest for a sparged fermenter and demanding for a closed loop with no gas port. The rate understates the problem; the inventory shows it. A 100 mL loop held at 30% of saturation contains 0.006 mmol of dissolved oxygen against a consumption of 0.174 mmol per hour, so cutting the supply empties it in about two minutes, and just under seven from full saturation. Oxygen here is a throughput, not a reservoir, and the move is to cap cell density at what the reactor can breathe and publish the cap.

Energy decides whether a unit can stand in a field at all. Design against Taiwan's worst-case winter peak-sun-hours, about three per day, not the annual average. At 75% harvest from panel to battery to load, a 45 W panel returns 101 Wh per day against a draw of 72 to 192 Wh; at a mid-point of 5 W the balance is 0.84 of what it needs, and closing it takes a 53 W panel. The entire spread comes from one quantity, the recirculation pump running continuously, and a clamp meter settles it in five minutes.

Loop dead time sums sensor sampling, controller cycle, LED ramp, the CcaSR activation half-time, secretion lag and pump transit, and the 105.1 ± 1.5 min activation half-time [2] dominates everything else by an order of magnitude. The system time constant is the shell exchange time, a hundred and fifty minutes at 2 LMH and fifteen at 20 LMH, so θ/τ lands between 0.7 and 7.0: level with the time constant at the design flux and dead-time dominated above it. A dead-time-dominated loop still works, slowly. Raising the flux shortens τ and makes the ratio worse, so delivery rate cannot be tuned on plant grounds alone.

The transmembrane pressure the scale-up method is supposed to hold constant is read at ±10 kPa against an axial drop of 11.6 kPa at the pump floor [10], so fouling has to be read off a trend rather than a single reading. A 0 to 100 kPa sensor resolves ±1 kPa and is the cheapest upgrade available to this reactor; the sensor spec is on Measurement.

Where the geometry comes from

Our own records give the module as both 150 cm² with eight fibres and 200 cm² with eleven. The one place the arithmetic has been checked against the bench agrees with eleven: on 16 August 2026, water at 22 °C, eight pump settings with the module fitted and with a jumper in its place, both fitting ΔP = aQ + bQ² through the origin at module R² 0.9954; at 263 mL/min the net module drop is 0.096 bar, 1.03 times the Hagen–Poiseuille prediction for eleven open fibres. A lumen weighed on 13 August gave 7.0 to 7.2 mL of holdup against a computed 3.74 mL. The shear tables above use eight fibres, so their shear values are the conservative, higher ones. The membrane area, permeate and medium figures use 200 cm² and eleven fibres, which is what the bench check and the rig software's constants agree on. Bench calibration of the pump puts setting 10 at about 100 mL/min against the vendor floor of 230, and three calibrations across three days span 100 to 140, which widens the usable shear window rather than closing it. The hydraulic fit is R1 on Digital Twin.
