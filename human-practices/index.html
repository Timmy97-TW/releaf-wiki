Overview

In our project ReLeaf, Human Practices acts as the heart of the team, connecting the research and work we have done in the lab with the real-world needs that help to shape our project. Rather than developing ReLeaf based only on what is scientifically possible, Human Practices allows us to consider how our project could fit into the agricultural world and how it may affect the people who would be interacting with it.

Sitting at the center of various topics such as synthetic biology, climate change, agriculture, and biotechnology, ReLeaf has reached out to a plethora of different stakeholders, including farmers, researchers, professors, agricultural organizations, companies, and members of the public. We hold deep value to every interaction, hoping to truly be able to gain and understand the different perspectives of differing groups of people, and to identify gaps and receive validation to further improve the quality and deepen the impact of our project.

Over the span of seven months, from March to September, our team actively engaged with 20 key stakeholders to refine and guide our project. To ensure a comprehensive and well-rounded perspective, we consulted experts across six diverse domains, including deployment, plant and stress biology, protectant design, gene circuit and cloning, bioreactor and hardware, and mathematical modeling.

This page is organized into four main sections. The Project Evolution Map provides an overview of how ReLeaf developed over time and highlights the key moments and stakeholder insights that significantly influenced our project. Expert Engagement documents our conversations and collaborations with experts from different fields, organized into individual pipelines based on different expertises and areas of discussion as well as our continuous engagement. Farmer Engagement focuses on our conversations with farmers, the primary beneficiaries of our project, and the insights we gained from understanding their experiences and needs. Finally, Public Engagement presents our efforts to communicate with and learn from the wider public. Together, these sections trace how our interactions with different stakeholders continuously shaped and refined ReLeaf.

Deployment

Deployment is where ReLeaf leaves the lab: who would use it, what the market looks like, what regulation allows, and whether farmers would trust it. The project began as a way to help smallholder farmers facing heat stress, in Ghana among other places, so our first goal was to check that the problem we had in mind was real.

From there we had three problems to solve. We needed to learn how to run a business around the device, to decide which farmers to serve first, and to find out which regulatory and market barriers stood in the way.

Plant and stress biology

The plant model is how we find out whether ReLeaf protects anything. We needed a plant species, a growth system and a way of applying stress that would give clean, repeatable results.

At the start none of these was fixed. The target plant was Medicago, hydroponics was the planned test system, and we did not know how hard to stress the plants or how to measure their response. We went to plant and stress biologists to settle each of these choices.

    Event summary

農友助手 LINE platform

Purpose

To improve farmers’ access to practical agricultural information, we developed a LINE platform 「農友助手」, designed to provide agricultural resources through a familiar and easy-to-use interface.

During our interactions at the Farmer Expo and Ms. Chen’s interview, we realized that although there is information all across the internet, including different websites, government pages, and social media, many smallholder farmers are still not familiar with technology, so they can’t assess the resources and find a solution to the problems they are facing efficiently and easily.

Since LINE is an app that is widely used in Taiwan, we established an official account to make our resources more accessible to users. By simply clicking the designated icon, users can be directly redirected to the corresponding website.

Platform design

The main menu of the LINE platform (農友助手) contains six functions: Latest News(最新消息), Product Introduction(產品介紹 ), Crop Stress Alerts(壓力警報), Crop Assistant(作物小幫手), Farmer’s Diary(農友日記 ), and Feedback(意見回饋 ).

Together, these websites were designed to help farmers from receiving information and recognizing problems to finding solutions, recording observations, and providing feedback more easily.

Latest news 最新消息

The Latest News section provides users with news throughout the year, including farmer expos, farmer markets, and workshops. Information will also be posted on the website, including dates and locations.

Product introduction 產品介紹

The Product Introduction section introduces our bioreactor to users. By integrating the information into a brochure, users can easily understand its function and use with our slogan: “An on-site, on-demand bioreactor for smarter crop protection. Instead of explaining to users the biological and engineering mechanisms behind our project, we decided to design the website in a way that users can clearly understand at a glance.

This allows users to understand not only how our bioreactor solution works, but also how synthetic biology and agricultural technology could potentially be integrated into real farming systems.

Crop stress alert 壓力警報

From our interviews with the farmers, we noticed that many were triggered by environmental stress. The Crop Stress Alert section was designed into a dashboard with a weather forecast and biological risk system. The system can predict heat stress for the upcoming days and also suggest users to take action.

The website is divided into sections based on different regions of Taiwan, including Northern, Central, and Southern Taiwan, allowing users to easily assess heat stress conditions by selecting the region in which their farm is located. Each regional section contains individual blocks representing different cities. Within each block, the heat stress index is displayed on the left, while the biological risk index for crops is presented on the right.

The range of the index represents the severity of the heat stress and biological risk, with each numerical value corresponding to specific recommended actions for users.

Below the index, it will provide more information on the heat stress based on the Taiwan Central Weather Administration’s heat stress information [2]. Additional biological risk information will also be provided based on the information from the Taiwan Animal and Plant Health Inspection Agency (APHIA) [3].

Crop assistant 作物小幫手

Crop Assistant is a website designed to support farmers by suggesting appropriate actions based on their reported farming problems and weather conditions.

Users will be required to answer the questions and report their problem in three steps, including select or entering the type of crop that is being affected, select the region where their crops are being grown. They may also enter their specific location, identify the part of the crop currently affected by the issue, such as the leaves, roots, or flowers, and describe the current weather conditions, the symptoms of the crop, the duration of the issue, the impact on the farm, and the presence of any pests. By providing these information about their situation, the website will be able to deliver a more accurate suggestion.

After completing the questions, the website will explain the causes of the issue and analyze how each factor may affect the crop, providing users with a better understanding of the problem. It will also suggest some possible solutions and prevention to this issue. If users have additional questions after reviewing the analysis, a built-in AI system will be available to provide further explanations and answer their inquiries.

However, as our website is not intended to replace professional agricultural expertise, we recommend that users consult official government resources before taking any action. Relevant government websites and resources will be provided below for users to access easily.

Farmer diary 農友日記

To establish a platform that enables users to communicate, exchange experiences, and share information, we developed the Farmer Diary. The platform allows users not only to share posts about their farming experiences but also to ask questions and receive responses from other users.

Each account is limited to a maximum of five posts per day. Users can select the day’s weather conditions and upload photographs of their crops along with a description. Additionally, users can record the soil moisture and provide information about pest conditions of the day.

After each post, users will earn 10 tokens, which can be exchanged for rewards, such as LINE stickers, once they reach a certain number of tokens.

There will also be a dedicated question section where users can ask questions about their problems related to crops without any posting limit. All users will be able to view and respond to these questions.

Overall impact of the booth activities

Our booth event at the Taipei Water Garden Organic Farmers’ Market gave us a great chance to connect our lab work with the general public. Through our six interactive stations, covering coffee farming, plant stress maps, drought sound recordings, ring toss games, an online defense game, and DIY crafts, we were able to explain synthetic biology in a way that felt approachable and easy to understand.

The feedback we collected showed that people really connected with the activities. Over 90% of visitors said they left with a better understanding of plant stress and how ReLeaf works, and more than half remembered specific details like Yilan being a high-stress farming area. Almost everyone had a positive experience at the booth, and 96.8% said they planned to share what they learned with friends and family.

More importantly, talking to visitors gave us real insights that helped refine our project. People were fascinated by the drought sound recordings, which confirmed that using natural plant signals was a great way to explain our biosensors. On the other hand, their questions about soil safety reminded us how important it is to be transparent about our non-harmful, biocontained design. These conversations helped ensure that ReLeaf stays focused on safety, real-world usefulness, and community trust.

Conclusion

Integration of Human Practices

Human Practices was embedded into ReLeaf from day one, serving as a continuous feedback loop that helped us understand how to improve our project further. Across seven months from March to September, every stakeholder’s insights directly impacted and led to changes in research direction, plant focuses, and hardware designs, ensuring that our technical development was constantly shaped by real-world needs.

Implementing Our Human Practices

Our outreach was also carefully planned around specific knowledge gaps and technical challenges we have identified as time went on. We provided clear context for each stage of ReLeaf by examining existing agricultural methods, prior work, and current biotechnology limitations, helping our team understand the current global reality and where our solution could step in to relieve these issues. Every expert interview and field visit had a defined purpose, where members of the team conducted deep research regarding current roadblocks we were facing and the person that would be able to help the most with our problems. This helped with ensuring our team approached stakeholders with well-researched questions and implemented their feedback with clear rationale, making each visit targeted and with a clear goal set in mind.

Responsible Innovation

Throughout our project, the question of how we could design a solution that was safe and helpful remained in our minds. To achieve this we engaged in interviews and meetings with several experts such as farmers, biosafety experts, and plant biologists. All these helped ensure that ReLeaf was designed with safety, ethics, and environmental consideration at its core. By consulting specialists in deployment and gene circuit safety, we prioritized biocontainment and responsible release mechanisms, ensuring our solution actively protects crops under climate stress without introducing ecological risks. Furthermore, we have also been in contact with farmers, the primary customer of our project, to ask for their opinions and concerns to make sure the problem we are trying to solve is actually an issue that farmers face.

Stakeholder Engagement

Together, we gathered perspectives across a broad spectrum of 20 stakeholders across six distinct domains: deployment, plant and stress biology, protectant design, gene circuit and cloning, bioreactor and hardware, and mathematical modeling. Combining these technical insights with the lived experiences of local farmers and public feedback allowed us to balance high-level scientific rigor with practical usability in the field.

Documentation & Continuity

In previous pages, we have also mapped out our entire Human Practices journey on this page, including our Project Evolution Map, pipeline breakdowns, stakeholder interview summaries, and the specific design changes triggered by each interaction. Our Integrated Human Practice page records and mapped out every single one of our interactions and the specific steps we have taken afterwards. By transparently detailing our successes, challenges, and methodology, we provide an accessible, step-by-step roadmap that clearly showcases the journey we have been through as a team.

Broader Impact

Lastly, ReLeaf demonstrates how synthetic biology can move beyond abstract lab research to tackle urgent global challenges like climate change and food security. By showing how open dialogue with communities and experts can transform a student project into a practical, human-centered solution, we hope to inspire future researchers to build science hand-in-hand with the people it is meant to help.
