Bronze Medal Criterion #3

Make a useful contribution for future iGEM teams and document it on this page.

What the criterion asks of a team, and where on this page each part is answered.

  1. Make a useful contribution for future iGEM teams. What we are contributing, four things listed in the order of how much work they save.
  2. Document the contribution. Hardware other teams can rebuild, Protocols and methods and Parts.
  3. Explain why the effort is a contribution to fellow iGEMers. How to reuse this, written as a three week plan, and The limits of what we are handing over for what nobody should build on.
  4. A team may offer a part, non-part documentation, or both. What we offer is methods and hardware rather than a part. Parts says how far our Registry documentation currently goes.

What we are contributing

The most useful thing this project can hand over is not a part. It is the set of measurement methods behind the two instruments we built, written with the mistakes that produced them still attached. A team that copies the blanking procedure on Measurement and the jumper run on Measurement will avoid two errors that each cost us about three weeks.

Four things are ready for somebody else to use, and they are listed here in the order of how much work they save.

WhatWho it is forWhere it is
Thirteen measurement methods, each written as a method rather than a result, with the way it fails named Any team measuring optical density in a loop, transmembrane pressure across a cartridge, or chlorophyll in a stressed seedling Measurement
Twenty-one protocols, each carrying a line saying what it was actually run for, and sixteen boxes naming the errors we found in our own written protocols A team repeating Bacillus subtilis cloning, electroporation, Western blotting or agar salinity work Experiments
Two instruments with a full design record: a dual-light-path photometer with a 0.2 mm flow cuvette, and a 24-channel two-wavelength LED array, each with printable models and four build iterations A team that needs inline optical density, or a controlled light dose across replicate tubes, and cannot buy either Hardware · photometer, Hardware · DiOPAL
A troubleshooting record: six cloning cycles, each ending in the change it caused, and twelve open contradictions between our own records, named rather than tidied away A team hitting the same Golden Gate, marker-clash and screening problems Engineering

What is not on this list is a characterised part. Our parts are documented on Parts, and the section below says plainly how far that documentation goes.

Hardware other teams can rebuild

Two of our four instruments can be rebuilt from what is on this wiki today. Two cannot, and saying which is which is part of the contribution.

The inline photometer

The instrument reads a culture through a 0.2 mm flow cuvette without opening the loop. Its optical train is an amber LED, a paralleling lens, a slit and a nominal 90:10 beamsplitter, with the reference sensor reading before the sample rather than after it, which is the arrangement that makes the ratio mean something. The teardown on Hardware carries the printable models, and the build was printed in black PLA with a 95A TPU lining at 0.2 mm layers and 15 per cent gyroid infill, with a 2 mm slit. The electronics are an Arduino UNO Q with GY-302 sensors, and the dashboard logs at ten-minute intervals.

The four iterations are the part worth reading before building one. Version 1 sat horizontally and trapped bubbles in the path. Version 2 turned the column vertical and added luer bleed valves. Version 3 tilted it by ten degrees and took the error from about twenty per cent down to eight, and left the LED off-centre. Version 4 locked the optical positions and lined the housing, and it is not yet validated: it reads thirty-three per cent low against a ten per cent target.

The DiOPAL light array

Twenty-four tubes over twenty-four LEDs in a light-tight box, arranged as two wavelengths by three tiers with four resistance-matched LEDs per column, driven from one Arduino Uno R4 WiFi. Forty LEDs were bought and each was measured individually; the twenty-four that fell into tight groups of four became the six channels. The models and the matching procedure are on Hardware.

Two admissions travel with it, and a team that copies the array should copy them as well. The three tiers are not intensity tiers: the measured green channels span 2.33 to 2.40 kLux, which is LED binning. And the dose is reported in lux, which is the wrong unit for an optogenetic experiment and has not been converted to photon flux.

What cannot be rebuilt yet

The perfusion bioreactor page says it directly: suppliers, costs and several specifications are still outstanding, and the fibre count, membrane area and hold-up volume in its own materials list are not mutually consistent. The membrane and the pump are identified by model number only in the reference list on Results. The hydroponics rig names four models that are not in this repository. Until those are fixed, what the reactor contributes is its failure record: the pump-head leak and the sedimentation that version 1 hit, the stirring that fixed both, and a retention claim the team withdrew in writing.

Protocols and methods

Experiments carries twenty-one protocols, and each one states what it was run for, including the ones whose answer is "no result on this wiki". That line is the contribution as much as the protocol is: it tells a reader which of our methods has been through a real experiment and which has only been written.

Six methods changed because of something that went wrong, and those are the ones worth taking.

MethodWhat changed, and whySource
Blanking the inline photometer Blank on distilled water, not on medium. Blanking on medium put a roughly twenty per cent offset into the readings. Measurement
Rejecting bad optical readings A burst median with a three-MAD window. Before the rule was written down, the rejection rate read zero per cent on one run and forty-three on another. Measurement
Transmembrane pressure Run the rig once with a jumper in place of the cartridge. A team that measures a pressure drop without measuring its own tubing first is reporting the tubing. Measurement
Chlorophyll reporting Say which normalisation is used. Normalising to fresh weight instead of plant count inverted the ranking of the same four samples. Measurement
B. subtilis electroporation At infinite resistance the cuvette arced and the time constant read zero; 300 Ω in parallel gave 6.7 ms. The time constant is the diagnostic to watch, and the voltage sweep is on the page. Engineering
Western blotting Photograph the ladder under white light before the exposure, and load the supernatant beside the pellet. Both are why our fractionation blot can be read at all. Measurement
Three protocols carry an uncorrected error

Three of our written protocols carry errors we have found and not yet corrected: the agar salinity protocol weighs out salt for 250 mL and then pours 200 mL, one chlorophyll sheet reads 633 nm where the method says 663, and another states a 2 mL extraction volume while the formula uses 4 mL. They are boxed on Experiments as E8, E12 and E13.

Parts

The reusable piece of our cloning work is the primer set. Nine parts the project needed did not exist in the SubtiToolKit, and each was converted with a primer carrying the same four-element tail: a spacer, a BpiI site for Level 0 entry, a BsaI site for Level 1 assembly, and the annealing region. The tail is explained once, with what each element does, on Engineering, and the thirteen primers are tabled there.

The honest position on the parts themselves: seven are sequence-confirmed at Level 1 and two at Level 0, eight exist as designs only, and none has a fluorescence, absorbance or enzyme activity number behind it. The Level 2 devices are junction-checked and have never been sequenced end to end. All of that is set out on Parts.

Where the documentation must live

If a part is used to fulfil this criterion, its documentation has to be on the part’s entry in the Registry. This page points at it; it cannot stand in for it.

Eight Registry entries have been opened and all eight are empty (Parts). Until they carry the sequence, the assembly and the screening evidence, no part on this wiki can carry Bronze criterion 3, and this page should not imply that one does.

How to reuse this

Assume three weeks and no budget beyond a printer and an Arduino. This is the order we would do it in.

  1. Read the four build iterations of the photometer before printing anything. Three of the four exist because of a mistake that is cheap to avoid and expensive to discover.
  2. Print the optical column and the holder, wire the two sensors, and blank on distilled water. Then run the titanium dioxide series on Measurement against whatever benchtop spectrophotometer you can borrow, because that comparison is what tells you your instrument works before any culture is at stake.
  3. If you need a light dose across replicate tubes, build the DiOPAL array, and measure every LED individually before you mount it. Report the dose in photon flux, which is the step we did not take.
  4. If you are cloning into B. subtilis, read the six cycles first. The two that cost us most were entering Level 1 straight from unverified PCR product, and using an intermediate vector carrying the same resistance marker as the destination.
  5. Before any protocol here is run, open its fix boxes on Experiments. Our own protocols contain errors we have found and published rather than quietly corrected.

The limits of what we are handing over

Nobody should build on a result we do not have. These are the limits as our own pages record them on 25 September 2026.

  • The photometer is not validated. Its fourth version reads thirty-three per cent low against a ten per cent target, and the fitted calibration equation, its R2 and its held-out validation are still marked as outstanding on Hardware. Our own pages disagree about the length of its longest run.
  • The light array has never been used for the experiment it was built for. No CcaS/CcaR induction run exists anywhere in this project (Results), so nothing here shows that the array can drive a genetic circuit.
  • The bioreactor is a record, not a kit. Costs, suppliers and several specifications are missing, its own materials list is internally inconsistent, and every measurement comes from versions 1 and 2 in June.
  • No part is characterised. No fluorescence, absorbance or enzyme activity measurement exists for any part we made, and the Registry entries are open and empty.
  • No protectant has been shown to help a plant. Six trehalose sets and four peptide trials produced no effect that survives its own controls (Results).
  • Some of our numbers are still contested by our own records. Twelve open fixes on Engineering and ten on Results name places where two of our sources disagree. Anyone reusing a number from this wiki should check whether it sits inside one of them.
