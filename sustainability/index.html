Overview

Sustainability has always been a topic on the world’s mind, and evident in every sector and corner of our lives. Our project started as a way to help farmers combat climate-related stress, but as we continued to do our project we realized that one of the best ways to combat that is to incorporate various concepts of sustainability into our project. Sustainability is not only limited to environmental sustainability, but also the sustainability of our societal structure, ensuring that the education and knowledge we provide to the next generation is sufficient to sustain current progress and advance, to sustain the rights of all people regardless of demographic. That is what sustainability is to GEMS-Taiwan, and here are some ways that we’ve tried to make our own contributions to the big mission that is sustainability. These are based on the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) agreed upon by the United Nation (UN) to achieve by the year 2050.

2 Zero hunger 4 Quality education 10 Reduced inequalities 15 Life on land

Why we chose the SDGs that we did

Out of all the 17 SDGs available, we chose our SDGs first based on their relation to our project, then we narrowed it down based on which ones we can actually make an impact on and change, whether through hypothetical implementation of our business plan, through current real-life action, or hypothetical usage and results of our bioreactor. Through the first condition, we can whittle the SDGs down to SDGs 2, 3, 4, 7, 10, 13, and 15.

All of these SDGs are ones that we want to impact and might be able to through our project, but ultimately we had to condense it down to only the ones that we can actually do. For example, while Climate Action can be affected through improving the environment through using our bioreactor, it isn’t the main point of our project and neither is what we do rooted in resolving climate change in itself. Therefore, we might have an impact on the SDG, but it’s not significant enough to be considered as an SDG that we can claim we target. After putting all the related SDGs through similar processes of elimination, we ended up with SDGs 2, 4, 10, and 15, all of which are targets that we actively considered and either have or plan to enact in our project.

Our SDGs

SDG 2

Zero hunger

Problem

While we are aware that there are a plethora of factors that contribute to the food crises worldwide, such as distribution, one of those factors is food production, where agricultural yield is actively decreasing due to issues such as climate stress and pollution, while the population grows at an exponential rate. This is especially acute for farming families that not only sell their crops, but rely on their farm to feed themselves, forcing them to make compromises between eating and earning money. Early on into the project, we talked with the leader of Ubuntu Social Enterprise Ghana. From that meeting, we learned that it’s true that climate is actively harming their crops and making it harder to farm on generational land.

Solution

Our project can directly impact this and increase food production, as the usage of our protectants on crops can prevent them from having intense adverse effects to stress and instead continue to focus on producing their crops. We’ve proven that plants can grow better under stress with our Arabidopsis, which as a relative to mustard plants we know will at least translate into some other crops. In the long term, our bioreactor can help gradually increase the amount of crops that are produced and reduce the impact of chemical pollution by allowing the land to heal over time on its own. Though we do not intend to directly solve world hunger, we do hope to alleviate not only farmers’ financial pain but also provide more healthy and sustainable food sources to the world.

SDG 4

Quality education

Problem

A lot of people have misconceptions regarding synthetic biology due to not knowing where to look or not having access to the resources to learn about it. This may cause them to act maliciously or ignorantly towards new inventions and products due to that very lack of understanding.

Solution

We have an education website where we compiled all of the education resources from this year and previous years, along with basic introductions to synthetic biology. Through this website, people can learn more about synthetic biology and teachers can sign up for us to teach lessons for their classes inside that website. Additionally, we’ve done a public forum where we introduce our project and explain about synthetic biology to the public. Through this, we hope to be able to reach out and tell people that synthetic biology is something that will be used to help and advance society, and encourage people to learn more about synthetic biology on their own.

SDG 10

Reduced inequalities

Problem

Industrial farms often have the resources, money, and ability to obtain various technologies and hardware that can help them run their farms, while small farmers often face monetary issues or reservations regarding using newer products on their farmland, because of limited space. This makes it so that large farms can afford to constantly advance with technology, grow more crops, and create a loop that can easily generate profit while small scale farmers must be more careful and conservative in terms of technology, which can prevent them from increasing profit.

Solution

We plan to use the profit we obtain from selling devices to industrial farmers or selling data to researchers to subsidise small farmers’ purchases or allow unions or similar organisations to rent bioreactors instead, making the price more manageable and friendly towards them. This way, both sides of the spectrum can use and enjoy the benefits of having more advanced regulation and protectant technology without breaking the bank.

SDG 15

Life on land

Problem

Chemical fertilizers and protectants often cause runoff and environmental pollution, which can lead to the destruction of local ecosystems. Issues such as eutrophication, poisoning, and accidental killing are all things that arise from the usage of chemical fertilizers and protectants on farms. This is not only detrimental to the environment, but also causes farmland to later become harder to cultivate crops on and make the resulting crops to be unhealthy for the human body.

Solution

While our product cannot reverse the damage done to the environment and soil directly, it can prevent further deterioration of its surrounding ecosystem. The protectants produced from our bioreactor are entirely natural and already exist in plants prior to exposure to our bioreactor, and the safety measures in the bioreactor itself ensure containment unless under extenuating circumstances. Usage of our bioreactor over time can allow for the previous pollution to slowly go away on its own while maintaining and gradually increasing the quality of soil.

Negatives

We can say without hesitation that we want our project to do as much good to the world as possible, but it’s unavoidable that the usage of our product might indirectly have adverse effects on the environment and attitudes of the people that use them. Here, we want to acknowledge those negatives as a part of our product that we hope we can change, but also realize that no matter how good something can be there will always be inherent adverse effects to everything.

“Sometimes, giving too much is not necessarily beneficial for a living organism; instead, it can take away its ability to survive and thrive on its own.”

Ms. Chen, Tamsui Happy Farm, 21 July 2026

Though we want to promote sustainability, and while our project’s method of producing protectants is undeniably more sustainable than chemical protectants, the usage of our product doesn’t promote longevity. Due to the fact that our product always immediately supplies plants with the necessary protectants, it might make it so that the plants never actually grow their own resilience and have to rely on our protectant to grow properly, which can actually be a net negative and set back the progress on plant resilience. This was mentioned by an organic farmer, Ms. Chen, who stated that her plants eventually grew to be as big as they are because she allowed them to grow and survive on their own – essentially survival of the fittest to let her plants thrive. Plants that use our protectants will have a bit of a hard time building resilience on their own, and may even succumb to climate stress more easily once taken off of our protectants.

Long-term impact

Our team does not only want to give lip service and not actually make any impact on these SDGs – we also want to have a long-lasting impact on future iGEM teams that may be following a similar path and on the community around us. We have already established part of this through our education website, which can provide resources for teachers that wish to inform their students of climate stress and shed light on synthetic biology and will remain permanently up. Additionally, through the business plan we have laid out development plans for up to 5 years, and what goals we want to achieve beyond iGEM. From our geospatial analysis, it can be seen where our project will have the largest impacts and where we are needed the most.
