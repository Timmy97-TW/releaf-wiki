Molecular Dynamics

The contact everything is measured against. Asn23, the last residue of BoPEP4, buried at the closed end of the PEPR1 groove where its free carboxylate reaches Arg487. Seven of the nine runs are, in the end, a question about whether that one bond holds.

Docking scores a pose. Molecular dynamics asks whether the pose survives being shaken. Nine all-atom trajectories were run on the PEPR1 ectodomain, eight of them carrying a peptide, and the set answers questions that no single run can: how much receptor motion would have happened anyway, what the acceptance criteria are actually measuring, and what the acidity outside a plant cell does to the interface.

What the method is

Molecular Dynamics (MD) simulation is a fundamental computational methodology that uses statistical mechanics to evaluate the physical properties, structural stability, and mechanism of action of engineered proteins and peptides. By integrating Newtonian equations of motion, MD tracks the velocities, positions, and interactions between molecules over time. We applied this to our project to understand the translation and secretion efficiency of our protectants by analyzing the interactions between different receptors.

9 trajectories

Eight carrying a peptide, one with the receptor alone.

30 ns production each

270 ns in total, 13,500 saved frames, one random seed per system.

127,600 atoms per system

The PEPR1 ectodomain, residues 29 to 738, solvated in explicit water.

25.9 GPU-hours

One A100, about 250 ns per day, one analysis pass over all nine runs.

Every system was built on the PEPR1 ectodomain from PDB 5GR8 and solvated in OPC water with AMBER ff19SB [1][2]. Hydrogen mass repartitioning allows a \( 4\,\mathrm{fs} \) time step. Because the numbers on every run come from one analysis codebase applied to all nine trajectories, the columns mean the same thing from row to row, which is what makes the cross-run reading in section 3 possible at all. The protocol in full, with the equilibration schedule, is on MD Simulations.

The nine runs

Four things change across the set and nothing else does: which ligand is in the groove, which residues of it are present, whether the C-terminus is free, and whether three histidines are protonated. Each run below links to its own report, which carries the trajectory, the per-residue occupancies and the plots.

The two controls

These are the runs that can falsify the rest. One has an experimental structure to check against; the other has no peptide at all, which puts a measured floor under every receptor-side number in the set.

  • AtPEP1 7 to 23 benchmarkKQRGKEKVSSGRPGQHNBenchmark

    The peptide actually resolved in the 5GR8 crystal structure, run under settings identical to the other eight. It is the only system with an experimental structure to check against, so it sets the scale the rest are read on.

  • Receptor aloneNegative control

    The peptide removed entirely. The receptor is no looser, the groove does not collapse, and the per-residue flexibility profile barely moves. That is what makes a receptor-side difference on any other run readable as a real difference.

The truncation ladder, in motion

The same four constructs the docking ladder produced, taken into simulation to see whether the plateau it found survives. It does not survive uniformly.

  • BoPEP4 wild type, 1 to 23Reference state

    The complete mature sequence with a free C-terminus. The other seven BoPEP4 trajectories are all read against this one.

  • BoPEP4 7 to 23Hypothesis falsified

    Putting Lys7 and Lys8 back onto 9 to 23 restores the clamp, and not because a free-terminus artefact went away: Lys7 grips Asp129, a salt bridge that is not on Tang's list of eight. This is also the first construct that is like for like with the benchmark.

  • BoPEP4 9 to 23The construct that was ordered

    Deleting residues 1 to 8 loosens the anchor at the far end of the peptide. The clamp flickers open 94 times in 30 ns, none of the gaps longer than 0.68 ns.

  • BoPEP4 15 to 23Control that did not fail

    Planned as a negative control that would fall off and supply a leaving time. It did not fall off, and it could not have: unbinding runs on microseconds to milliseconds, three to six orders of magnitude past 30 ns.

The histidine tag

  • BoPEP4 with a C-terminal 6xHisFalse pass

    Six histidines fused after Asn23. All four acceptance criteria pass and the C-terminus scores as steadier than wild type. But Asn23 is no longer a C-terminus: its carboxylate was spent forming the amide bond to His24, so the criterion is measuring a neutral carbonyl rather than a salt bridge.

Why this run is the one the layout document points at

Here a check passes for the wrong reason. Ranked on backbone RMSD, the tagged construct comes first in the whole set, and it is the one construct whose salt bridge does not exist. A criterion written before a construct existed can measure atoms that construct does not have, and nothing in the score says so. That is the argument for putting the tag at the N-terminus, and why N-His against C-His stopped being an arbitrary choice.

Protonation: the apoplast at pH 5.5

The peptide does its work outside the plant cell, where the pH is nearer 5.5 than 7. Two runs test what that changes, by switching three histidines to their protonated form and touching nothing else.

  • BoPEP4 1 to 23 at pH 5.5Positive result

    A Glu12 and His227 pair appears at once and holds, and the AtPEP1 benchmark already makes that same pair at neutral pH. The C-terminal anchor is untouched.

  • BoPEP4 9 to 23 at pH 5.5Failed the gate

    Truncation crossed with protonation. Every metric except the anchor improved and the anchor collapsed: the now-positive His22 folds back onto the peptide's own C-terminal carboxylate and displaces Arg487.

What “pH 5.5” means here

Molecular dynamics has no pH. The label names a protonation state held fixed for the whole 30 ns: His14 and His22 on the peptide and His227 on the receptor carry a positive charge, every other histidine is neutral, and all aspartates and glutamates are deprotonated. The assignment comes from model pKa values, not from a constant-pH simulation.

What the set says

Four results need all nine runs to exist. Each is stated here in one line, with the full working on MD Simulations.

A stability score does not track the bond it is standing in for

Backbone RMSD over the shared 15 to 23 window, the kind of number constructs usually get ranked on, correlates with the Asn23 and Arg487 salt bridge at Spearman \( +0.29 \), and against the four-contact clamp it runs \( +0.03 \) the wrong way. The practical consequence appears twice in this set: ranked on RMSD, the tagged construct comes first and the failed run comes second.

Ten receptor residues hold in every run

Pool the per-residue contact occupancies across all eight peptide-bearing runs and ten receptor residues stay above 50% in every one, whatever the peptide is doing at the other end. Five of the ten are on the list of eight hotspots Tang and colleagues read off the AtPEP1 complex [3], and five are not.

The apo run sets the floor

With no peptide at all, the receptor Cα RMSD is \( 1.57\,\text{Å} \), below all seven peptide-bearing runs it can be compared with, and the binding groove holds at \( 35.91 \pm 0.55\,\text{Å} \). Any receptor-side difference smaller than about half an ångström elsewhere in the set is inside that floor and should not be read as a result.

Acidity recovers a contact the benchmark already has

At pH 5.5 the Glu12 carboxylate locks onto the His227 ring at \( 2.75 \pm 0.11\,\text{Å} \) in 99.9% of frames; at pH 7 the same pair sits \( 5.33\,\text{Å} \) apart and appears in 0.8%. The AtPEP1 benchmark makes that contact natively at neutral pH. The recommendation that follows is that future BoPEP4 simulations default to pH 5.5 with pH 7 as the control.

Limits

Thirty nanoseconds is three to six orders of magnitude short of a dissociation event, so nothing on this page is a binding affinity, a leaving time or a free energy. Each system has one random seed, so a difference between two runs may be a difference between two relaxation paths rather than between two constructs. The acceptance gate was written before the tagged construct existed and, on that construct, measures atoms it does not have. And none of it has been checked at the bench: no binding assay, no thermal shift, no plant.

The set is useful for deciding what to build and what to measure next. It is the reason the tag moved to the N-terminus, and the reason pH is now a parameter rather than an assumption.
