Reading a docking score

Method · HADDOCK score and clustering

Every run below reports a HADDOCK score, and the score means less than its precision suggests. This applies to the whole page, so it is stated once here instead of repeated in each record.

This series is the one place in the project where the scoring function is measuring the variable under test, and the record should say so plainly. The methodology audit on 30 July found the HADDOCK score across BoPep4 variants 93% explainable by peptide length and net charge alone, and that its correlation with Pearce's measured activities is rho ≈ +0.05, where zero is no relationship. A truncation ladder changes length and net charge at every rung: 23 residues at +4.1 down to 15 residues at +2.1.

So the ladder's score differences are being produced by the two quantities the audit identified as the score's actual content, and a monotone trend across the ladder would be uninformative no matter which direction it ran. A second case is sharper, because it is a failure, not a confound. The K8Q+K18R double collapses on PEPR1 by every geometric measure available: AF3 ipTM 0.49 against 0.75 to 0.89 for the rest of the panel, interface PAE 9.45 Å against 1.62 to 2.78 Å, the fewest receptor contacts at 42, nine hydrogen bonds against 16 to 21 elsewhere, and a position-18 anchor that has slipped off Asp320 onto Glu296 at a broken 3.42 Å.

HADDOCK scores that complex −194.7, which sits inside the −187 to −218 band occupied by the nine healthy complexes. The matrix logs it as DISCORDANT-HADDOCK-rescues. The function looked at a mis-registered interface with half the hydrogen bonds and called it normal. Two consequences follow, and both are already in the methodology. The score is interpretable within a protocol and not across ligands. The alanine scan is the favourable case: same receptor, same restraints, same length, same charge, one side chain removed from one fixed pose, so a score difference has nowhere else to come from.

The ladder is the unfavourable case, which is why the cliffs are reported as a hypothesis about which residues matter and not as a measurement of how much binding is lost. And it is why the load-bearing evidence moved. Where the cliff claim now rests is geometry and occupancy, not score: the salt-bridge inventory showing which bridges dissolve at which cut, and then contact occupancy over 30 ns showing which of those bridges were held and not sampled.

Both were measured on the same ladder. Neither is a HADDOCK score. The ladder picked the cut points worth simulating, which is what a confounded ranking can honestly be used for.

One note covers every cluster table below. The statistics were recomputed from the run archives against 5GR8 chains A/J, not copied from a report; fnat reproduces exactly on the four representatives still on disk, and L-RMSD and i-RMSD to within 0.14 Å.

Alanine substitution

18 single-point mutants · HADDOCK 2.5 water refinement at 40/10/10

Methodology

The BoPep4 interface map was built from one validated docking pose: residues buried, salt bridges measured, contacts listed at a 4.5 Å cutoff. It says which side chains sit against PEPR1. It cannot say what the interface loses if one is removed, and by late July that was the question the engineering panel needed answered, because every proposed substitution and every truncation was about to assume a position was expendable.

An alanine scan answers it with a perturbation, not a measurement. Delete one side chain, let the complex relax, record what the energy function says, and repeat at every position. The ranking that comes out is produced by a method sharing no assumptions with a burial calculation, so agreement between them is evidence and disagreement is a question. Eighteen single-point mutants, Ser6 through the C-terminal Asn23, were built on the validated native-mode complex instead of being predicted from scratch: side-chain atoms beyond Cβ deleted, backbone and Cα-Cβ geometry held fixed.

Mutating on a fixed pose is what isolates the cost of the substitution; re-docking each mutant would have let it find an unrelated register and returned a number about that instead. All 19 systems, 18 mutants plus an unmutated baseline reused from trunc1-23_PEPR1, went through identical HADDOCK 2.5 water refinement at 40/10/10 with the same restraints, so score differences are attributable to the mutation. Electrostatic and van der Waals terms were kept separate, to tell a lost salt bridge from lost packing, and peptide backbone deviation was recorded as a pose metric independent of the score.

All 18 completed.

ResultsKey results6 findings
  • Eight hotspots that fail the same way. K7, R9, R11, E12, H14, K18, H22 and N23 clear +7 on both metrics, and every one is carried by a large positive ΔEelec, from +32.6 at H14 to +150.4 at K18, with ΔEvdw flat or favourable throughout. That is the signature of removing a charged side chain from a salt bridge, and it rules out a packing artefact introduced by the mutagenesis. K18A and K7A are the largest hits. K7A is also the only mutant that moves the peptide, by 1.62 Å, so it costs binding energy and pose retention together.
  • Cross-validation at 13 of 13. Of the positions where the interface map made an unambiguous call, eight anchors and five non-contacts, the scan agrees with all of them. Two methods with no shared assumptions returned the same interface.
  • Asn23 ranks last of the eight hotspots. N23A is +9.5, which sits oddly against Asn23 having the largest buried surface at 170 Å² and four hydrogen bonds. The explanation the data support is that several of those contacts engage the C-terminal carboxylate and backbone amide, not the side-chain atoms an Asn to Ala substitution removes, so part of the clamp survives. That distinction is what the C-terminal tag question turned on later: substituting at position 23 keeps the chain terminus, appending to it does not.
  • Ser15 and Ser16 disagree with their own conservation. Both are invariant across all 16 characterised Pep-family sequences and were assigned as core hydrogen-bonded contacts. Neither registers, at −1.3 and −8.0. A serine hydroxyl contributes little to an electrostatic term next to a full salt bridge, so the conservation is real and this assay is not positioned to explain it.
  • The scan would not have found either control. S15A and G17A were ordered as loss-of-function controls, on published activities roughly 100-fold and more than 4000-fold below wild type. The scan returns −1.3 and −2.9 at exactly those positions. A method that misses the two substitutions the published work shows matter most is a method whose silence elsewhere carries no weight, which is the same conclusion the July audit of HADDOCK and AlphaFold3 reached by a different route.
  • The glycines move and the prolines do not. G20A and G21A show the largest backbone deviations in the set, 1.21 and 1.42 Å, with no score effect, consistent with glycine accommodating dihedrals an alanine cannot. The hypothesis floated before the run, that the prolines would show the same signature from losing backbone rigidity, failed: P10A, P13A and P19A sit at 0.75 to 1.03 Å, inside the neutral range.

  • The binding site is closed and the construct is open. Eight positions are protected as binding-critical, and five more, S15, S16, G17, G20 and G21, are protected on conservation grounds even without a score penalty. Truncation ladder

Δ1-2, Δ1-3, 7-23, 9-23, 15-23 · HADDOCK and AF3

Methodology

The alanine scan had just established which positions the interface cannot lose, and by implication which it can: S6, K8, P10, P13 and P19 neutral on both metrics, with the map putting residues 1 to 6 outside the contact region entirely. That licenses editing the N terminus. It does not tell you where to cut. The distinction matters because a substitution and a truncation are not the same experiment. A point mutation removes one side chain and leaves the chain.

A truncation removes residues, shortens the peptide, changes its net charge, and moves the N terminus to a new residue that now carries a free α-amino group. Which consequence dominates could not be predicted, so the series was run as a ladder: cut at successive points, score each one on the same protocol, and find where the interface stops tolerating it. Two things made this the sprint the project needed, not a curiosity.

The published work already reports the 9-23 core retaining activity within a factor of ten, so a short form was known to be viable and the question was only which short form. And the manufacturing case was strong enough that if the binding cost were tolerable, the decision was already made. The ladder existed to find out whether it was tolerable, and where.

ResultsKey results4 findings
  • The anchor belt lies downstream of the cut. The atlas established the architecture the ladder is cutting into: an extended peptide clipped into an acidic LRR groove by a three-point salt-bridge belt at Arg9, Lys7/Lys8 and position 18, with the buried Asn23 anchor at the end. The first belt residue is Lys7. Shaving the N terminus removes only Gly1 to Leu3, which make no anchor contact, and the belt is retained intact in every truncated complex.
  • Removing the anchorless N terminus costs nothing measurable. The shallow cuts keep position 18 on its canonical partner and come out ahead of both substitutions on AF3 confidence, which is the structural licence for treating N-terminal length as an engineering axis. Those numbers sit in the Δ1-2, Δ1-3 record instead of being repeated here.
  • The series then moved past them. Δ1-2 and Δ1-3 were the July finalists and were not ordered; the cut moved further down the ladder to 9-23, with 7-23 alongside it, on manufacturing grounds and not on a binding result.
  • MD located the boundary, and then moved it. The wild-type run put the fluctuation break at the same residue the ladder's cliff falls on: Gly1 fluctuating by 3.51 Å, everything from Glu12 onward under 1.0 Å, and Arg9 at the boundary with an RMSF of 1.32 Å, a mean closest approach of 2.79 Å and 100% contact occupancy. Two methods with no shared assumptions landed on Arg9. The truncation runs then complicated it: clamp integrity is 100.0% for 1-23, 98.5% for 7-23 and 99.9% for 15-23, but 77.1% for 9-23. The cliff is real and it is not where length predicts, because 9-23 keeps the 9-14 segment while removing the Lys7-Asp129 salt bridge that anchors it, and 15-23 removes both together. Shorter is not worse; losing an anchor without the segment it anchors is worse. Project
ResultsProject3 findings
  • The production molecule came out of this series. 9-23 is the manufacturing candidate and the construct already in hand as D-01 and D-04. Against full length it carries 33% less mass, 1,612.73 Da against 2,409.74 Da, one trypsin-type cut site instead of three, and half the positive charge at +2.1 against +4.1, so less of it sticks electrostatically to the producer cells. It also removes the greasy GILI front segment, which is the part that raises a self-toxicity risk in B. subtilis, where no amount of promoter tuning would recover the yield. The cost is the published factor of ten in activity. It costs nothing to settle at the bench. Both lengths were ordered, 9-23 as D-01 and D-04 and full length as D-03 and D-05, so the head-to-head titre can already be run in one sitting. That is the clearest example in the project of a dry lab result shaped so the wet lab can decide it.
  • Engineering moved away from the binding site. With the C terminus off-limits and the eight hotspots protected, N-terminal length was the only axis left that changes the molecule's manufacturability without touching the interface. The truncation series is what established that axis, and the later tag-placement result depends on it: the N terminus is the end that flaps and the end the ladder shows is removable, so it is where a tag can sit without touching a contact.
  • Nothing computational separated the rungs, so the whole ladder was sent. 7-23 is more stable than 9-23 on every MD metric recorded, with clamp integrity 98.5% against 77.1%, Asn23-Arg487 99.9% against 81.1%, and 151 receptor contacts against 129, while 9-23 is the cheaper molecule to make. 15-23, 9-23, 7-23 and full length all went to the bench as one series, so the titre settles a question the dry lab could not.

Tandem repeat analysis

17-sequence Pep-family alignment · chain-end chemistry

Methodology

The alanine scan and the truncation ladder both ask what the receptor needs. The alignment asks what the peptide family keeps, which is a different and cheaper question: every position that is invariant across a family has been held there by selection, and selection sees the whole job, not only docking. That was needed because the methods in hand had failed on exactly this axis. The 30 July audit found the HADDOCK score 93% explainable by length and charge and uncorrelated with measured activity, and AF3 unable to separate BoPep4 from a peptide more than 4000-fold dead.

Conservation is the one line of evidence in the sprint that is independent of both, since it is observation and not prediction. Tandem repeats were the same week's second question. If a single copy engages one receptor, two copies on one chain might engage two, and for a production platform a potency gain per molecule secreted is worth more than a binding gain.

ResultsKey results5 findings
  • The conserved region stops at 20, not 23. 15 to 23 had been described as the conserved block. The alignment supports an invariant SSG at 15 to 17 and an invariant Gly at 20, with 21, 22 and 23 variable. BoPEP4's own Gly21 is a minority residue at that position.
  • Asn23 is not invariant in this set. All seven characterised AtPEPs carry Asn there, and so does BoPEP4, but five of the nine Brassica entries do not: BnPep4_E5L6 has Ser, BnPep6_DM88 has Asp, and the three Pep1 orthologs end in His. BnPep4_E5L6 is BoPEP4's closest relative at two differences. The defensible sentence is that Asn23 is invariant across the characterised AtPEP set and 12 of 17 in the Brassica-inclusive alignment, which is not what the pages currently say.
  • The invariant positions are the ones the docking score ignores. This is the finding that ties the sprints together. The alanine scan returned −1.3, −8.0, −2.9 and −3.9 at S15, S16, G17 and G20: no penalty at any of the four positions the family has never changed. Published work puts S15A at roughly 100-fold and G17A at more than 4000-fold weaker, and Tang reports G20A harmless while G20I kills binding. So the invariant block is held by selection for something a binding-energy refinement does not measure, most likely backbone register or a step after engagement. Conservation and docking score are answering different questions, and the conserved block is where they visibly disagree.
  • BoPEP4 aligns to AtPEP1 gapless, 10 of 17 ordered positions identical, at mature 7, 9, 12, 15, 16, 17, 19, 20, 22 and 23. Every identity falls inside the 7 to 23 region. The confirmed figure matches what the MD page already states.
  • Tandem repeats cannot work for this peptide, on chemistry. Only the final copy in a tandem chain has a free C-terminal carboxylate. In every upstream copy that carboxylate is consumed into the peptide bond joining it to the next copy, and that carboxylate is what salt-bridges Arg487 at 100% occupancy inside a 0.54 Å window. An n-copy construct therefore carries n−1 copies that cannot engage, which is the same mechanism that kills a C-terminal His tag. The groove compounds it: Asn23 sits buried in a pocket at the end of the run with no exit channel, so there is nowhere for a linker to thread out. A linker placed further upstream to avoid that lands inside the invariant 15 to 20 block.
ResultsProject4 findings
  • Multivalency is closed as a potency route. Yield per litre and length per molecule are the levers that remain, which is where the truncation decision came from. No tandem construct is in the panel, and this is the reason to give.
  • A second independent route reaches the same tag conclusion. The tandem argument and the 6xHis result rest on one chemical fact about chain ends. The codon-optimised cassettes designed in June and July append CATCATCATCATCATCAT directly after the mature peptide, so the tag sits on exactly that carboxylate; the constructs ordered after 16 August carry the tag at the N terminus instead. Two analyses reaching it by different routes is why tag placement moved to the front instead of being treated as a modelling caveat.
  • Support for the crop-range claim. BrPep4_A5G1 differs from BoPEP4 by one residue and BnPep4_E5L6 by two, so the Brassica Pep4 orthologs are effectively the same molecule. A peptide designed for one Brassica crop is not obviously specific to it, which is the in-silico support under the crop-range experiment.
  • The assay controls gained a rationale beyond the literature. S15 and G17 are two of the four positions the family has never changed, a stronger reason to expect them to break than a transferred activity figure.

A second question travels with tag placement and was settled separately: whether a spacer is needed between the tag and the mature protein. The concern is that an N-terminal tag sits next to the signal-peptidase cleavage site, so it could move the site or lower the confidence of the cut, and a short neutral spacer is the conventional insurance against that. SignalP 6.0 answers it directly, and the test was run as paired submissions: seven N-His designs, each submitted once without a spacer and once with one, fourteen sequences in total. These were the LEA14 constructs, not the peptide ones, and the result is reported here because it is what settled the question for the tag.

ResultsKey results4 findings
  • The spacer earns nothing, so there is none in the constructs. All fourteen sequences return Sec/SPI at 0.998 to 0.999 with the cleavage site between positions 35 and 36, and every pair agrees: the spacer never moves the site, and it shifts the cleavage probability by at most 0.002, from 0.9709 to 0.9745 across the whole set. Fourteen submissions to show seven were unnecessary is a cheap way to retire a worry, and the paired design is what makes the negative readable and not an absence of evidence.
  • The C-terminal tag scores best and places worst. C-His returns the best HADDOCK score anywhere in the nine-run set at −138.4, and the largest buried areas at 2,260–2,582 Å². It also reaches only the same CAPRI ceiling as the rest. A tag that adds surface adds score, which is the confound the methodology audit describes.
  • N-His keeps the anchor; C-His does not. N-His cluster 1 holds the Arg487 contact at 3.0 Å, inside the 2.40 Å crystal figure plus tolerance. No C-His cluster passes the anchor check. That is the structural form of the chain-end argument made on chemistry grounds in the tandem repeat sprint.
  • Best geometry in the set, on a construct that would not be shipped. C-His cluster 2 is one of only three medium-tier clusters across all nine runs, at fnat 0.308 and L-RMSD 4.71 Å. Score and tier both favour the construct the chemistry rules out.

Conservation and stability screen

ConSurf 1791277560 · Q5PWZ8 AlphaFold model · DDMut 17915452271577165

Methodology

Docking was the obvious method and it was rejected on the chemistry. ACC deaminase is a pyridoxal-5′-phosphate enzyme of fold type II, and its cofactor is not a guest in the active site: PLP is held as an internal aldimine, a covalent bond to the ε-amino nitrogen of Lys51. The substrate then displaces Lys51 to form an external aldimine, so it too is covalent before catalysis begins. A docking search returns the best non-covalent pose, which is a pose this enzyme never adopts. Every atom below is placed by superposition onto an experimental structure instead.

The four ACC deaminase designs all rest on one question the sequence can answer more cheaply than a structure can: which positions has the family held fixed. ConSurf was run in ConSeq mode on the Q5PWZ8 wild-type sequence, searching UniRef90 with MMseqs2, removing redundancy with CD-HIT at 95%, aligning with MAFFT and rating by the Bayesian method, returning 150 homologues, and the grades were then read against distance and burial for the eight candidate positions.

Running conservation before the stability screen was deliberate. A variant the family has never produced does not need a ΔΔG calculation spent on it, and the ordering from the grades changed which variants were worth submitting. The stability screen then went to DDMut as a batch of 17 single point mutations, scored independently.

The template is a different organism, so the transfer was checked instead of assumed. The construct is UniProt Q5PWZ8, ACC deaminase from Pseudomonas putida UW4, which has never been crystallised. PDB 1TZ2 is a 2.10 Å structure of the enzyme from Pseudomonas sp. ACP, UniProt Q00740, solved with PLP and ACC both bound in all four chains. The two sequences are 82.8% identical, and of the 58 positions where they differ exactly one, D164E, lies within 8 Å of the substrate, a conservative solvent-exposed swap of one carboxylate for another. The active site being reasoned about is identical in the two proteins.

The superposition was then gated, with the threshold written down before it was measured: catalytic Cα RMSD under 1.0 Å and all ten residue identities matching. If the catalytic residues had not landed on each other the transferred ligand positions would be wrong, every distance measured off them would be wrong, and no stability number computed on that model would mean anything. Stating the threshold in advance is what makes passing it informative.

One correction sits underneath all of this. Three HADDOCK runs were submitted earlier on the Pseudomonas ACP background, before the switch to Q5PWZ8: 772607-ACCD_V293T, 772601-ACCD_Y156C and 772641-ACCD_CYS-Ala. Val293 and the cysteine positions are identical in both sequences, so those two transfer. Position 156 is phenylalanine in Q5PWZ8, not tyrosine, so the Y156C run does not transfer and the design is correctly named F156C.

ResultsKey results13 findings
  • The alignment passes its own internal control. MSA size 150 sequences, median per-position coverage 149 of 150, no position of the 338 flagged as insufficient, and a grade spread of 56 positions at grade 9 against 16 at grade 1, so the scale discriminates. The catalytic residues Lys51, Ser78, Asn79, Tyr268 and Tyr294 all return grade 9, which is the check that the alignment is biologically sensible.
  • A third control residue came back wrong in an instructive way. Gln80 scores grade 9, which is what an active-site residue should score, but the alignment column at that position is histidine at 89% and glutamine at only 4%. The grade is measuring that something is fixed there, not that the residue carried here is the fixed one.
  • The homologues are fold-type II, not ACC deaminases. Glu295 scores only 37% glutamate and Leu322 only 37% leucine. Those two residues are what distinguish ACC deaminase from D-cysteine desulfhydrase, so their variability shows the 150 homologues span the wider family. Conservation here measures structural importance across fold-type II, not ACCD-specific function, and the wiki has to say so.
  • The project got a structure of its own enzyme. ConSurf's model export returned the AlphaFold structure of Q5PWZ8: 338 residues, sequence matching the construct exactly, mean pLDDT 97.7. The B-factor column carries pLDDT, not conservation grades. This is the first structure of the enzyme actually being built.
  • The instructor's gating question is answered. Superposing the Q5PWZ8 model onto the ACP structure gives global backbone RMSD 0.61 Å over 331 residues, catalytic CA RMSD 0.36 Å with a worst case of 0.58 Å, and 10 of 10 catalytic residues matching, with Lys51 NZ at 0.81 Å, Tyr268 OH at 0.70 Å and Tyr294 OH at 0.44 Å. The catalytic residues superpose with a wide margin.
  • Conservation inverted the risk ranking. At positions 162, 196 and 67 the residue the family carries is alanine or glycine, not cysteine, so the construct holds the minority residue at each and substituting to alanine moves towards the family consensus and not away from it. C196A matches the most common residue outright at 43%, and C162A matches the second most common at 36% behind glycine at 54%. Distance alone had ranked these as the risky ones.
  • Alanine is cheaper than serine at all six cysteines. By +0.02 to +1.06 kcal·mol−1, with the margin growing with burial. Cys196 is the only cysteine where nothing passes the cutoff. C162A is the cheapest Cys→Ala at −0.26 and is also the one variant with published experimental support, Murakami et al. 1997 reporting activity unaffected. The same paper reports that chemical modification of that thiol abolishes activity outright, and that C162G and C162V raise Km for ACC without changing kcat much. The side chain at 162 takes part in substrate binding, so C162S, bulkier and polar, carries more Km risk than C162A does, and both are run.
  • All six cysteines are genuinely unpaired. The two Cys41 side chains face each other across the two-fold axis at SG–SG 7.90 Å, far too distant for a disulfide, and no other pair is close. That licenses treating every cysteine as a free-thiol liability, not a structural one.
  • Three of the seventeen variants came from the alignment and not from convention. Reaching for alanine or serine whenever a cysteine has to go is the conventional move, and Dalton et al. 2020 argue against it: their REP-X strategy takes the replacement from the closest homologue carrying anything but cysteine. Read against the 150-sequence alignment, that endorses positions 67, 162 and 196, where alanine is what the family carries at 77%, 36% and 43%, and challenges two others, so C41L, C41V and C282L were added and the conventional C41S and C282A kept alongside them.
  • Conservation grade does not predict ΔΔG, and that is the useful result. Across the six cysteines the correlation between grade and ΔΔG is ρ = −0.09, and across all seventeen ρ = −0.33. What does predict the cost is packing: ρ = −0.64 between heavy atoms within 6 Å of the sulfur and the best ΔΔG available at that position. Cys282 sits at the most tolerant position on the list, grade 3, yet C282A costs more than C162A at grade 7. Two filters that agreed completely would mean one of them was redundant.
  • The four subunits are equivalent, so chain A was not a special choice. Cys41 sits 3.85, 3.68, 3.89 and 3.86 Å from its partner in chains A, B, C and D, the D2 symmetry holds to about 0.2 Å, and every position has the same set of residues within 8 Å in all four chains. For thirteen of the seventeen rows the single-chain calculation is not an approximation at all: every position except 41 has its nearest neighbouring subunit at 8.3 Å or more.
  • The 39–50 loop is not a flexible lid in this crystal. Mean Cα deviation between the four chains there is 0.11–0.15 Å, below the whole-chain average of 0.23–0.25. The genuinely variable regions are 129–143 and 249–252. This matters because another variant under discussion treated 39–50 as mobile, and the crystal gives that no support.
  • The monomer was a legitimate model for the active site, and only for that. None of Lys51, Ser78, Asn79, Gln80, Trp102, Tyr268, Tyr294, Glu295 or Leu322 has a single atom within 5 Å of another subunit. The interface objection applies to Cys41 alone.

Unpaired cysteines removed

six free thiols · HADDOCK 772641 (ACP background, transfers)

Methodology

Six unpaired cysteines are a shelf-life liability in a protein meant to work outside the cell: free thiols oxidise, cross-link and aggregate. The question is which of the six can be removed without costing activity.

The cysteine positions are identical between the ACP sequence and Q5PWZ8, so 772641-ACCD_CYS-Ala transfers despite predating the correction. The screen then ran conservation first and stability second, with both alanine and serine submitted at every buried position because the local environment does not discriminate between them reliably.

ResultsKey results8 findings
  • The six removals separate into two groups by distance, not by grade. On the supplied model, Ala196 sits 3.97 Å from the cofactor and Ala162 7.23 Å; the other four sit between 12.4 and 22.1 Å. The two the stability screen found expensive are the two inside the pocket, and the four it found cheap are the four outside it.
  • Three of the six are consensus-directed substitutions. At Cys67, Cys162 and Cys196 the family carries alanine or glycine. Removing the cysteine moves the protein towards the consensus and not away from it.
  • Cys95 is the one removal that goes against the family. Cysteine is the norm at that position at 73%, with alanine at 19%. It is retained in the proposed construct for that reason.
  • Cys196 is the one position where nothing passes. C196A at −1.32 and C196S at −2.05 both fail. It is also the most tightly packed of the six, with 49 atoms within 6 Å of SG and Tyr294 O at 3.67 Å and PLP C5A at 4.55 Å, packing directly against the catalytic nucleophile. Retained.
  • Cys162's sulfur hydrogen-bonds to an active-site residue. Gln80 NE2 at 3.08 Å and OE1 at 3.39 Å. This does not rule out substitution, since the family carries glycine and alanine there, but it belongs in the write-up.
  • Cys41 is not the safe exposed residue it looked like. On the monomer it reads as 68.3 Å² exposed, which made it the lead variant. On the tetramer it is 37.0 Å² with the thiol itself at 15.4 Å², sitting at the A–B interface with eight cross-subunit atoms within 5 Å and its sulfur resting on the face of Phe13 from chain B at SG–CZ 3.85 Å. It has to be scored on the tetramer.

A large obligate interface, which is why the monomer was the wrong model for position 41.

Conservation alignment

446 LEA14 homologues · run locally · exposure from the 1XO8 structure

Methodology

The question is where LEA14 touches AtPP2-B11, and nothing in the literature answers it: the two proteins are known to interact and that is all. There is no structure of the pair, no mutation that breaks the interaction, and no crosslinking data. The surface had to be predicted.

Conservation alone is not the signal. A position that never changes but sits buried is holding the fold together, not binding anything, so the analysis crosses two axes that have to be measured separately.

For the first, 446 orthologs were pulled from UniRef50 and aligned with MUSCLE 5, and each of the 151 positions was scored as Henikoff-weighted Shannon entropy, which down-weights near-duplicate sequences so that a clade over-represented in the database cannot manufacture conservation on its own. For the second, relative solvent accessibility was computed by Shrake–Rupley against the Tien 2013 maxima, on PDB 1XO8 model 1. 1XO8 is an NMR structure covering all 151 residues with nothing missing, so no position had to be inferred.

Crossing the two axes splits the protein into 18 residues that are both conserved and exposed, 18 conserved and buried, and 115 that are neither. The conserved-and-exposed set is where a partner would be expected to bind, and the question is whether it forms patches or scatters.

ResultsKey results5 findings
  • Two conserved patches, and only one of them survives the exposure test. Patch 1 is residues 56, 57, 79, 125, 127 and 130; patch 2 is 111, 112 and 113. Patch 2 is the one that goes on to be confirmed by an independent method.
  • Trp112 is the single strongest signal in the protein. It is the only tryptophan in all 151 residues, it holds tryptophan in 92.3% of the 446 aligned sequences with no gaps at that column, and its relative accessibility is 52.4%. Tryptophan is the most common residue at protein-protein interfaces. An exposed, never-changing tryptophan is rarely an accident.
  • It sits between two near-invariant aspartates. Asp111 scores 98.9% and Asp113 99.0%, both with no gaps in the column. A charged-aromatic-charged triplet held almost fixed on the surface is the shape of a binding site and not of a folding constraint.
  • Strictly invariant is a higher bar than any of them clears. Exactly seven positions score 1.000 across the 446 sequences: Met1, Pro22, Gly74, Gly80, Tyr116, Pro127 and Pro135. None belongs to either patch, and Met1 is the initiating methionine, which is an alignment artefact, not a finding. Patch 2 is the strongest signal in the protein without being the most conserved thing in it, and the write-up should say so in those terms.
  • LEA14 is stoichiometric, not catalytic, which is why this matters. An enzyme turns over, so one molecule does many jobs. LEA14 binds and stays bound, so one molecule does one job and the amount that can be made and delivered is the whole constraint. A tighter binder is a smaller dose, which is what makes the interface worth mapping at all.
ResultsProject2 findings
  • It produced the one testable prediction on this strand. W112A, replacing the single tryptophan with a small inert residue, is one codon. If binding drops, the interface has been located experimentally, which is the thing no amount of computation was going to deliver.
  • The exposure figure is on the re-run list. 52% comes from the local calculation; the WHISCY run computes 53.8% relative accessibility for the same residue by its own method. The two agree closely enough that the conclusion is unaffected, but only one of them is a job with a record behind it, and the provenance rule applies here as it does on the ACC deaminase strand.

WHISCY on LEA14

WeNMR run 44IUlMAx · local run 9 October · 117 surface residues

Methodology

WHISCY applies the same conservation logic as the alignment above and adds two things it did not have: knowledge of which residue types turn up at real interfaces, and smoothing, so it finds patches and not isolated positions. Running it is an independent check, not a repeat.

Inputs were 1XO8 model 1 as chain A at full length, and the same 446-sequence alignment, with interface propensities and surface smoothing both on. Two options that look like formalities are the reason this is an independent check and not a repeat: propensities bring in which residue types actually turn up at interfaces, which the entropy score knows nothing about, and smoothing combines each residue’s own score with its neighbours’ by distance, so the output is patches, not isolated positions.

The run is six stages, and the smoothing is where the two scores meet: whiscy_setup derives the surface list and the conversion table from the structure, whiscy scores conservation twice, globally and locally, whiscy_consadjust applies the propensity adjustment, whiscy_resdist builds the residue distance matrix, and whiscy_parasmooth combines the adjusted score, the local score and the distances into the ranking that is read. Two further stages write the score into the B-factor column and into a HADDOCK-ready file.

One preparation step was not optional and is worth recording, because getting it wrong would have produced a confident and meaningless answer. WHISCY requires the query sequence to be first in the alignment. MUSCLE had reordered both alignments, leaving the LEA14 query at index 251 and the AtPP2-B11 query at index 9. Both were moved back to position 1 and the ungapped query was checked character by character against the PDB chain sequence, 151 and 257 residues respectively, before upload.

It was run twice, once on the WeNMR server and once locally. Both score the same 117 surface residues and return the same passive set. They differ in where the active cut falls, and that difference is reported below instead of resolved by preference.

ResultsKey results4 findings
  • It confirms patch 2 independently. Asp113 ranks 2nd of 117 surface residues, Asp111 3rd, Asp107 next door at 4th and Trp112 8th. Two methods with different logic return the same answer, which is the basis for saying LEA14’s side of the interface is reasonably well mapped.
  • Its top-ranked residue is one the alignment analysis never mentioned. Leu144 scores 0.200, ahead of Asp113 at 0.199 and Asp111 at 0.184. It appears in neither conserved patch and is absent from the pipeline note. Either it belongs in the restraint set or there is a reason to exclude it, and neither has been written down.
  • The two runs disagree about which residues are active. The server run lists twenty residues as active and the identical twenty as passive. The local run splits them, with 107, 111, 113 and 144 active and the same twenty passive. For HADDOCK the distinction is not cosmetic: an active residue is penalised when it does not end up at the interface and a passive one is not, so a run with both tiers identical gives the protocol no tier information at all.
  • Patch 1 does not appear in the result. None of 56, 57, 79, 125, 127 or 130 reaches the top of the WHISCY ranking. The patch that survives is the one the second method also finds, and the other is not carried forward.
ResultsProject4 findings
  • The local split is what the restraint set should use. Four active and twenty passive matches the explanation this strand actually gives, in which Trp112 ranks 8th and not in a top tier, and it gives HADDOCK a real distinction to work with. The server run is kept as the citable job record. This is a recommendation, not a decision on file.
  • Leu144 is an open item before any docking run. It is the method’s own first choice and it is in the local active set. Including or excluding it changes the restraint list, and the reason either way has to be written down before a run is submitted and not after.
  • An independent cross-check is available and has not been run. 1XO8 is in the PDB, so WHISCY can be run against its HSSP alignment instead of ours by passing HSSP_ID = 1XO8. That substitutes a different sequence set for the one built here, which makes it a genuine second opinion, not a repeat: agreement would be hard to argue with, and disagreement would show the result depends on the alignment choices. It was identified as worth doing when the inputs were prepared and has not been done.

The other side of the interface

AtPP2-B11 · WeNMR run 44IUuepJ · 29 then 2,224 sequences

Methodology

AtPP2-B11 is the only published LEA14 binding partner: 257 residues, part of the cell’s protein disposal machinery, with a handle at residues 1 to 44 that clips it into that machinery and a target-grabbing half at 92 to 255. There is no experimental structure of it, so the model used throughout is AlphaFold.

The same two methods were applied, with one deliberate decision about the input. The full-length chain was uploaded and not the trimmed one. Residues 1 to 44 are the F-box, which is occupied by ASK1 inside the SCF complex and so is not available to LEA14 at all, but trimming the structure while supplying a full-length alignment risks a numbering mismatch between the two, which is the kind of error that produces a clean wrong answer. The full chain went up and predictions below residue 45 are discarded when the output is read.

The conservation analysis had produced candidate patches here too, so the WHISCY run had something to agree or disagree with: patch 2 at residues 147, 150, 221, 223, 224 and 225, and patch 1 at 171, 185, 186, 187, 189, 235 and 238, the second of which sits on a low-pLDDT loop and was already held loosely for that reason.

Both methods failed, and the way each failed is the result.

ResultsKey results5 findings
  • The first alignment was flagged as too shallow before the run, and retracted after it. Twenty-nine related sequences were all that the obvious similarity cutoff returned, and the output looked excellent: a surface covered in perfectly conserved residues. The setup note already recorded 42% of the protein scoring exactly 1.00 and called it a depth problem, not a quality one, with Pfam PF14299 across plants named as the fix. The run went ahead anyway, labelled provisional, and the rebuild then confirmed the diagnosis. With twenty-nine near-identical sequences, “never changes” and “has not had a chance to change” are indistinguishable.
  • Rebuilt on 2,224 sequences, the answer inverted. Sequences were chosen from across the plant kingdom for the same two-part architecture. The residues that had looked conserved now rank in the bottom third, and the whole protein holds just two conserved exposed residues, not adjacent to each other. Everything genuinely conserved is buried, holding the fold.

Such a family evolves fast precisely at the gripping surface. If that is right, conservation-based prediction cannot work here at any alignment depth, a property of the protein family, not a gap more work would close.
ResultsProject3 findings
  • It stopped a confident model from being built. A docking run guided by the 29-sequence result would have produced a plausible complex and an indefensible one. The retraction happened before the restraints were used, which is the part worth reporting.
  • Alignment depth is now a checked parameter, not an input. The failure mode was specific and cheap to test for: if a large fraction of a protein scores as perfectly conserved, the alignment is too shallow. That check belongs on any conservation analysis this team runs, including the 446-sequence LEA14 one, where it passes.

Those point in opposite engineering directions: tighter binding is good under one and bad under the other. No structure settles it. Until it is measured, the project does not know whether a tighter binder is the goal, and that matters more than the interface map.

Methodology

The confidence threshold was written down before the LEA14 result was looked at.

One further prediction was then run containing three chains, LEA14, AtPP2-B11 and ASK1, the component of the disposal machinery that clips onto AtPP2-B11’s handle. That interaction is well established, so a single prediction contained a known answer and an unknown one at the same time.

ResultsKey results5 findings
  • This is the run that ended the LEA14 interface work. Nothing was submitted on this strand afterwards. The alignment collapse on AtPP2-B11 had already made the restraints unreliable, and a strand can survive a weak restraint set; it cannot survive a pre-declared confidence gate failed by a factor of five with its own controls passing in the same breath. The decision to stop was taken on this result and it was the right one.

Synonymous recoding on Window A

ViennaRNA 2.7.2 · 8,000 synonymous candidates per gene · three phases

Methodology

ViennaRNA was used on the assumption that reducing mRNA secondary-structure stability around the MF001 ribosome binding site (RBS) and the start codon improves ribosome attachment. Translation performance was assessed with minimum free energy (MFE) values and unpaired-probability predictions, alongside codon adaptation index (CAI) values taken against B. subtilis 168 codon-usage data from the Kazusa database. The approach follows Castillo-Hair et al. 2019 on optogenetic control of B. subtilis gene expression, which the CcaSR system here is built on.

All analyses ran on the RS-inclusive Window A, which models the pre-recombination transient mRNA state: RS (28 nt) + MF001 RBS (24 nt) + A spacer (1 nt) + SamyQ signal peptide (93 nt) + CDS first 90 nt. MFE is the free energy of the most stable secondary structure. start+15 and start+30 are the mean unpaired probability over the first 15 or 30 nucleotides from the ATG. SD core is the mean unpaired probability over the 7-nucleotide Shine-Dalgarno core, AAGGAGG.

Phase 1 held the 93-nt SamyQ sequence static and recoded only the first 15 codons (45 nt) of each passenger gene independently, from a library of 8,000 random synonymous candidates, scored on a balance metric bal = start+15 + SD core. Phase 2 addressed elongation through CAI against the host matrix and appended a C-terminal 6xHis purification tag, but full-length optimisation degraded translation initiation by lowering start+15. Phase 3 departed from standard host codon adaptation by making SamyQ codons 2 to 31 editable, which reduced secondary-structure stability around the initiation site further and increased accessibility.

Results, RS-MF001 construct

Candidate variants with SamyQ and CDS both editable. The GR candidate, highlighted in yellow, is the Castillo-Greedy candidate, which prioritises the start+15 value and disregards the SD and CAI values. The best candidate, in green, was selected on the highest balance score.

ResultsKey results3 findings
  • Each construct gains on the axis it started worst on. BoPep4 and ACCD start with an almost closed SD core and take most of their gain there, 9.62× and 7.45×; AmilCP, which starts with the most open start region at 0.678, moves start+15 by only 1.30× and the SD core by 13.45×. LEA14, the one that begins with the most open SD core, moves least on both.
  • Changing more bases does not by itself open the start region. The greedy control on BoPep4 differs at 51 nt yet improves the first 15 ORF codons only from 0.456 to 0.496. On LEA14 the greedy control ends up worse than the wild type, dropping from 0.512 to 0.505 on ORF accessibility and from 0.895 to 0.748 on balance, so a recoding rule applied without folding each candidate can move a construct the wrong way.
  • The highest start+15 values are bought with the SD core. Some BoPep4 candidates reach start+15 as high as 0.911, but only by leaving the SD core closed and dropping CAI to 0.56. On AmilCP the top start+15 values sit between 0.990 and 0.995, so the balance score is effectively choosing on SD core alone.
ResultsProject2 findings
  • ACCD was ordered twice over. Its best candidate carries the lowest CAI of the four, 0.59 against 0.85, which is why ACCD went out as both a CDS-only design (v2) and a SamyQ + CDS design (v3) instead of committing to the recoded signal peptide untested.

The leader swap re-run

RiboJ + RBS0 · pre-cleavage transcript 310 nt and mature mRNA 282 nt

Methodology

Cloning the constructs with the MF001 RBS proved difficult, so the construct was changed to the STK EEBD-RBS0 part, which places RiboJ, a self-cleaving hammerhead ribozyme insulator, upstream of RBS0. RBS0 carries the same Shine-Dalgarno core (AAGGAGG) as MF001 and sits 6 nt from the start codon against MF001's 8 nt, both inside the B. subtilis optimum, and every recoded codon lies downstream of the ATG. On that reasoning the prediction was that the ranking should survive the part swap.

It does not follow. A ViennaRNA accessibility score is a property of a folded window and not of the coding sequence, and if the first 25 nt of that window are replaced by a different 99 nt, the window no longer describes the construct that was built. The eight cassettes had already been ordered and carry MF001 and both RS copies, so the question was whether the recoding still helps in the new context, and whether the ranking that selected it still holds.

The re-run repeated the Window A protocol with the 99-nt RiboJ + RBS0 leader substituted for the 25-nt MF001 + A spacer leader. Everything from the start codon onward, the 93-nt SamyQ signal peptide and the first 90 nt of each CDS, was unchanged, so reported differences are attributable to the leader. Folding conditions were identical (ViennaRNA 2.7.2, 37 °C, MFE plus partition function, noLonelyPairs on, dangles = 2), and start+15, SD core and balance were defined as before. Because RiboJ is designed to cleave, the construct has two states and both were folded: the pre-cleavage transcript including the RS (310 nt) and the mature mRNA after excision and cleavage (282 nt).

ResultsKey results4 findings
  • Changing the leader opened the SD core on its own. The wild-type SD core improved for three of the four constructs simply by changing the leader: AmilCP by 8.8× (0.056 to 0.496), BoPep4 by 5.7× (0.067 to 0.378) and ACCD by 4.3× (0.088 to 0.378). Across the four cassettes the SD core rises from 0.056 to 0.357 behind MF001 to 0.301 to 0.496 behind RiboJ + RBS0.
  • The occlusion came from the recombination site. MF001 folded on its own, without the RS in front of it, returns an SD core of 0.553 to 0.741. The 53-nt MF001 leader folds into one long duplex in which the 5′ arm of the RS pairs with the 3′ half of MF001, leaving the SD core almost completely base-paired at P(unpaired) = 0.055. The RiboJ + RBS0 leader leaves it at 0.726 and the published RiboJ orientation at 0.380. Both RiboJ leaders are more stable overall, MFE −22.7 and −23.3 kcal/mol against −9.0 for MF001, so the improvement comes from insulation and not from a stronger ribosome binding site.
  • Six of the eight ordered cassettes still beat their wild type. The balance score was higher than the wild type for six of the eight, for example AmilCP-v3-top1 (0.917 to 1.343) and ACCD-v3-top1 (0.884 to 1.062), but not for the two BoPep4 variants (0.986 to 0.937 and 0.926). The gain is smaller than the one predicted with MF001 and the RS, where AmilCP-v3-top1 had reached 0.734 to 1.636. start+15 moved in both directions and should not be reported as a uniform improvement.
  • RS sensitivity is largely resolved. Adding the RS changes the SD core by 0.441 on average with MF001 but only by 0.009 with RiboJ, which was the main limitation recorded for the earlier run.
ResultsProject3 findings
  • A ranking cannot be carried across a change in the 5′ UTR. A variant selected as best in one context is not guaranteed to improve on the wild type in another, which is what the two BoPep4 variants show. Any future leader change means recomputing the window, not reusing the table.
  • The bottleneck was partly of our own making. The structure Phases 1 to 3 recoded against was created in part by RS-MF001, and the swap to EEBD-RBS0, made for cloning reasons, removed it. The recoding still helps in the context that was built, but less than predicted and for a different reason than assumed.
  • The fixed cut-off is gone. Ho1, the benchmark from a well-expressed gene, moved from start+15 = 0.681 to 0.613 with RiboJ + RBS0, so the reference itself changes with the leader. 0.50 is no longer used as a cut-off and designs are compared by rank within one leader context.

Limitations. ViennaRNA diagnoses translation initiation only, and only for the processed mRNA, so a higher start+15 or SD core identifies a construct worth testing and does not predict yield. P(unpaired) is a proxy for ribosome accessibility and does not capture co-transcriptional folding, ribosome unwinding or RNase processing, so the ranking between designs is more reliable than any single value. It also does not capture secretion efficiency, SRP targeting, co-translational export, or degradation by extracytoplasmic HtrA/HtrB, which matters particularly for disordered proteins such as LEA14. The 50,000 variants are a sample and not an optimum, the cleavage position is assumed and not sourced, and the orientation finding should be confirmed against the source plasmid.
