Protein Design

BoPEP4 (orange), twenty-three residues, threaded onto the AtPEP1 backbone from the 5GR8 crystal structure and laid along the concave face of the PEPR1 receptor. The charged side chains picked out at each end are the contacts every engineered variant was judged against.

ReLeaf produces plant protectants in Bacillus subtilis, at the edge of a field. This section is the dry lab work that decided which protectants to produce and what to change about them: how the three candidates were chosen, how a peptide was turned into a docking problem the software could actually answer, what each design sprint returned, and where the simulations agreed with the scoring and where they did not.

On this page

The problem

Heat, drought and salinity damage a crop before a farmer can see it. Molecules that protect a plant through that stress exist and are described in the literature, but they are peptides and proteins: expensive to buy, awkward to store, and delivered as a finished product from somewhere else. ReLeaf makes them on site instead, which turns the question from which protectant works into which protectant a bacterium can make, export and keep intact.

That is a protein engineering problem with two constraints at once. The receptor has to recognise the molecule, and Bacillus subtilis has to translate it, push it through the secretion pathway, and leave it whole in a protease-rich supernatant. Every decision recorded here was taken against both.

Three protectants came out of the selection: BoPEP4, a twenty-three residue plant elicitor peptide from Brassica oleracea that signals drought stress through the PEPR1 receptor; LEA14, a folded late embryogenesis abundant protein that stabilises other proteins under osmotic stress; and ACC deaminase, an enzyme that lowers stress-induced ethylene and covers the heat stress the other two do not. BoPEP4 took most of the modelling effort, because it is the only one of the three with a solved receptor complex to work from.

What the section found

Four results carried decisions into the wet lab. Each links to the page that shows the working.

1 to 6 The residues that can go

BoPEP4 opens with an uncharged GILIGS segment that makes no receptor contact and is conserved nowhere. Removing it leaves the docking score flat; the score falls away only at deeper cuts. Over that plateau the peptide gets shorter at no cost. See the ladder.

10 of 23 Identity with AtPEP1, all of it C-terminal

BoPEP4 and AtPEP1 are the same length and align without gaps, so the crystal structure of one can be read onto the other residue by residue. Every identical position falls in the contact region, and the conserved PEP motif at 15 to 23 is intact. See the alignment.

K18R, K8Q Two substitutions, two different purposes

Both restore the AtPEP1 residue at that position. K18R targets the receptor anchor, where the alanine scan says the position is load-bearing. K8Q removes a positive charge to make the peptide harder for a protease to cut, which is a secretion argument rather than a binding one. See the sprint.

pH 5.5 The apoplast changes the answer

Protonating three histidines for the acidity the peptide actually meets outside the plant cell forms a new contact in nearly every frame of the simulation, and the same pair already exists in the benchmark at neutral pH. The same change applied to a truncated peptide collapses its anchor instead. See the runs.

One standing limit

None of this has been applied to a plant. Every statement in this section is about a molecule, a model or a sequence, and the docking score that ranks them is a weighted sum in arbitrary units that did not track structural accuracy in our own validation. The section says so in the students' words on Triage and Sprints and Results, and nothing here rests on a score difference alone.

Future work

Two things the layout document lists and the section has not done.

Co-immunoprecipitation

Everything about the three-body complex rests on structural compatibility: BAK1 can be placed against a PEPR1 and BoPEP4 model without clashing. That is geometry. It becomes a claim about association only with a physical pull-down of a tagged receptor from treated tissue.

A receptor abundance model

A docking model treats the receptor as present. A field does not. Reading PEPR1 transcript abundance out of ePlant [4] would let the section ask at what applied concentration the peptide stops being limited by its own affinity and starts being limited by how much receptor is on a leaf, which is the number the bioreactor would have to deliver. Nothing has been extracted yet.
