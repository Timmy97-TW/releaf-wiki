Score and clustering

The HADDOCK score is a weighted sum of energy terms computed on a refined model: van der Waals and electrostatic interaction energies, a desolvation term, and a penalty for violating the restraints the run was given [1, 2]. It is reported in arbitrary units and lower is better. It was built to separate near-native models from wrong ones inside a single docking run, and the people who wrote it have published on how far it can be pushed beyond that [5].

Clustering is the second half of the step and it is meant to carry most of the weight. Models that are structurally similar are grouped, each group gets a mean score over its best members with a spread, and the unit of analysis becomes the cluster and not any individual structure. A cluster that is large, tight and well separated from the next one is a result. One structure with a good number is not.

One further tool sits on the workflow chart and carries no claim anywhere on this wiki. PRODIGY estimates binding affinity from the contacts in a complex [10], which makes it the obvious candidate to do the ranking job the HADDOCK score could not. It has not been run on any model in this section.

To fix · P7

The methodology text describes clustering as though it had discriminated between binding modes, and in the runs that produced most of this project's numbers it did not.

  • As written: “Protein Design Writing.docx”, Binary Docking, which states that models were clustered according to structural similarity and that structurally plausible clusters were retained for comparison.
  • As audited: the later campaigns produced a single cluster containing every structure, so clustering carried no information in them. Recorded in the 30 July audit and on Peptide Design.

The likely cause is on Assembly: forty rigid-body models and a loose contact-similarity cutoff [9]. Rerunning one representative complex at the recommended sampling with a geometric cutoff would settle whether the single cluster is a property of the system or of the settings, and that is a single run, not a campaign.

Works. Explaining what the HADDOCK score was built to do, separate near-native models inside one run, before explaining what it cannot do, is the fair order and makes the later retraction read as method rather than apology. Naming PRODIGY as the obvious candidate that was never run is a useful admission.

Fix P7 names a single re-run that would settle whether the one-cluster result is a property of the system or of the settings, so schedule it: one representative complex at recommended sampling with a geometric cutoff. Say who owns it and roughly what it costs in compute, because a fix that looks expensive gets deferred.

Limits of the score

The scoring step is where a reader expects the answer to come from. On this project it could not give one, and there are three independent reasons, each of which would be enough on its own.

Seed-to-seed spread

Docking is stochastic. Run the same complex again with a different random seed and the reported score changes. On this pipeline that seed-to-seed movement is between 13 and 36 units. Every score difference this project ever quoted as meaningful is smaller than that. A difference inside the noise of the method is not a finding, whatever direction it points in, so no comparison of two HADDOCK scores appears anywhere on this wiki as evidence.

Circular runs

Of 121 docking runs on record, 113 started from the pose they were meant to be testing and took their restraints from measurements on that same pose. That operation relaxes a model under a force field, which is a real and useful thing to do, and it cannot reject the model it started from. Results from those runs are evidence about geometry under a force field. They are not independent confirmation of anything, and the claim that they confirmed the co-folding predictions was withdrawn on 30 July. The provenance is set out on Restraints.

The activity benchmark

The project did run the test that would settle whether its scoring could rank designs, which is the part of this story worth being proud of. Pearce et al. measured half-maximal activity for an alanine scan on this peptide family [3]. Sixteen of those analogues share the same 9 to 23 scaffold, so length and buried surface are near-constant and the substitution is the only large variable. Docking all sixteen against their measured activities is about as clean a test of a scoring function as a project of this size can build.

The rank correlation came back at about +0.05 with p > 0.8. That is no relationship at all. An earlier internal report gave +0.237, and the difference is that the earlier figure included the wild-type reference point inside its own correlation, which inflates it. The corrected number stands, the claim that our docking reproduces the Pearce activity trend is retracted, and the full benchmark with its chart is on Peptide Design.

Two further observations came out of the project's own tables with no new computation. In the founding campaign's mutant table every point mutant scores better than wild type, and the two mutants the literature calls most damaging score best of all. And on the wild-type run the top-ranked cluster is a non-native pose 5.80 Å from the crystal. Both point the same way.

What the score is still good for

Two things, and they are worth keeping. Inside a single run it separates near-native models from wrong ones, which is what it was designed for, and the positive control demonstrates it: re-docking AtPep1 into its own receptor returns the crystal pose at 0.77 Å, top ranked, with a 35-unit gap to the first non-native pose. That gap is a within-run separation and is not a comparison between designs. The other use is as a cheap filter for models that are physically broken. Neither of those is a ranking of sequences by affinity, and neither is reported as one.

Review · Limits of the score

Works. Three independent reasons, each sufficient on its own, and the third one is a test the team designed and then reported failing: sixteen equal-length Pearce analogues against measured activity gave a rank correlation of about +0.05 at p > 0.8. Finding that an earlier internal +0.237 was inflated by including the wild-type reference in its own correlation, and correcting it in public, is the strongest evidence of scientific integrity in this section.

Fix. State how many seeds the 13 to 36 unit spread was measured over and on which complexes, because that number now carries the whole section. The benchmark deserves its chart on this page too, or at least the three dead analogues' ranks, so a reader does not have to leave for Peptide Design to see the failure.

CAPRI criteria

CAPRI is the community assessment that sets the standard vocabulary for how close a predicted complex is to a reference structure [4]. It compares on three measures at once: the fraction of native contacts recovered, the root-mean-square deviation over the interface, and the deviation of the ligand after superposing the receptor. Those three together sort a prediction into incorrect, acceptable, medium or high quality. The value of the scheme here is that it is about distance from a known answer and contains no energy term, so it is untouched by everything in section 2.

It can only be applied where a reference structure exists, which on this project means the AtPep1 re-docking control against 5GR8. That control lands in the top band.

Declared gap

The control is reported through one number, a ligand deviation of 0.77 Å, and CAPRI wants three. The fraction of native contacts recovered and the interface deviation are not recorded, and both come out of the same comparison at no extra cost. The section needs all three for the positive control, and a sentence saying explicitly that no BoPep4 model can be assessed this way, because there is no BoPep4 crystal to assess it against.

Review · CAPRI criteria

Works. Choosing a criterion with no energy term, so it is untouched by the scoring problems above, shows the team understands what its own limitation does and does not contaminate.

Fix. The gap is right that one number is not CAPRI. Compute the fraction of native contacts and the interface deviation for the AtPep1 control from the same comparison and report all three, and add the sentence saying no BoPep4 model can be assessed this way because no BoPep4 crystal exists.

Interface analysis

PDBePISA takes a structure and describes its interfaces in physical terms: buried surface area, the residues on each side, hydrogen bonds and salt bridges, and an estimate of how much of the assembly is likely to be real rather than a crystal-packing artefact [6]. On this pipeline it is the measurement that does the work the score cannot. Buried area per residue and a list of contacting residues are geometric quantities, they are stable across seeds, and the two results in this section that survived every audit are both of that kind: the tandem sweep on Generate, and the finding that residues 1 to 6 bury zero area on either receptor.

The 5GR8 interface list is the reference the modelled interfaces are compared against, because it is the one interface in this system that was measured rather than predicted.

Declared gap

The 5GR8 interface list is named in the outline and is not in the source folder. It is a table anyone can generate in a few minutes, and the section cannot make its main argument without it: the PEPR1 residues contacting AtPep1 in the crystal, each with its buried area, and the hydrogen bonds and salt bridges across the interface. Put beside the same table for a BoPep4 model, it becomes the comparison this page is for.

Review · Interface analysis

Works. Identifying geometric measurements as the ones that survived every audit, and naming the two results that did, is a clean statement of what this pipeline can still be trusted for.

Fix. The 5GR8 interface list is the reference every comparison on this page depends on and it is not in the source folder. It takes minutes to generate, so generate it and put it on the page as a table with buried area per residue, hydrogen bonds and salt bridges.

Geometry validation

The checks above ask whether a model is right. This one asks something cruder and prior to it: whether the model is a physically possible protein. MolProbity scores all-atom contacts and covalent and dihedral geometry against high-resolution reference data, and returns clashes, rotamer outliers and a Ramachandran analysis [7]. A backbone conformation in a disallowed region of the Ramachandran plot is a structure the software built, not a structure a peptide would adopt.

This matters more for a docked flexible peptide than for a crystal structure. The peptide's backbone is rebuilt during refinement, so it is exactly the part of the model most able to end up in impossible geometry, and it is also the part every conclusion in this section depends on. The workflow chart describes the target as 5GR8-style verification: hold our models to the standard a deposited structure is held to, using the validation reports the wwPDB publishes.

Declared gap

No validation report has been run on any model in this section. This is the largest single gap on the page and the easiest to close, because it needs no new modelling: upload the canonical wild-type complex, the truncation lead and the ternary model, and record the clash score, the Ramachandran favoured and outlier percentages, and the rotamer outliers for each. If a model that carried a conclusion turns out to have outliers at the interface, that conclusion needs revisiting, and it is better to find that out here than after the freeze.

Review · Geometry validation

Works. Asking the crude prior question, whether the model is a physically possible protein, and pointing out that the peptide backbone is both the part most likely to be broken by refinement and the part every conclusion rests on, is a sharp observation.

Fix. No validation report has been run on any model. Run MolProbity on the canonical wild-type complex, the truncation lead and the ternary model, and record clash score, Ramachandran favoured and outliers, and rotamer outliers. If a model carrying a conclusion has interface outliers, that conclusion has to be revisited before the freeze rather than after it.

Literature check

The last filter is reading. A model is checked against what is already known about this receptor and this family, and where the two disagree the literature wins, because it rests on measurements and the model does not.

Three checks carried real weight this year. The anchor: Tang et al. show the C-terminal asparagine carboxylate held by an arginine in a bidentate salt bridge, 2.40 and 2.80 Å in the crystal, and it is the most buried residue of the peptide [8]. A model that does not reproduce that contact is wrong regardless of its score, and the residue is given priority in the restraint mapping for that reason. The base rate: three AtPep family members carry natural C-terminal extensions past that asparagine, two of them fail to bind PEPR1 at all, the third binds and cannot recruit the co-receptor, and truncating the extension restores activity in all three [8]. That published pattern, and not any run of ours, is what condemned the C-terminally tagged constructs on Packaging. The dead controls: Pearce measured which single substitutions destroy activity [3], which gives a table of answers the pipeline is supposed to already know, and using them that way is how the scoring failure was found.

The general form of the check is worth stating, because it is the most transferable thing on this page. Find a case where the answer is already published, run the pipeline on it blind, and compare. A pipeline that cannot recover a known answer is not ready to produce an unknown one.

Review · Literature check

Works. The three checks are concrete and each one changed a decision: the anchor rules out any model missing the Asn23 salt bridge, the base rate from three natural C-terminal extensions condemned the tagged constructs, and the dead controls exposed the scoring failure. The closing rule, that a pipeline which cannot recover a known answer is not ready to produce an unknown one, is the most reusable sentence in the section and belongs in the contribution page too.

Fix. Give the reference numbers for the three AtPep members carrying natural extensions, since Peptide Design names them as AtPep2, AtPep4 and AtPep5 and this page does not name them at all.
