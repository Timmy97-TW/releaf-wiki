Digital Twin

The twin and the asset it mirrors. The same system map on the operator interface and on the laptop beside the reactor: seven measured channels and six motor-driven valves on one Arduino Uno Q. Shown on demo data, which the interface says on its own title bar.

BR-01 is a hollow-fibre perfusion bioreactor that runs for days beside a field with nobody watching it. The twin is what lets it be measured anyway: a virtual copy that reads the loop continuously, says when a reading should not be trusted, forecasts where the batch is going, and carries a growth prior from a plate assay into the reactor.

On this page

Best Measurement

A bioreactor in a field cannot be sampled by hand. Everything this page reports is an attempt to measure a batch that nobody is standing next to — and to know, from the data alone, when that measurement has stopped being true.

The twin adds four dimensions to what a single OD reading gives you. Each is a measured number, and each links to the result behind it.

  1. Continuity. 434 hours of in-line OD600 on one batch with no sample drawn.
  2. Self-diagnosis. A reference beam separates “the culture changed” from “the instrument changed”, and gave 26.7 h of warning before the OD channel became unusable.
  3. Forecast. Recursive state estimation beats persistence by 41% at 2 h ahead under a rule fixed before the data were examined.
  4. Transfer between scales. A growth prior fitted on a 96-well plate predicts 24 h biomass to within 7%; the same data without the prior miss by 29 to 68%.

What iGEM asks for →

Also written against the Best Software Tool criteria
  • Validated by experimental work? Five measured results in Results; two of them (R3, R4) were scored under rules fixed in advance, and R5 on held-out arms. A forecast for the next batch is registered with a hash before the batch runs.
  • Useful to other projects? Four things another team can lift without our reactor, including the serial protocol and the trust layer.
  • Standards and data formats? Partly. The channel table, units and provenance travel with every export, which reaches the syntactic level of the interoperability framework of Satwekar et al. [5]; a published JSON schema would reach the semantic level and is must have. No SBOL or RFC is used: nothing on this page describes a genetic part.
  • Integration with external tools? One documented serial protocol in, one CSV and XLSX export out, and an Open-Meteo forecast pulled in. No package or public API is offered.
  • User-friendly? The interface, six tabs, bilingual, one file, no install. No usability session with an operator outside the build team has been recorded.
  • Documented for future groups? Contribution and Limits; the firmware ships with a desktop test harness that replays a run through the page's own parser with no board attached.

What the reactor measures now

Seven channels are read live and six valves move under software control. Six months ago the same rig had three pressure readings and no actuator at all.

Every value is tagged by its route from the process, following the process analytical technology idea that a number is only as good as how it was obtained [4, 5]. in-line is an instrument on the loop. soft is computed from measured values through a model. planned has no instrument behind it yet, and is drawn dashed on the interface for the same reason.

The interlocks run on the microcontroller, not in the browser, so no fault in a model or a page can drive the loop past a limit: trans-membrane pressure above 0.50 bar opens the pinch valve, and five seconds with no command from the interface opens it and turns the light off.

Strains on this page

Everything measured before 19 September is B. subtilis 168, the chassis the method was built on. The production strain is WB800N, in two versions: constitutive ACCD secretion, and ACCD under CcaS/CcaR green-light control. R5 and the registered forecast are WB800N.

Results

Five measured results. Three were scored on data the model had not seen; two are calibrations and say so.

  • 26.7 hWarning before the OD channel failed (R3)
  • 41%Forecast error cut against persistence, 2 h ahead (R4)
  • ≤ 7%Plate-to-reactor prior error on held-out arms (R5)
  • 1.9%NRMSE of the growth fit, against a 15% criterion (R5)

R1 · Module pressure drop calibrated

16 August 2026, water at 22 °C, eight pump settings, one point each, measured with the module fitted and with a jumper in its place. Both fit \(\Delta P = aQ + bQ^2\) through the origin: module R² 0.9954, residual SD 10.1 mbar; jumper R² 0.9950, SD 5.7 mbar. At 263 mL/min the net module drop is 0.096 bar, 1.03 times the Hagen–Poiseuille prediction for 11 open fibres — the agreement is what justifies using the model as a soft sensor for flow. No point was held out.

R2 · Photometer calibrated against a known dose calibrated

23 August 2026, 45 paired points from cumulative TiO₂ additions. The method matters more than the number: both instruments were scored against the known dose rather than against each other, which is what revealed that above 30 mg it is the benchtop BioDrop that loses linearity (R² 0.8501, SD 0.116), not the in-line photometer (R² 0.9901, SD 0.021). Below OD 1.2 the two agree: \(y = 1.018x - 0.031\), R² 0.995, residual SD 0.024 OD.

The calibration was then fitted a second way, as a Gaussian process, to check its intervals. The linear fit wins inside its own range (RMSE 0.0235 against 0.0254) and is kept. Extrapolated past OD 1.2, which is what happens in a batch nobody is watching, the linear error rises to 0.1715 while the GP holds 0.067, and the GP found its own validity ceiling at OD 1.55 without being told where the hand-drawn limit was. Its 95% band covered 88.9% of held-out points, short of the 95% it claims, so its intervals are too narrow and are reported as such. TiO₂ establishes linearity, not cell number.

R3 · Telling a sensor fault from a process event scored on unseen data

21 July to 8 August 2026, 434 hours of B. subtilis 168 at 22 °C, 2,671 valid rows, no sample drawn. Two axes are scored every 10 minutes from separate features: a sensor axis on the reference beam, which never passes through the culture, and a process axis on the specific growth rate. Both were trained on the first 120 h only, with the alarm limit fixed in advance at the 99.5th percentile of the training score, then scored on everything after.

The sensor alarm came at 216.3 h and the OD channel became unusable at 243.0 h: 26.7 hours of warning in which a controller would have known to stop trusting the channel. The rule most monitoring software uses, an alarm on any jump above 0.05 OD, fired on 23% of the instrument event and on 0.8% of the process event and could not say which was which.

What this is not. One run, one process event, one instrument event. It is a worked counterexample and a demonstration of the method, not a detection rate. A rate needs faults made on purpose, several of each kind, on batches the layer has not seen.

R4 · Forecasting the batch forward rules fixed in advance

102 hours of perfusion, 2 to 6 September 2026, B. subtilis 168, OD600 0.83 to 3.77. The protocol was written before the numbers were looked at: forecast origins every hour from 12 h, each forecast may use only data up to its origin, and every model is scored against the same observation. Persistence is the baseline a model has to beat to be worth its complexity.

HorizonPersistence RMSEKalman RMSEImprovement

A two-state Kalman filter on level and slope beats persistence at every horizon, and the margin narrows as the horizon lengthens, which is the expected shape. Two of the three simpler alternatives (a global linear fit, an exponential fit) were worse than persistence at short horizons, so the comparison is not a straw man.

R5 · Carrying a growth prior from plate to reactor held-out arms

19 September 2026, WB800N on the light-programmable array, six light arms, five time points to 24 h. A logistic model was chosen over logistic-with-lag by AICc and fits at NRMSE 1.9% (R² 0.997), against the 15% acceptance criterion used for bioprocess models [3]: \(r = 0.299 \pm 0.017\;\mathrm{h^{-1}}\) (doubling time 139 min), \(K = 1.005\), \(X_0 = 0.0235\).

Two things come out of it. First, green induction costs no growth: r spans 0.279 to 0.317 h⁻¹ across all six arms, within one standard error of each other, and the highest-green arm reaches the highest plateau. Second, and this is the measurement claim, the fit becomes a prior that can be handed to another vessel. Holding out one arm at a time and forecasting its 24 h biomass from data up to 8 h only:

Error at most 7% with the prior, 29 to 68% without it, from the same eight hours of data. This is the plate acting as a scale-down model for the reactor, which is how process transfer is done in biomanufacturing [3], and it is the reason the next batch can be predicted before it is run.

What this is not. The six arms are biologically near-identical, so this measures reproducibility across wells, not prediction under a new condition. It is the right shape of test and not yet a strong one. The run has no sample between 8 and 24 h, where the inflection falls, so K is pinned by one time point.

The forecast that tests it, registered before the batch

The prior from R5 predicts a batch that has not been run. The prediction was written, hashed and archived on 1 October; the batch runs on 2 October.

Every result above is a calibration or a retrospective analysis of data already in hand. The honest test of a twin is a prospective one, so the first reactor run of WB800N with constitutive ACCD secretion is being used as exactly that. The forecast file is prereg_forecast_20261002.json, SHA-256 bceda7c0…6e0388fc, and it may not be edited after the run.

What is deliberately not predicted. Only r transfers. \(K\) and \(X_0\) were fitted on the plate-reader scale in a 150 µL well, and the reactor reads a 2 mm in-line flow cuvette with no cross-calibration between them yet, so they are excluded from the primary endpoint rather than quietly rescaled. The LPA run's temperature was not recorded, and the team's 37 °C shake-flask fit is about four times faster, so a reactor batch run at 37 °C falls outside this prior and the primary criterion does not apply to it.

A failure of the rate with a pass of the shape means the curve is right and the rate is not, and temperature is the first place to look. A failure of both means the prior does not cross from plate to reactor — which is itself a result, and will be reported as one.

The same batch carries the first ACC deaminase activity assay, which turns the product from an unmeasurable quantity into a critical quality attribute with a number against it. Quantification by ELISA follows on the CcaS/CcaR version once the western blot band is strong enough.

Must have, yet to have

The gaps that change what may be claimed, named here rather than left for a judge to find. Each one says what it unlocks.

Two of these are cheap and change what the page may say: a temperature probe and an OD cross-calibration together would let the whole growth prior transfer, not just its rate.

The interface

One HTML file, no dependencies, no install, English and Traditional Chinese. It runs on demo data in the frame below; attach a board and the same page goes live.

Six tabs, each answering one question an operator actually has:

TodayWhat to do right now, in one sentence, with the four headline channels and the switches under them
System mapThe rig as a live P&ID: every tag reads its own value, and valves and light are tapped here
Growth twinMeasured biomass against the forecast and its band, the model parameters, and where the prior came from
WeatherField forecast and crop-stress risk — the demand side that decides when protectant is needed
DataTrends, the batch record, the action log, and CSV and XLSX export with units and provenance in every header
SetupConnection, calibration, safety rules and twin settings

The link is Web Serial over USB at 115 200 baud, so it needs Chrome or Edge and a page served over HTTP. The board sends process values once a second; the page sends its command frame every two seconds as a heartbeat, and the firmware opens the valve and kills the light if that heartbeat stops. Firmware and page are versioned together, and the firmware ships with a desktop harness that compiles the sketch, replays its output through the page's own parser and checks the result — no board and no browser needed.

On the rig today

The 2 October batch is being recorded by the team's own Arduino App Lab rig application, which is the build the hardware sub-team has been running all season. This interface takes over on the following batch, when the firmware is flashed to the matching protocol.

Limits

  • The loop is not closed. Valves move under software control; every decision about the culture still goes through a person. In the maturity terms of the literature this is a digital shadow with actuation available, not yet a closed-loop twin [1, 2].
  • n = 1 almost everywhere. One long run behind R3, one perfusion run behind R4, one plate behind R5.
  • Two chassis. R1 to R4 are B. subtilis 168; the production strain is WB800N. Whether the method transfers with the strain is the point of the 2 October batch.
  • 37 °C fits, 22 °C operation. The fast growth parameters come from 37 °C; the field unit runs near ambient. Transfer is a test, not an assumption.
  • Beer–Lambert at scale. The photometer is linear only to about OD 1.2 in a 2 mm cell, and induction light will be attenuated by a dense culture in a way nothing on the rig measures.
  • Trans-membrane pressure is below the resolution of the transducers at the operating point, so lumen ΔP is used as the hydraulic observable instead.
  • The 120 to 160 h event is unexplained. The trust layer called it biological and was right to; nobody knows what the culture did.

For other teams

Four things that work without our reactor.

  1. The two-axis trust layer. A reference channel that never sees the sample, scored separately from the process, trained on the healthy start of a run with its threshold fixed before scoring. Any team with an optical sensor can run it, and the 434 h batch is included as a test case.
  2. Calibrate against a known dose, not against another instrument. Cumulative TiO₂ additions score both instruments against truth. It cost us one afternoon and told us our in-line photometer was the more linear of the two — the opposite of what we assumed.
  3. A plate assay as a scale-down model. Fit growth on a light-programmable array, hand the posterior to the reactor as a prior, and register the prediction before the batch. The method is the contribution; the parameters are ours.
  4. The serial protocol and its test harness. A documented two-line ASCII protocol with a heartbeat watchdog, interlocks on the microcontroller rather than in the browser, and a desktop harness that tests firmware and page together with no hardware attached.

Code and data are in the team repository; the mirror to gitlab.igem.org/2026/software-tools/ is made before the wiki freeze. The reactor itself is on Hardware, the growth and transport models on Math Model, and the assays on Measurement.
