Digital Twin

Abstract

ReLEAF's digital twin software aims to help the bioreactor run for weeks with minimal manual instruction, automated control and risk evaluation. It is an AIoT system that connects the bioreactor's sensors and valves, alongside local sensors and online forecasts, to models that learn from new data.

The digital twin reads optical density, pH, pressure and flow from the reactor. All live. We calibrated its photometer against TiO₂ and SiO₂ and ran B. subtilis in the reactor four times, the longest for 434 hours. Replayed over that run, the twin flagged the photometer 26.7 hours before its reading failed. Green light, dissolved oxygen and the field sensors have been tested separately and are not yet integrated. The future version adds leaf temperature, leaf wetness and the weather forecast into the full loop, designed but not tested yet. Its Ask tab lets a farmer ask about the reactor in plain language and act on the answer, without expertise in biomanufacturing.

Digital Twin and ReLEAF's System

A digital model represents a physical system but is updated by hand. A digital shadow receives live data automatically, but changes back to the system still need a person. A digital twin closes the loop: data flows automatically from the system to the model and back [1, 2]. In the field of biomanufacturing, a digital twin can not only help monitor live data, it also acts as a second simulated world that runs the exact same work as the physical world, but ahead, giving the users risk assessments and action suggestions before things actually happen.

Our bioreactor is meant to run for weeks beside farmland, far from laboratories where results can be analyzed precisely. Because no one will always be there to take samples or interpret every reading, we developed a digital twin that can monitor live readings from sensors and weather forecasts, acts as the decision-making “brain” of the system, and allow user to loop back the result to predict more accurate projections and risks. The next version adds an Ask tab, so that a farmer with no expertise in biomanufacturing can ask what the reactor is doing in plain language and act on the answer.

ReLEAF is an AIoT system: the sensors and valves above are wired to a board that logs every reading, and models learn from those readings. An autoencoder our team trained on the healthy first 120 hours of the 434-hour run flagged a failing photometer 26.7 hours before its reading became unusable (R2). The models have so far run on recorded data after each run, and a person still makes every decision. The next Digital Twin joins them to the live loop and adds the Ask tab, where a farmer with no expertise in biomanufacturing can ask about the reactor in plain language and act on the answer.

Photometer calibration against TiO₂ and SiO₂

TiO₂, 23 August

On 23 August 2026, cumulative TiO₂ additions into a stirred 400 mL loop gave 45 paired points between the in-line photometer and the BioDrop spectrophotometer (Figure 2). Up to an optical density of 1.2 the photometer follows the BioDrop with slope 1.018 and R² 0.995.

SiO₂, 10 October

On 10 October 2026 the same loop ran the calibration iGEM's protocol actually asks for: silica in 2.5 mg steps from 2.5 to 100 mg in 500 mL of 1% SDS, read at every step on the BioDrop and on both photometer builds, and logged in-line every five seconds for 4 h 27 min (Figure 4).

The run carries its own check. At 137.6 min the suspension was diluted and then restored, which tests proportionality without the dose being known: 2.3610 before (SD 0.0033, 29 readings), 0.7943 after (SD 0.0017, 38), and 2.3665 restored (SD 0.0157, 16). The ratio across the dilution is 2.97 one way and 2.98 the other, and the restored reading returns within 0.2% of the original. A reading of 2.36 therefore carries the same proportional meaning as one of 0.79, which is the property a calibration needs for the twin to compare one reading against another.

The leaf and air sensors on the bench

On 10 October 2026 the field sensor rig was tested at three sites with stable air temperature: an incubator at 22 °C, a room at 26 °C and a stairwell at 26.4 °C. The rig is an ESP32 reading two sensors: an SHT31 for air temperature and humidity, and an SN-3001-YM-N01 for leaf temperature and leaf wetness over RS485. Both sensors were held in air beside each other, with no leaf. Each site had three five-minute runs logged every five seconds, giving 540 paired readings.

The SHT31 matched the nominal temperature of each site. Sharing the same air, the two sensors should agree, yet the leaf sensor read 1.2 to 1.9 °C warmer once settled, and the size of that gap changed from site to site, so one fixed offset will not correct it.

Leaf wetness read 0.0% in every sample. The sensor was never wetted, so this test cannot yet show whether the channel responds.

In the field the leaf sensor will be on a plant and the two readings are expected to differ: the leaf minus air temperature difference is one of the signals of water stress. A bias of 1.2 to 1.9 °C is large enough to hide that signal or invent one, so it has to be measured and removed before the twin acts on it.

Module pressure drop

On 16 August 2026 we pumped water at 22 °C through the reactor at eight pump speeds and measured the pressure lost along the way, once with the hollow fibre module in place and once with a plain tube where the module sits (Figure 10). At 263 mL/min, the flow we run at, the module loses 0.096 bar, 1.03 times what the textbook formula predicts for 11 open fibres, so none are blocked. At that flow the force of the liquid on the fibre walls, calculated once from the pressure sensors and once from the flow meter, agrees to 2.8%, so each sensor can check the other.

Batch forecasting

We ran 102 hours of perfusion, 2 to 6 September 2026, with B. subtilis 168 secreting ACC deaminase constitutively; OD600 rose from 0.84 to 3.77. We compared Kalman filter, local linear, global linear and exponential forecasts against persistence, which predicts no change. A forecast was made every hour from 12 h, each using only data up to that point.

Most of the run is above the linear limit. The 12-hour median OD rose from 1.06 to 3.75, so R5 forecasts the photometer reading, not biomass. The model comparison still holds, because every model forecasts the same signal.

Global linear weighs early data as much as recent data, so it points the wrong way when growth changes speed. Exponential assumes growth keeps accelerating, which it does not over 102 hours of perfusion, so it overshoots. Local linear follows the recent level and slope; the Kalman filter also weighs each reading against the noise, so it follows real changes without chasing scatter.

Light plate

On 19 September 2026, B. subtilis WB800 grew in light-programmable array wells under six light arms, read on a plate reader at five time points to 24 h. A logistic model beat logistic-with-lag by AICc and fits at NRMSE 1.9% (R² 0.997), against the 15% criterion for bioprocess models [3]: \(r = 0.299 \pm 0.017\;\mathrm{h^{-1}}\) (doubling time 139 min), \(K = 1.005\), \(X_0 = 0.0235\).

To test the fit as a prior, we left out each arm in turn, fitted the other five, and predicted its 24 h OD from its first 8 hours, with and without the prior.

The Software

The Digital Twin

It has three parts. Firmware v3.10.9 (sketch/sketch.ino) runs on the Uno Q's microcontroller, reads the instruments and holds every interlock. Backend v4.12 (python/main.py) runs on the board's Linux side, computes derived values and alarms, writes the log to the board's disk and serves the page on port 7000. Interface v4.13 (assets/index.html) is one file in the browser, which displays and commands and protects nothing. The firmware pushes readings to the backend and the backend calls the firmware for commands, over the board's Bridge link.

The next Digital Twin

The digital twin we ran in experiments has not integrated leaf wetness data and weather forecast predictions into the green light trigger decision, due to time constraints. We did develop this new digital twin for future testing that has six tabs: what to do now, a live dashboard, the growth twin against its forecast band, weather and blast risk, the batch record, and setup. And a self learning function that allows users to record every run's results and data for more precise future predictions.

In the Ask tab, the farmer users can paste an API key from their own AI provider (Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini) and can then ask about the screen in plain language, such as whether the reactor is fine or why the green light is still off. With each question the assistant receives the readings, warnings and advice on screen. It can explain them and cannot move a valve or the light. The key stays in the browser tab and is not saved. On the iGEM wiki the chat is switched off, because the wiki allows no request to an outside server.

Current limits

Four things are not yet in the twin. Loop temperature: the probe is fitted but has not been answering, so the runs used a temperature typed in by the operator. Dissolved oxygen: we had trouble calibrating the sensor and neither probe can be autoclaved, so it is not on the reactor. Green light on the reactor: induction was tested on the light-programmable array and is not built into the reactor, so the twin cannot yet switch it on. Induction light in the culture: it is not measured, and a dense culture will dim it by an amount nothing on the reactor reads.
