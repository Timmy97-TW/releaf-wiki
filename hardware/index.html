Hardware

Three instruments
we had to build ourselves

RELEAF makes plant protectants on demand, in the field, from engineered Bacillus subtilis. To do that we had to know two things continuously: how much culture is there, and how does light drive it. Nothing we could afford did either, so we built our own.

01

Photometer

In-line optical density

Reads OD600 straight from the culture as it flows, through a 0.2 mm path, without ever removing a sample. A beam split two ways cancels LED drift, so it can run for weeks unattended.

  • 336 h continuous
  • 0 samples withdrawn
  • 15 components
Take it apart →
02

DiOPAL

Dual-wavelength LED array

Green light switches protectant production on, red switches it off. DiOPAL holds twenty-four cultures under six defined light conditions at once, so a single run answers how wavelength and intensity drive expression.

  • 24 LEDs
  • 6 conditions
  • 4 replicates each

03

Bioreactor

The vessel itself

The hollow-fiber reactor the other two were built for. Culture grows inside the fibers; the protectant diffuses out, the bacteria do not.

  • Hollow-fiber lumen loop
  • 0 colonies, shell side

designed to read
01 Photometer

Sits in the return leg, reading without sampling

The two have not yet run together. The 336 h continuous record was taken on a simplified circulation loop with no hollow‑fiber module fitted, and the integrated configuration is still ahead of us.

02 DiOPAL

Sits outside the reactor loop entirely

DiOPAL never touches the reactor. It answers the question upstream: what light, at what intensity, produces the most protectant. The reactor then applies that answer.
