LPA: Light Plate Apparatus

The LPA is a 24‑culture LED array designed to test how wavelength and intensity affect our CcaS/CcaR optogenetic system in engineered Bacillus subtilis WB800. Green and red LEDs are arranged across six light conditions, four replicate cultures each.

Purpose

Reliable testing of the CcaS/CcaR circuit requires controlled, repeatable illumination. The LPA exposes 24 cultures to defined green and red light conditions while minimizing variation between wells.

Dual-Wavelength Array

Intensity is set by pulse‑width modulation (PWM) from an Arduino, one metal‑oxide‑semiconductor field‑effect transistor (MOSFET) per intensity tier, and is fixed for the whole run.

Design

Prototype 1 carried one green and one red LED per column, uncalibrated until the layout was shown to work.

Build

Green and red LEDs sat in a 3D‑printed holder, wired to a breadboard for direct control.

Test

The green and red channels switched independently.

Learn

The layout was feasible, but two sources of variation remained:

  • LEDs at the same setting gave different intensities, so the tiers were not consistent across the array.
  • Ambient light reached the open array, with the largest effect at the low tier.

Prototype 2: Matched Output & Light‑Shielding

  1. DDesignTiers from matched LEDs
  2. BBuildEach LED measured, then rewired
  3. TTest40 LEDs sorted into matched groups

Design

We measured every LED, grouped similar LEDs into matched groups per wavelength for the low, mid and high columns, and shielded the array from ambient light.

Build

Each LED was driven at 5 V, read on a lux meter in a printed sensor fixture at the same distance and orientation, and tagged by row and column (A to D, 1 to 5).

Test

All 20 green and 20 red LEDs were sorted into matched groups of four, rewired as six channels.

Learn

Before sorting, green output varied by 26% and red by 22%. After matching, each matched group differed by about 0.4–1.7% (green) and 1.5–1.8% (red). The groups stayed too close to form three tiers, especially green, whose high group, built from the LEDs left over, was less consistent. The tiers are therefore set by PWM duty cycle. Still open: exposed electronics, intensity set by hand, no fixed tube position.

Design

Matched groups and shielding are kept; four changes follow.

01 · LPA_Box

Enclosed electronicsA printed frame houses the wiring and perfboard.

02 · Arduino code

Intensity in softwareThe three intensity tiers are PWM duty cycles set in code.

03 · Left_C, Right_C

Tool‑free assemblyPrinted sliders let the LPA come apart without tools.

04 · Tube_Holder

Fixed tube positionEach culture sits at the same distance from its LED.

Build

The MOSFET stage moved from a breadboard beside the array to a perfboard beneath the LED holder, inside the enclosure.

Test

Overnight on a shaker, a bottom‑plate post broke off and several wires came loose; the shaker frequency was likely too high for the print. Light also leaked through the white tube holder into adjacent wells, affecting that run’s data.

Learn

Base reinforcement at four points

All four screw positions are now used, up from two, and every solder joint is reinforced. The rebuilt base has not been re‑tested on a shaker.

Light blocking between wells

The tube holder is now black, with walls raised from 2 loops to 4.

Final Design

The final LPA is a reinforced base plate, housing, internal perfboard, sliders and a black tube holder.

Limitations

Fixed dose, no feedback

The dose is set before the run and does not respond to the culture; closed‑loop control would require added feedback.

Wavelengths assumed, not measured

The wavelengths are assumed from published CcaS/CcaR values; our own LEDs have not been characterized.

No correction for drift

Output drift as LEDs warm up or age is not corrected.

Photometric units

Lux is weighted for human vision, so green and red readings are not equivalent biological doses; photon flux is required.

Proof of Concept

No end‑to‑end culture run under the LPA is on record yet.

The LPA was built for one test, at one wavelength across the three tiers: whether protectant production changes with the intensity applied.

References

Illuminating a plate of cultures from below with per‑well control is the Light Plate Apparatus pattern from the optogenetics literature; our LPA follows it and is named after it. We added the dual‑wavelength column layout, LEDs matched by measured output, and an enclosure sized for our tubes.

The CcaS/CcaR green/red switch is also from the literature. Our dose target comes from the Castillo‑Hair characterization of that system, whose half‑maximal point is 4.66 µmol m−2 s−1.
