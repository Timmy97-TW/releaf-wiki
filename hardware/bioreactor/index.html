Technical record

ReLeaf Perfusion
Bioreactor

The production stage of RELEAF. Engineered B. subtilis grows in a stirred reservoir and is perfused continuously through a PES hollow‑fiber membrane. Protectant is intended to diffuse across the fiber wall into the shell side and be drawn off to the field; the bacteria are retained on the lumen side for the life of the run.

This record is written so that the device can be rebuilt independently from the information given here. It does not meet that standard yet — suppliers, costs and several specifications are still outstanding. Values, parts and statements that are not yet established are marked as pending rather than estimated.

await measured values. Two DBTL cycles are complete and reported here; the third is in progress.
Part1

Overview

1.1 What the Device Has to Do

RELEAF asks a bacterium to manufacture a plant protectant on demand, in the field, next to the crop that needs it. The protectant has to get into the soil; the bacteria that make it must not. That is the whole design constraint, and the biology cannot solve it alone.

0colonies

No colony formation on LB agar plated from shell‑side medium, cycle 1 — one plating. The separation the design depends on was tested directly rather than assumed — see .

The answer is a perfusion loop built around a hollow‑fiber membrane. Culture is pumped from a stirred medium reservoir through the fiber lumens and back to the reservoir, continuously. The fibers are permeable to the secreted protectant but not to B. subtilis, so the shell space surrounding the fibers should fill with product while staying free of cells. The cell‑free half of that was tested directly; the transfer of protectant across the wall has not yet been measured, and remains an open parameter (5.4). That shell‑side fluid is what leaves the machine.

Because the culture does not have to be opened to be measured, optical density is read in‑line by the photometer sitting in the feed leg, between the pump and the membrane inlet. The loop is designed to stay closed for the whole run and still yield a continuous OD trace; the photometer and the membrane module have not yet been run together (see the score card in Part 5). The Luer valves on the loop are used for bleed and for the occasional reference reading, such as the reservoir values in cycles 1 and 2.

1.2 The Two Circuits

Everything in the record below refers to one of two fluid paths. They meet only at the membrane wall.

Lumen side

Culture loop

Medium reservoir → peristaltic pump → in‑line photometer → fiber lumens → back to the reservoir. Contains the organism. Closed for the duration of the run; the only access to it is the dual Luer valve listed in the bill of materials, used for bleed and sampling.

  • Reservoir 500 mL vessel, 300 mL working volume
  • Inoculum 20 mL overnight B. subtilis culture
  • Flow rate 94 mL/min
  • Stirring 200 rpm magnetic (added cycle 2)
Shell side

Protectant harvest

The space around the fibers. Fills by diffusion across the fiber wall, is drawn off through the shell ports, and carries protectant to the delivery stage. The 0.2 µm rating passes the protectant freely — ACC deaminase is roughly 36 kDa — while still stopping whole cells; it does not stop everything, and what that costs in containment terms is set out in 5.2.

  • Cartridge hold‑up ~200 mL confirm
  • Membrane PES hollow fiber
  • Pore rating 0.2 µm, 150 cm² membrane area, eight fibers confirm
  • Delivery to soil or hydroponic line
Needs

A measured rejection or transmission figure for the protectant across the fitted 0.2 µm membrane. The pore is a size filter, not a molecular‑weight cut‑off (see 5.2), so the separation margin has to be measured rather than read off a rating. Also needed: the reasons for the cartridge as fitted — PES rather than another fiber material, and 150 cm² across eight fibers for a 300 mL working volume.

Architecture

2.1 System Diagram

The full setup as designed, including the sensing and delivery stages that sit either side of the reactor itself.

01 / 08 Culture in the reservoir
  1. 01

    Culture in the reservoir

    A stirred 300 mL working volume. Expression is induced by a green light signal driven by the AIoT stress predictor. A magnetic stir bar runs at 200 rpm — added in cycle 2, after sedimentation was diagnosed.

  2. 02

    Read without opening the loop

    The peristaltic pump draws culture from the reservoir outlet, past the Luer sampling valve, and drives it at 94 mL/min through the in‑line photometer and into the top of the membrane column. The pump acts on the outside of the tubing only, so the fluid path is not breached at the pump head.

  3. 03

    The only crossing

    A hollow-fiber membrane splits the system in two. This wall is the single place the two circuits touch, and the organism is retained on the lumen side.

  4. 04

    Nothing leaves the culture loop

    Culture returns down the lumens and through a pinch valve to the reservoir. Nothing leaves the culture loop except through the membrane wall.

  5. 05

    Harvest, on the shell side

    A second pump moves shell-side fluid out of the column. Anything in that stream crossed the fiber wall, whose 0.2 µm pore is smaller than a B. subtilis cell; shell‑side medium plated in cycle 1 produced no colonies (5.2). The stream is cell‑free for as long as the cartridge stays intact.

  6. 06

    Sensing the output

    Shell‑side fluid passes an optical sensor and a pH sensor on its way out, reading the product stream after the membrane. Culture density itself is read upstream, in‑line in the culture loop by the photometer.

  7. 07

    Regulate, then deliver

    Shell-side fluid passes a pH regulator on its way out to the delivery stages.

  8. 08

    One closed loop, one open path

    Culture circulates and never leaves. What crosses the membrane wall and goes to the field is the protectant together with anything else below the 0.2 µm pore — medium components, vesicles, debris (5.2). Whole cells are what the wall is there to stop. Containment is physical at the membrane wall; the Luer sampling valves are the one place procedure still matters.

2.2 Flow Path and Instrumentation

Perfusion

Peristaltic pump, 94 mL/min, single continuous loop. The pump acts on the outside of the tubing only, so the fluid path is not breached at the pump head. Fluid drawn off the shell side is made up across the membrane from the culture loop, and no medium feed is fitted, so total loop volume falls as harvest proceeds and run length is bounded by the reservoir working volume.

Mixing

Magnetic stir bar at 200 rpm in the reservoir. Added in cycle 2 after sedimentation was diagnosed.

Induction

Green light signal to the culture vessel, delivered by a emitter, on the light conditions the DiOPAL array is built to establish; triggered by the AIoT stress prediction upstream.

Optical density

In‑line, dual‑sensor ratiometric; the continuous trace needs no sample drawn. Specified in the design; not yet run on the membrane loop. See the photometer record.

Pressure

One transducer on the feed leg, to catch over‑pressure before the membrane is damaged. Transmembrane pressure would also need lumen‑outlet and shell‑side transducers; those are not fitted. confirm

pH

Monitored and regulated on the harvest side before delivery. set point.

Why perfusion rather than batch

A batch reactor must be opened to be harvested, and every opening is a containment event and a contamination risk. Perfusion is designed to move product out continuously through a barrier that retains the organism, so the culture vessel stays closed from inoculation to shutdown.

Part4

Iteration History

Two complete design–build–test–learn cycles, each reported with what failed and what that failure changed. A third is in progress.

  1. 01

    Continuous flow, proof of containment

    Cycle 1 · 6 June 2026

    The first build established that a continuous‑flow loop around a PES hollow‑fiber membrane works at all: membrane, medium reservoir, peristaltic pump and luer valve, nothing else. A 500 mL reservoir held 300 mL of LB inoculated with 20 mL of overnight culture, perfused through a fiber cartridge of roughly 200 mL total hold‑up.

    The pump delivered 94 mL/min, but the run ended early on leakage at the pump head — lumen‑side culture, not sterile medium, so the loop lost containment as well as flow. The membrane holds the organism only inside an envelope of tubing, connectors and pump head that is part of the containment boundary too. Final readings were OD600 0.304 in‑tube against 0.48 in the reservoir.

    Learned

    The gap between tube and reservoir OD pointed at sedimentation: with no active mixing, cells settled and nutrients stratified, so the reservoir was not representative of the loop. Two hardware changes followed — biocompatible BPT tubing for a reliable seal at the pump, and an active magnetic stirrer to keep the reservoir homogeneous. The sampling port also moved to the reservoir outlet to make the reading representative.

  2. 02

    Active mixing, and a full growth curve

    Cycle 2 · 14 June 2026

    A 200 rpm magnetic stirrer was added to the reservoir, and the sampling point moved to sit close to it — with the stirrer running the reservoir is the homogeneous compartment of the loop, which it was not in cycle 1. The run was held at 37 °C rather than room temperature, with a shake flask as control.

    The pump held 94 mL/min for the entire run and nothing leaked. The bioreactor reached plateau at OD600 0.8–0.9 after 2 hours; the flask control reached 0.6–0.8 after 4.5 hours.

    Learned

    Stirring was added to address the sedimentation diagnosed in cycle 1, and this run produced the first complete growth curve. In this run the reactor reached a plateau at least as dense as the flask arm's, in less than half the time. Cycle 2 changed tubing, stirring, the sampling point and the temperature together, so stirring is not isolated as the reason for either result — see 5.1. Next: run at 22 °C to characterise field conditions, and upgrade to higher‑durability BPT tubing to stop the tube degradation seen in earlier trials.

  3. 03

    Field temperature and durability

    Cycle 3 · in progress

    Planned: characterise growth kinetics at 22 °C, the temperature the device will actually see in the field, and qualify BPT tubing over a long run to establish service life.

    Needs

    Results of the 22 °C run, and a tubing service‑life figure.

Results

5.1 Growth Against a Flask Control

0.8–0.9Reactor plateau OD600
2hReactor, time to plateau
4.5hFlask control, time to plateau
0Colonies, shell side (one plating)

The cycle 2 comparison is the central result so far. Matched on medium and temperature, the perfusion reactor reached its plateau in less than half the time of the shake‑flask control, at a density at or above the flask's (OD600 0.8–0.9 against 0.6–0.8; the ranges meet at 0.8). The arms were not matched beyond those two variables — see the caveat below. A plausible contribution is continuous mixing: cycle 1 traced its tube‑to‑reservoir OD gap to sedimentation in an unstirred reservoir, and cycle 2 added the stirrer that removes that condition. Dissolved oxygen was not measured in either arm, and the loop carries liquid on both sides of the membrane wall with no aeration path described, so no gas‑transfer explanation is offered here. What this bounds is the culture stage only: whether faster growth means faster protectant supply depends on transfer across the fiber wall, which has not been measured (5.4).

Needs

The raw OD600 time series for both arms, so this can be replotted as an interactive chart with fitted growth rates and error bars — the same treatment the photometer's continuous record gets.

How far this comparison actually goes

"Identical medium and temperature" is true, and it is not the same as a controlled comparison. The flask's working volume, inoculum and shaking speed are not recorded here, and the reactor holds 300 mL of medium against a flask volume we have not stated — so the two arms may differ in more than the thing being tested. Prototype II also changed four variables at once — tubing, stirring, the sampling point and the temperature — which means stirring is not proven on its own. Read this as a promising difference, not a measured one, until the arms are matched and the series published.

5.2 Containment

Medium drawn from the shell side and plated on LB agar produced no colonies (). The membrane blocks whole B. subtilis, and that plate is the evidence for it.

An earlier version of this specification claimed a 50 kDa molecular‑weight cut‑off and stated that the membrane retains secreted membrane vesicles. Neither is correct for the part fitted, which is rated 0.2 µm — large enough to pass ACC deaminase at roughly 36 kDa, which is its intended function, and therefore large enough to pass small vesicles and cell debris. Containment here rests on cell size alone. The earlier claim was withdrawn in week 18.

Confirm

Number of biological replicates, and incubation time and temperature — the plated volume is on record at 2 mL, but a containment claim needs its n, and this one currently rests on a single plating.

Confirm

Whether a cartridge integrity check — pressure hold or bubble point — is run before or after a session. The plate tests the barrier at one moment on one cartridge; a broken fiber or a failed seal partway through a run would put culture straight into the harvest stream, and nothing in the record above would catch it.

5.3 What This Reactor Has Actually Shown

The machine described in Parts 2–4 produced the Part 5 results; it is not the one on the bench now.

Demonstrated

  • The membrane blocks whole B. subtilis. Shell-side medium plated on LB agar produced no colonies. Cycle 1 · 5.2 Containment · one plating
  • The culture reached plateau in the reactor, and faster than the flask arm run alongside it. Cycle 2 · see the caveat in 5.1 Growth Against a Flask Control
  • The loop runs closed. OD is read in line, so the vessel is not opened to produce the OD trace. 2.2 Flow Path and Instrumentation
  • A wrong claim was found and withdrawn. The 50 kDa cut-off and the vesicle claim were checked against the part actually bought. Week 18

Not established

  • Product in the harvest stream. No shell‑side protectant assay has been run; the plating speaks only to what is absent from the stream, not to what is in it. 5.4 Open Parameters · transfer rate open
  • Containment with an n. One plating, with replicate count and incubation conditions unrecorded. 5.2 Containment · and the pore passes vesicles and debris
  • The flask comparison as a controlled test. Flask volume, inoculum and shaking speed are unstated, and prototype II changed four variables at once. 5.1 Growth Against a Flask Control · stirring not proven on its own
  • Why either arm plateaus where it does. No aeration path is described and dissolved oxygen was not measured, so oxygen limitation is not excluded. 5.1 · oxygen not measured
  • Membrane integrity over a run. The plating covers one cartridge at one moment; no cartridge integrity check is recorded before or after a session. 5.2 Containment · the barrier is the only containment stage
  • Continuous operation. No medium feed is fitted, so volume taken off on the shell side is not replaced. 2.2 · run length bounded by the reservoir working volume
  • Fouling and service life. No transmembrane pressure logged, and the lumen‑outlet and shell‑side transducers it needs are not fitted. Highest-priority open parameter
  • Field temperature. All kinetics are from an incubator, not 22 °C. Cycle 3, in progress
  • Running with the photometer. The 336 h record was taken on a simplified loop with no membrane module fitted. Photometer 3.5 · continuous growth curve

5.4 Open Parameters

Membrane fouling

Highest priority

A cake of retained cells, plus protein and debris, accumulating on the lumen wall will reduce flux and eventually protectant transfer. No fouling measurement has been made over a long run, and it is the most likely limit on service life.

Needs

Lumen‑outlet and shell‑side pressure transducers, then transmembrane pressure logged across a multi‑day run.

Protectant transfer rate

Open

How fast product crosses into the shell side, and whether it, not growth, sets system throughput.

Needs

Shell‑side concentration against time.

Field temperature

Open

All growth data so far is at 37 °C. The device will run near 22 °C.

Needs

The cycle 3 growth curve.

Run duration

Open

Longest continuous run with the membrane module fitted is hours, not days; the 336 h record was taken on a simplified loop without it. Field use implies weeks.

Needs

An extended run with the membrane module fitted and the photometer logging throughout.

Role & Outlook

6.1 Role in the Project

RELEAF runs predict → produce → protect. The AIoT stage predicts plant stress and issues a light signal; this bioreactor is the produce stage that receives it. The DiOPAL array is the instrument built to establish which light conditions drive protectant expression, and the photometer is embedded in this loop as its eye. What leaves here is cell‑free protectant, bound for soil or a hydroponic line.

6.2 Where the Build Stands

The machine described above is the one that produced the results in Part 5. It is not the one currently on the bench. A rebuild was underway when this record closed on 13 August 2026, and the honest version of the specification is that three of its mechanical choices are being replaced:

  • The peristaltic pump is being swapped for a magnetic one, so the loop can be driven from an Arduino and run unattended. The magnetic pump is weaker and cannot lift liquid out of the serum bottle, which is what forced the next change.
  • The reservoir is becoming a clear rectangle with ports top and bottom, so feed enters high and draw leaves low and gravity does part of the work. It was bought for its shape.
  • Dissolved‑oxygen and pH probes are moving into the reservoir itself. This relocates pH sensing from the harvest side, where 2.2 places it, into the culture vessel, and adds a dissolved‑oxygen probe that is not part of the build described above. Neither probe fits the reservoir cap: a two‑piece printed cap that clamps around the cable and screws shut is designed but unproven, and keeping the vessel airtight through it is the hard part.

Three things raised earlier are still open with no design response: foaming from B. subtilis surfactant, flagged twice in review; the fact that prototype II changed four variables at once, so stirring is not proven on its own; and, since the membrane claim narrowed, the absence of any second containment stage. A full list, across all the instruments, closes the notebook at week 24.

6.3 What Would Make This Deployable

  • A multi-day run with the photometer logging continuously, to establish that the loop is stable over the timescale a field deployment needs.
  • A fouling curve from transmembrane pressure, converting "it works for hours" into a stated service interval.
  • 22 °C kinetics, so the throughput claim reflects field conditions rather than an incubator.
  • Replicated containment plating with a stated n, so the safety claim carries a number.
  • A costed BOM against a named commercial perfusion system.

That list is aimed at a deployable configuration rather than a further single‑channel bench loop. RELEAF requires three protectants, and producing them one at a time on a single loop limits throughput. The intended form is three of these channels in a single housing, off one controller, each producing a different protectant concurrently.
