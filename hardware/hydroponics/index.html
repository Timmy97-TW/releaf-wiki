Hydroponics
Growth Plate

A printed floating raft for deep water culture. Thirteen seed holders sit on a fixed pitch over a nutrient reservoir, and the raft carries itself on two sealed box sections rather than on a foam float or an open skirt.

This document reports the device to the standard of strict reproducibility: a team in another country, with no contact with us, should be able to rebuild it from what follows. Where a specific number, part, or statement must be supplied before publication, it is marked explicitly rather than invented.

the geometry is measured and complete; the purpose, the iteration history and every performance figure are still to be supplied.

Section02

What makes it stand out

2.1 What the geometry establishes

28.7cm³ sealed

Two closed box sections, one down each long side — and nothing at all beneath the middle of the plate. The raft is carried by air the water cannot reach, not by a foam float or an open skirt.

Three things are true of the part as measured, independent of any claim we make for it.

  • Sealed buoyancy, not foam and not a skirt. Two closed box sections, one inside each long side, enclose 28.7 cm³ that water cannot enter. The centre of the plate carries no structure below the slab at all.
  • Four printed parts, no fasteners. One plate, two handles, one seed holder printed thirteen times. The handles clip into slots moulded into the plate.
  • Thirteen identical positions on a fixed pitch, so a run is described by its contents rather than by its layout.

Review · 2.1 What the geometry establishes

Works. “The raft is carried by air the water cannot reach, not by a foam float or an open skirt” explains the design idea in one sentence. Stating only what the part itself proves, and keeping claims for 2.2, is a good discipline.

4.2 Key specifications

Measured geometry
FeatureMeasurement
Plate envelope 117.50 × 92.50 × 25.08 mm
Top slab 3.175 mm thick
Bores 13 × ∅11.26 mm on a 21.59 mm pitch, staggered 4–5–4, outer rows half a pitch off the middle
Air chambers Two, one per long side. Each internal void 113.80 × 6.90 × 20.32 mm, capped underneath by a 1.7 mm skin. 28.7 cm³ the pair.
Chamber profile Each is a bracket: a run the length of its long side, hooking inward at both ends and tapering from x −42.25 at y −34 to x −50.25 at y −18.4, where it stops short of the handle pocket
Below the centre Nothing. The middle of the plate is flat slab
Handles 12.34 × 25.40 × 32.89 mm, one at each end, clipped into moulded slots
Seed holder ∅13.19 mm flange over a tapered basket, 12.07 mm tall. Modelled once, placed thirteen times
Worth measuring

The sealed voids come to 28.7 cm³, while the solid material across all sixteen printed pieces comes to 74.71 cm³. The mass therefore depends on wall count, infill and filament, none of which are recorded here, and the displacement needed to float the raft is larger than the sealed voids alone provide — the remainder has to come from the rails' outer envelope sitting below the waterline. Whether it floats level, at what freeboard, and with what payload of medium and plants, is untested. It is the single most useful number this page could carry.

Review · 4.2 Key specifications

Works. The measured-geometry table is precise and checkable, and the “Worth measuring” box correctly says the float has never been tested.

Fix. A rough check the team can confirm: at a typical PLA density of about 1.24 g/cm³ and solid printing, 74.71 cm³ of plastic weighs about 93 g, while fully submerged it displaces about 75 g of water plus 28.7 g from the sealed voids, about 103 g. That is a margin of roughly 10 g before any medium or seedlings, more with a sparse infill. Weigh a printed plate and float it with a known load to replace this estimate with a measurement.
