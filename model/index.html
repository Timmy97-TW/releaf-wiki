Math Model

Abstract

Our bioreactor makes a crop protectant only when its green lamp is on, so the whole system depends on one number: how stressed is the crop right now?

Three blocks answer that, and then answer what follows from it. Block 1 lets Taiwan's own harvests define the number: 32,767 candidate models fitted to county rice yields since 2001, scored only on seasons they never saw. The surviving index cuts 32% off the trend's error in the second crop and 8% in the first, and a frozen 2026 forecast is being marked against the real harvest as counties publish. Block 2 turns that index into a lamp setting through the measured CcaSR transfer function, and measures what comes out: 7.25 mU mL−1 of secreted ACC deaminase with the samyQ signal peptide, 7.3 hours from decision to protection. Block 3 follows the enzyme onto the leaf and finds the stressed apoplast is the only place it works. The titre is the bottleneck, and ultrafiltration is the one lever we control that is large enough to matter.

Best Model

This award is for teams who build a model of their system and use it to inform system design or simulate expected behavior before, or in conjunction with, experiments in the wet lab.

The four questions a judge is given for this award, and where this page answers each one.

  1. How impressive is the modelling? Three blocks, each with its own out-of-sample or measured test: Stress index (32,767 models fitted, scored on years they never saw, and a frozen 2026 forecast now being marked), Optogenetic kinetics and Protectant delivery.
  2. Did the model help the team understand a part, device or system? What the model changed, one entry per decision that went back into the build.
  3. Did the team use measurements of a part, device or system to develop the model? Yes, in two places: the DNPH activity assay on our own supernatant sets the titre the whole dose budget runs on, and the Arabidopsis salt experiment sets the efficacy coefficient the index is scaled by.
  4. Does the modelling approach provide a good example for others? Every block states its assumptions before its results, every figure is reproducible from the draft, and the places where the three blocks still disagree are collected in Numbers to reconcile instead of being quietly smoothed out.

What the model does

Abiotic stress (heat, soil salinity, and vapor pressure deficit) reduces crop yields across Taiwan's farmland long before visible damage appears, by disrupting plant hormone balance and protein stability at the cellular level. ReLeaf addresses this by deploying an optogenetically controlled B. subtilis bioreactor that senses field stress in real time and produces tailored protectant proteins. Our mathematical model asks the question that connects every part of that system: given today's stress conditions in a Taiwanese farm field, how much engineered protectant actually reaches the plant?

Answering that means crossing three physically distinct domains, and no single equation spans all of them. So the model is three blocks run as one chain. Each block takes one quantity from the block before it, and hands one quantity to the block after it.

Block 1 · Stress index

Our bioreactor makes a crop protectant only when its green lamp is on. So the whole system depends on one number: how stressed is the crop right now?

Most crop stress indices add temperature, water and light with weights chosen by experts, and are rarely checked against what happened in the field. We took a different route: let Taiwan's own harvests decide. The Agriculture and Food Agency (農粮署) has published county rice yields for every season since 2001. A season whose yield fell below its trend was a stressed season. If weather alone can predict which seasons fall, and by how much, that prediction is a stress index, and every harvest becomes a test of it.

Assumptions

The five assumptions the stress index rests on, with the reason for each
#AssumptionWhy?

2The same weather stress causes more damage at some growth stages than others.Rice sensitivity is stage-specific (e.g. cold at heading, rain at tillering).
3Stress can only reduce yield, and only after it passes a threshold.Physical damage is one-sided: a calm day does not add rice.
4The two rice crops respond differently to weather and should be modeled separately.Different seasons, weather and stresses.
5The value of lost rice can be estimated using the current farm-gate price.Official daily price exists; public purchase price is close.

Result 1 · Variable selection

We gathered 18 candidate stresses. For each crop, every one of the 215 − 1 = 32,767 combinations of 15 candidates was fitted and scored on years it had not seen. Each candidate was then judged three ways:

First, by how much the out-of-sample error moved when it was added, averaged over every model it could enter. Second, by how often it helped at all: the percentages on Figure 3 are the share of the 16,384 subsets of the other fourteen variables in which adding this one lowered the error, so 100% means it helped in every model and 50% means a coin flip. Third, by a permutation test, which breaks the link between the variable and the harvest by shuffling it and asks what that costs.

Which stresses were supported by the data. After testing each candidate stress with three different methods, these were the variables that consistently improved the model and were kept in the final version.

Hours ≥ 35 °C helps in 0% of first-crop models and 45% of second-crop ones, and shuffling it costs nothing in either. Taiwan's hottest hours and its warm nights are the stresses a greenhouse experiment would put first, and on twenty-five years of harvests they earn almost nothing. What does cost rice is light (dark spells and radiation deficit), wind (typhoon and lodging gusts) and water in the wrong week.

Result 2 · Our model

One equation predicts county yields, and its damage half is the stress index. Each index value has a price in kilograms and New Taiwan dollars.

ACCD efficacy measured by our wet lab: salinity in rice

Efficacy is the share of the yield a stress costs that ACC deaminase could save: 1 means all of it, 0 means none. The values in Figure 11 come from the literature. Methods

The draft carries these equations only inside the block diagram in Figure 1. They are transcribed here. A county-season's weather enters as stage-weighted exposures: a daily variable is spread across growth stages by the probability that day fell in each stage, so a wet week counts differently at tillering and at grain fill.

Stage-weighted exposure \[ x_{\mathrm{stage}} \;=\; \sum_{d} P(\mathrm{stage}\mid d)\; x_{d} \tag{1} \] Summed over the days d of the season. P(stage | d) is the probability that day d fell in that growth stage, which smooths the transplanting-date differences between counties.
The yield model, and the index inside it \[ \mathrm{anomaly} \;=\; a \;+\; \gamma P \;+\; \sum_{i} b_i\, z(x_i) \;-\; \underbrace{\sum_{j} \beta_j E_j}_{\textstyle S} , \qquad \beta_j \ge 0 \tag{2} \] \[ z(x) \;=\; \frac{x - \mu_{\mathrm{county}}}{\sigma_{\mathrm{train}}} , \qquad \hat{y} \;=\; \mathrm{trend} \times \exp(\mathrm{anomaly}) \tag{3} \] One fit per crop. P is the sunshine-potential term, z(xi) are the stage terms standardised against the county's own history, and Ej are the damage exposures. The constraint βj ≥ 0 is assumption 3 written as an equation: a damage term can take yield away and never add it. The stress index S is the damage sum alone, which is why it is bounded below by zero and why it reads as a loss.
Index to yield loss, and to the lamp \[ \mathrm{loss} \;=\; 1 - e^{-S} , \qquad S_{\mathrm{addr}} \;=\; \sum_{j} \varepsilon_j\, \beta_j E_j \tag{4} \] The first identity is what makes a harvest a test of the index: predicted yield is the county trend times e−S, and the Agency publishes the realised yield beside it. The second is what Block 2 receives: each damage term is weighted by εj, the share of that stress ACC deaminase can act on (0.58 for salinity, Figure 10), and the normalised result is the demand f.

Coefficients are not tabulated in the draft. Figure 5 reports every term's effect in kg/ha with a bootstrap interval, which is the same information in the units a reader can use, but the raw βj and bi are not yet written down anywhere.

Conclusion

Q1 · Which weather costs Taiwanese rice?

Light shortage, typhoon wind and lodging, and water (heavy rain, rain at tilling). Heat and warm nights barely register.

Q2 · What is the model and what does its index mean?

One equation per crop; its damage half is the stress index. First crop: 0.15 is a normal year, 0.35 has meant ≈ −4%, −290 kg/ha, −NT$7,500/ha.

Q3 · Does it hold up on unseen data?

Yes: 32% less error than the trend (second crop), 8% (first crop), under the strictest test; most of the gain is in bad seasons. A real blind test (2026) is under way: 4 of 16 counties published, county error 7.9% (trend 8.8%).

Block 2 · Optogenetic kinetics

The reactor holds Bacillus subtilis WB800 carrying the cyanobacterial CcaSR two-component system. Green light at 535 nm activates the sensor kinase CcaS, which phosphorylates CcaR. CcaR~P activates the PcpcG2 promoter, transcribing acdS fused to a secretion signal peptide. The Sec translocon exports the enzyme and cleaves the signal peptide, leaving active ACC deaminase in the culture supernatant.

Result 1 · Light setpoint

The measured transfer function. Castillo-Hair et al. (2019) characterised CcaSR in B. subtilis and fitted the steady-state response to a Hill function with half-maximal intensity K½ = 4.66 ± 0.63 µmol m−2 s−1 and Hill coefficient n = 1.88 ± 0.16, over a dynamic range exceeding 70-fold. The same system in E. coli gives K½ = 1.075 and n = 2.737, so the parameters are host-specific and the B. subtilis values are the correct ones here.

Inversion. Block 1 produces addressable stress Saddr and converts it to a demand f between 0 and 1. The lamp setpoint is the light intensity that drives promoter output to that fraction of maximum:

Lamp setpoint from demand \[ I \;=\; K_{1/2} \left( \frac{f}{1-f} \right)^{1/n} \tag{5} \] K½ = 4.66 µmol m−2 s−1, n = 1.88. This is equation (5) solved for I after setting the Hill output In/(Kn + In) equal to f. It diverges as f → 1, which is why the lamp ceiling, not the biology, sets the top of the control range.

Result 3 · Reactor output

Measured activity. ACC deaminase activity was measured by DNPH colorimetry on culture supernatant. The enzyme converts ACC to α-ketobutyrate and ammonia in 1:1 stoichiometry, so α-ketobutyrate production equals ACC cleaved. Reaction conditions were 43.0 mM ACC, pH 8.0, 30 °C, 30 min, with the WB800 host supernatant as blank.

Signal is not a biomass artefact. WB800 has the highest OD600 and the second-highest absorbance; samyQ has lower biomass and higher signal. A background from cell density or secreted carbonyl metabolites would produce matching ranks. This argument is independent of the standard curve and survives any calibration error.

Every number downstream of here inherits this assay, so its limits travel with them. One replicate, one day, one culture. samyQ clears the detection limit and does not clear the quantitation limit, so the correct reading is secretion detected, titre not yet quantified. The rank order (samyQ > csn > host) is what survives any calibration error; 7.25 is a provisional point estimate, and Block 3's dose budget is built on it.

Calibration correction. The standard-curve concentrations were originally computed against a 110 µL reaction volume while the actual standard volume was 160.6 µL. Correcting this reduces every derived activity by a factor of 1.460.

Implied enzyme concentration. Substrate was 43.0 mM against a Km of 3.4 mM, so the assay ran at 93% of Vmax. Correcting to Vmax = 7.82 mU mL−1 and applying kcat = 146 min−1 (P. putida UW4) gives a secreted enzyme concentration of 54 nM, or 1.96 mg L−1.

From activity to protein \[ \frac{v}{V_{\max}} = \frac{[S]}{K_m + [S]} = \frac{43.0}{3.4 + 43.0} = 0.927 , \qquad V_{\max} = \frac{7.25}{0.927} = 7.82\ \mathrm{mU\,mL^{-1}} \tag{6} \] \[ [E] \;=\; \frac{V_{\max}}{k_{\mathrm{cat}}} \;=\; \frac{7.82\ \mathrm{nmol\,min^{-1}\,mL^{-1}}}{146\ \mathrm{min^{-1}}} \;=\; 53.6\ \mathrm{nM} \;=\; 1.96\ \mathrm{mg\,L^{-1}} \tag{7} \] Michaelis–Menten with no inhibition. The mass uses the 36.5 kDa subunit, because kcat is per active site and the active tetramer carries four of them: 53.6 nM of sites is 13.4 nM of tetramer, and both come to the same 1.96 mg L−1 of protein. The published range for B. subtilis heterologous secretion is 4 to 200 mg L−1, so this sits a factor of two below its floor.

Accumulation. Enzyme accumulates while the lamp runs and decays in the medium, so banked activity saturates at production rate × mean lifetime. Running the reactor longer past that point adds nothing.

Culture reached OD600 3.06. At a doubling time of 29 min in LB at 37 °C, that is about 5.9 doublings from a 1:100 inoculum, or 4 to 6 h including the approach to stationary phase. Batch duration was not recorded, so the production rate is inferred rather than measured.

Conclusion

Q1 · What light for a given demand?

Control is useful between f = 0.015 and 0.94. Below that the lamp floor takes over, above it the promoter saturates.

Q2 · How fast does it respond?

105 min to half-maximal transcription, about 7.3 h from decision to enzyme active in the leaf. Uptake is 41% of that. The controller must act on forecast stress.

Q3 · How much enzyme?

7.25 mU mL−1 with the samyQ signal peptide, equivalent to 1.96 mg L−1. Below the published range for B. subtilis secretion.

Key findings

  • Signal peptide selection. samyQ gives detectable secretion; csn does not. The comparison is the basis for carrying samyQ forward.
  • Controller bounds. The lamp ceiling saturates the promoter at f = 0.94, defining the upper limit of useful control.
  • Forecast requirement. A 7.3 h decision-to-protection time makes reactive control structurally late, which is why Block 1 supplies a forward index.
  • Titre as the bottleneck. The measured 1.96 mg L−1 is below the published range and propagates directly into the Block 3 dose budget.

Block 3 · Protectant delivery

Under waterlogging, ACC is synthesised in oxygen-starved roots and carried to the shoot in the xylem, where delivery rises about threefold within a day of flooding. In the leaf it is imported into cells by LHT1 and converted to ethylene by cytosolic ACC oxidase. Our enzyme must reach the apoplast and cleave ACC before that import occurs.

Block 2 ends at the reactor outlet. Block 3 covers concentration, spraying, uptake and catalysis, and returns the interception fraction η that Block 1 uses as its efficacy coefficient.

Result 1 · The spray

From bottle to tank. Total activity per hectare is conserved through dilution. What ultrafiltration changes is whether that activity fits in a sprayer. At the measured titre a hectare needs about 3,757 U of ACC deaminase, which is 68,795 L of bottle supernatant without a concentration step.

Droplets and coverage. A spray deposits discrete droplets, not a film. At 300 L/ha with 150 µm droplets spreading threefold on impact, a hectare receives about 170 × 109 droplets, each covering 0.16 mm2. Allowing for overlap, that wets 42% of a canopy at leaf area index 5.

The wet window. Stomatal infiltration is driven by mass flow and only proceeds while liquid is present. Enzyme still on the surface when the droplet dries is stranded. Three processes therefore compete from the moment a droplet lands: entry, UV destruction, and drying.

The formulation is worth as much as the biology here. Holding the droplet wet for half an hour instead of five minutes triples what gets in, and it is the one lever in this block that needs no new construct, only a humectant and an encapsulant in the tank.

Result 2 · Getting in

The cuticle is closed. A spray on the cuticle is wasted. Cuticular aqueous pores are 4 to 5 nm across, and diffusion through them falls sharply with molecular size: ions up to about 800 g/mol pass, while molecules above roughly 14 kDa stay on the surface. ACC deaminase is a 36.5 kDa subunit that assembles into an active multimer, so it is well outside that range.

The stomata. Rice carries 187 stomata per mm2 on the upper leaf surface and 621 on the lower. At the stoma, size is not the constraint: the pore is hundreds of times wider than the enzyme. What excludes liquid is surface tension, which is why an organosilicone surfactant rather than a smaller molecule is the relevant intervention.

Result 3 · Inside the plant

The pH gate. ACC deaminase has an optimum at pH 8.5 and no measurable activity below pH 7.0. Our assay ran at pH 8.0, so every environment is quoted relative to the assay rather than to the peak.

Everywhere the enzyme might end up by accident (a leaf surface, the soil, an unstressed leaf) sits below pH 7 and runs at 2 to 3% of assay activity. The one place it works is the apoplast of a plant that is already stressed, which alkalinises as part of the stress response. The protectant is gated by the condition it treats, and nobody designed that in: it falls out of the enzyme's own pH curve.

What it competes with. ACC oxidase is cytosolic and nuclear. Apoplastic ACC deaminase does not compete with it directly; it competes with LHT1-mediated import of ACC into the cell. Under continuous ACC arrival the apoplast reaches a steady state and the intercepted fraction is

Interception at steady state \[ \eta \;=\; \frac{k_{\mathrm{ACCD}}}{k_{\mathrm{ACCD}} + k_{\mathrm{import}}} \;=\; \frac{r}{1+r}, \qquad r = \frac{k_{\mathrm{ACCD}}}{k_{\mathrm{import}}} \tag{8} \] Two first-order routes out of the same apoplastic pool, so the fraction taken by either is its share of the total rate. η = 0.28 needs r = 0.39; η = 0.50 needs parity. The form has no free parameters, which is why the uncertainty sits entirely in kimport.

Neat supernatant gives ACC a half-life of 5.0 h at assay pH. The apoplastic residence time of ACC, which sets kimport, has not been measured. It is scanned from 30 to 240 min and is the second-largest source of uncertainty in the budget.

Result 4 · Dosage

A hectare needs about 68,795 L of bottle supernatant at the measured titre. Ultrafiltration is the single largest lever and the only one in the top three we control directly.

What decides the amount?

Required activity rises about 1.9× faster than demand across the operating range. Block 2 concluded that dose time is flat under a linear requirement; with this superlinear relation it rises by roughly 89% from low to high demand. That resolves the question of whether the horizon should depend on stress severity: it should, modestly.

The reason is in equation (8). Required activity scales with η/(1 − η), and if the interception target is set proportional to demand, that ratio curves upward as η climbs. A reactor answering a severe forecast therefore needs a longer run than a linear reading of Block 2 suggests, and the controller's horizon should carry the severity with it.

What the model changed

Four results did not stay on this page. One changed the construct, one the controller, one the hardware, and one changed what the index is looking for.

samyQ, not csn

Block 2's activity assay is the only side-by-side comparison of the two signal peptides. samyQ clears detection at 7.25 mU mL−1, csn does not clear it at all, and that comparison is why samyQ is the construct carried forward.

The controller runs on a forecast

7.3 hours from decision to enzyme active in the leaf, of which the reactor owns only 4.2. A controller reacting to today's sensor reading would always be late, so Block 1 was built around a three-day forecast.

Ultrafiltration became part of the device

At the measured titre the dose does not fit in a sprayer by three orders of magnitude, and the tornado in Figure 29 puts concentration factor at the top with a 50× span. A membrane step moved from optional to required, and it is the one place extra engineering buys the most.

Heat stopped being the headline

The project was framed around heat and salinity. Twenty-five years of harvests say Taiwanese rice loses yield to light, wind and badly timed water, and that hours above 35 °C earn nothing in either crop. The index follows the data; the salinity arm stays because that is the stress ACC deaminase addresses.

Numbers to reconcile

Three blocks written separately do not yet agree everywhere. Each one below is a specific thing to go and check, so they are listed here and not smoothed over.

  1. The dose budget does not close. Block 3 says a hectare needs 3,757 U and that this is 68,795 L of supernatant. At the measured 7.25 mU mL−1, 68,795 L carries about 499,000 U, which is 133 times the stated requirement. The losses quoted in the same block (42% coverage, 18% entry, 23% pH) multiply to about 1/57, not 1/133. Either the requirement is stated after a different set of losses or one factor is being applied twice.
  2. Two entry fractions. The wet-window table gives 27% unprotected and 79% encapsulated; the conclusion says 18%. The 18% does not correspond to either row and needs its wet window stated.
  3. Tank loads. 68,795 L divided by a 500 L tank is 138 loads, not the 223 in the draft. 223 implies a 308 L load.
  4. Where 0.58 comes from. The Arabidopsis experiment measures fresh weight on agar; the efficacy coefficient is a share of rice grain yield saved. The published rice studies that bridge the two are referred to but not named, and the ACCD groups in that experiment gained weight under salt, which the simple "share of the loss recovered" arithmetic cannot represent.

Still unmeasured

  • τdecay, the half-life of secreted activity in the medium. Three assumed values span a 30-fold range in what a reactor can bank (Figure 19). Nothing else in Block 2 is this loosely held.
  • The accumulation step, 2 h. A placeholder inside the 7.3 h horizon. It depends on the required mass, which Block 3 now says is superlinear in demand, so it is not one number.
  • kimport, the apoplastic residence time of ACC. Scanned from 30 to 240 min. It sets the denominator of equation (8) and is the second-largest uncertainty in the dose.
  • The pH profile of our own enzyme. Figure 26 is a two-anchor fit to published values. The draft states that a different profile moves the answer by about 8×.
  • Replication of the titre. One culture, one day, below the limit of quantitation. The result is a detection, and should be quoted as one until it is repeated.
  • The βj coefficients of Block 1. Figure 5 reports every term's effect in kg/ha, but the fitted coefficients themselves are not written down anywhere yet.
