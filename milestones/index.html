Abstract

From our first meeting as strangers in December 2025 to presenting our project as a team nearly a year later, ReLEAF has evolved through continuous feedback, engineering pivots, and real-world testing. This page tracks our year month by month, highlighting the milestones we have achieved throughout the months.

On this page

December 2025

Our iGEM journey began with our very first team meeting, where our instructors gave us an in-depth introduction to the scope and expectations of the competition. Starting as strangers with no set project direction, every team member took on the task of pitching individual project proposals based on their own research interests. This initial phase was driven by intensive brainstorming, proposal presentations, and continuous discussions with our instructors and advisors as we began evaluating our options and narrowing down potential topics.

  2. 27 Dec

January and February 2026

Following our initial topic pitches in January, we spent the first two months of the year filtering our concepts down from five candidate ideas to two finalists, ultimately selecting a single focus on plant stress protection. During this time, we held our very first Human Practices meeting online, presenting our early project pitch to external reviewers to gather outside perspectives. By early February, we established our first formal project roadmap, structuring our work into four parallel tracks for biosecurity, modeling, a stress-response switch, and protectant delivery. Each track was targeted toward a field test, while hardware was noted as an initial collaboration point with the Dry Lab.

March 2026

All team members completed foundational lab safety training to prepare for future wet lab work. During this month, the team visited Professor Cheng’s lab at National Taiwan University for an academic outreach meeting. The visit focused on learning standard plant protocols, inspecting seedling growth cabinets, and receiving Arabidopsis thaliana seeds alongside technical advice.

April 2026

April marked a defining shift in our project’s biological and hardware design. Guided by consultations with experts in various areas, we pivoted to a light-responsive bioreactor model. The new architecture integrates a hollow-fiber membrane cartridge to safely isolate engineered bacteria while allowing stress-protectant molecules to reach the plant, establishing the core framework for both our hardware prototyping and wet lab circuit design.

May 2026

In May, our project expanded across plant cultivation testing, custom hardware prototyping, and real-world stakeholder research. We evaluated three distinct Arabidopsis thaliana growing methods (agar plates, hydroponic containers, and potted soil) to identify optimal growth conditions for stress assays. To overcome resource limitations, our hardware team conceptualized and assembled breadboard prototypes for a custom photometer and light plate apparatus. Concurrently, our Human Practices team engaged with local agricultural producers at the Taipei Expo and researchers at the World Vegetable Center while beginning to test education events for schools.

June 2026

In June we were busy testing our hardware, learning from mistakes, and sharing our work with the community. We ran our very first bacterial growth test inside the hollow-fiber bioreactor loop, showing that our culture could stay contained without leaking. While working in the lab, we ran into a few troubleshooting moments, such as misaligned seedling plates and contamination during trials. We also welcomed Professor Chang and Professor Chen to review our progress, while Dr. Sattely from Stanford University helped us refine our protectant delivery plan. Outside the lab, we launched our first educational outreach by hosting interactive synthetic biology workshops at three elementary schools.

July 2026

July brought major strides in both our hardware build and real-world outreach. In the lab, we assembled our 3D-printed light plate apparatus, connected pressure sensors, and used our inline photometer to track live cultures inside the bioreactor loop. We expanded our stakeholder connections by consulting with industry leaders at CH Biotech, learning about bioreactor essential logistics and business plan development at the BIO Asia-Taiwan Exhibition, and spending a day with Farmer Chen to see how a bioreactor could fit into local natural farming. Moreover, we continued moving forward with GIS and education initiatives.

August 2026

August saw our bioreactor transition into a fully integrated prototype alongside major outreach and fieldwork expansion. We integrated pH and dissolved-oxygen sensors into our reactor loop and calibrated our custom photometer against standard instruments before housing the entire hardware setup inside a portable chassis. We visited National Chung Hsing University (NCHU) to consult with Professor Huang and acquired Bacillus subtilis strain WB800, a protease-deficient host optimized for protectant secretion. Our stakeholder outreach extended to the YesHealth iFarm hydroponics facility to study environmental control systems, a high school synthetic biology workshop, and a second visit to Professor Cheng’s lab to refine our hydroponic seed-holder design. Towards the end of the month, we synthesized our four-stage mathematical model and prepared interactive data physicalization materials as well as presentations for our upcoming public forum.

September 2026

September focused on validating our system and connecting directly with end users and the public. We hosted a public forum and interactive booth activities at the Taipei Water Garden Organic Farmers’ Market, gathering crucial feedback from local farmers and the public. At Taiwan SMART Agriweek, we identified young farmers as key adopters for our tech while noting the need for targeted messaging for traditional growers. We gathered technical feedback during visits from Professor Chang, the NCHU Symposium, and an online consultation with Dr. Sattely and Professor Endy, refining our ACCD mechanism explanations and experimental controls. In the lab, we photographed essential safety equipment, conducted our first light-control assays using red safelights, and evaluated the legal and regulatory frameworks surrounding synthetic biology deployment.

October 2026

October marked our final push to validate our hardware, refine our presentation, and prepare every deliverable before the wiki freeze. Early in the month, our entire team came together for our first mock judging session, breaking into presentation groups to master the Jamboree judging criteria. In the lab, we performed a double photometer calibration test using silicon dioxide standards. To ensure our real-world deployment strategy remained grounded, we consulted with Director Wu Tsz-Hsien at the Miaoli District Agricultural Research and Extension Station to validate our outreach channels, and met with Agriweather founder Lu An-Bang to discuss microclimate data integration.
