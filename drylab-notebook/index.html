The written record is the view you land on: one entry a week from 28 March, giving the pipelines that ran and the stage each had reached, the results, who took part, the deliverables folded underneath and the photographs taken that week. The board is Felix Yu’s, a lane for every pipeline and a column for every week, with a mark wherever a pipeline was alive, sized by how much that week carried, the branches where one pipeline grew out of another, and the eight crossings to the wet lab, each holding what was sent, what the wet lab did with it and what came back. The photos view gathers all 221 frames week by week, and any of them opens full size. Weeks the team has not written up itself are drafted from the photographs, the wiki pages and the team’s Discord record and are marked in orange; the week after today is the plan to the wiki freeze on 21 October.

Results
  • Both photometers are read against one silica series. On 10 October the complex build, the slim fibre-coupled build and the BioDrop were taken down the same forty-point series of silicon dioxide, 2.5 mg at a time from 2.5 to 100 mg in 500 mL, logged into a shared sheet as it ran. The complex build tracks the reference one for one to OD 2.5, slope 0.995 and intercept −0.074, and reads low above that. The slim build is straight across the whole range and six times compressed, OD = 5.573r − 0.073 at R² 0.993 against the complex build; rescaled it agrees with the reference as closely as the complex build does and resolves half as finely. The reference is the first to run out: the BioDrop's direct channel returns exactly 2.50 from 70 mg on, so every value above OD 2.5 is reconstructed from a dilution, and that reconstruction falls as silica is added at 7 of its 20 steps above 50 mg. Against weighed mass over the same span our build scatters five times less and never reads lower when more went in. The high-OD gap is left unassigned, because multiple scattering at that turbidity would look the same and this run cannot separate the two.
  • The instrument records are rebuilt, and one outlet corrected. Between 10 and 11 October the four hardware records, bioreactor, photometer, DiOPAL and hydroponics, were rebuilt so that each one reads as a set of chapters under a single pinned control, and the bioreactor page took Anton's new opening scene. Chars corrected the rack drawing on the morning of 10 October: on the shell side only the outlet at the top of the hollow-fibre module is open, beside the lumen outlet, and the port at the foot of the shell is closed behind a luer valve that connects to nothing.
  • The Digital Twin is cut back to what its logs carry. Across 12 and 13 October the page went from about 8,500 words to 6,600, the runs renumbered R1 to R6 and the results put in the order the instruments produced them: photometer, the 434-hour run, field sensors, pressure, forecast, light plate. The application that recorded every batch is now in the repository, firmware, backend and interface together with its licence, so a judge can read the code that made the numbers. Re-reading the September log in the process turned up one of our own figures: over its first 53 h 22 min that run rises from OD 0.83 to 2.45, and the 0.17 this notebook and the Engineering page had both been carrying as its starting point is nowhere in the file.
  • Protein Design is checked against the cloning record. Six corrections went in on 14 October. Both LEA14 truncation boundaries, 1–16 and 1–23, were queued for Level 0 assembly on 26 September and neither reached a sequence-verified construct, so every LEA14 plasmid on file carries the full-length 151-residue protein. The N-terminal His tag decision belongs to BoPEP4's order after 16 August; the June and July codon-optimised cassettes and every LEA14 and ACC deaminase construct carry a C-terminal 6×His. The Lys7 salt bridge holds at 96.6 % and 2.85 Å in the full-length run, and the 98.7 % at 2.81 Å the page had quoted belongs to the 7–23 trajectory.
  • The buffer is doing some of the work. A labelled photograph of the ACC deaminase salinity plate went up on 10 October: four enzyme dilutions from 1/60 to 1/10 and a no-enzyme arm, at 0 and at 100 mM NaCl, with 1/20 TB buffer controls at both salt levels. Reading it that evening, instructor Kyle Peng said the buffer arms show TB itself holding growth back from 1/20 strength upward, which is enough to cover whatever the enzyme is doing, so the plate as it stands says nothing about salt tolerance from appearance alone. Each treated well is 0.5 mL of enzyme preparation in 9.5 mL of medium; the CH Biotech peptide is given at 1/500, where its own solvent hardly counts.
  • The soil salinity weights are handed to the modelling team. Fresh weights off the soil salinity assay were posted on 10 October: 0.066 g for buffer without salt and 0.107 g for buffer at 300 mM, against 0.071, 0.022 and 0.199 g for the enzyme given before, with and after the salt. The after-treatment arm comes to nearly twice its own buffer control at the same salt, and the modelling and business teams were told the same afternoon to start working from it. Every bar is one weighing of one group, so the figure sets a direction and carries no error bar.
  • Rice may be the wrong crop to map. At the 12 October meeting, 吳姿嫺 of the Miaoli District Agricultural Research and Extension Station said rice is in oversupply in Taiwan, so protecting its yield answers a question nobody is asking. The GIS team was asked that evening whether there is time to move the maps onto a crop worth more per hectare and more exposed to heat and salt. Four people each take one or two crops and go after the data; some of it is expected around 18 October, and if it does not arrive the gap goes into the reflection and the survey plan instead of the map.
  • The last fortnight before the freeze. The freeze is 21 October at 23:00 Taiwan time, and the instructors set two rules going into it: the team name is ReLEAF in capitals everywhere a reader sees it, and 10 and 11 October were the last two days for open-ended changes to writing, layout and figures. Something over thirty pages moved in that window. On iGEM's own GitLab the Homepage, the seven Engagement pages and the Description page are live, and the other twenty-four of the thirty-three addresses are still blank template pages.
  • An outside reader takes the photometer question back to Beer's law. Pak K. Yuet asked what was on the axes of the calibration plots, then set out why two builds can return different OD for the same sample: OD is −log10(I/I0) and scales with path length and with concentration, so a shorter path reads lower, and he suggested plotting OD divided by path length to compare the two fairly. Noah Tau answered that both builds use the same flow cuvette, 2 mm of solution, and that what changed was making the light reaching the reference sensor equal to what reaches the sample sensor. With the path lengths equal the explanation has to lie elsewhere, in stray light or in how far the beam spreads before it enters the liquid, and they arranged to sit down with the parts in front of them.
  • Two things the calibration leaves open. The unit measured on 10 October has its beam splitter out as well as its lens, which makes it single-beam with no drift correction and contradicts the design-changes card above it on its own page; which of the two is the final slim design has to be said before the freeze.
